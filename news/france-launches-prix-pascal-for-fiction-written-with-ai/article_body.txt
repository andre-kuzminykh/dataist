## What the prize asks for

The Prix Pascal is named for Blaise Pascal, the French thinker who helped create one of the first mechanical calculators and wrote theological works, including *Pensées*. Pope Francis praised the book for its philosophical depth and literary charm.

Submissions must be works of fiction, and they must also propose a solution to an ethical, social or environmental problem that threatens humanity. The prize’s name links the contest’s literary ambitions to Pascal’s work across computation and philosophy, though the rules leave open how much of a manuscript’s writing must involve AI.

The prize was founded by technology entrepreneurs Philippe Rodriguez, Albane Servian and Gilles Mantré:

- Rodriguez co-founded Avolta, a mergers and acquisitions consultancy. The company describes him as a prominent figure in Europe’s crypto and blockchain industries.
- Servian is a digital media entrepreneur and former chair of LGBTQ+ magazine Têtu.
- Mantré is a former civil servant, holds a PhD and teaches at Harvard University and Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne University. He also co-founded Electis, an online voting platform.

## A test of authorship

The announcement follows months of debate about possible AI use by finalists and winners of literary competitions, including the Commonwealth Short Story Prize, the Caine Prize and France’s most prestigious literary award, the Prix Goncourt.

Applications opened on Tuesday through the Prix Pascal website. The jury will include five or six writers, critics, journalists, philosophers and publishers; its members are due to be announced in early November.

I think the prize’s most consequential choice is not that it welcomes AI, but that it asks for a socially useful novel. That requirement gives the contest a clear premise, while raising a harder question: what will count as a solution in fiction, and how will judges distinguish a compelling idea from a prescription pasted onto a story? The rules establish what manuscripts must attempt, but the announcement says nothing about how AI’s role in writing will be assessed.
