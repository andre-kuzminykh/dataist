## From drafting to design

Dram estimates architects spend about 80% of their working time preparing drawings and 20% on design. He wants the software to reverse that split.

Blueprints AI trained its models on plans approved during eight years of work at Dram’s architecture and construction firm, which is still operating. The company’s first version produces architectural and construction documentation. Subscriptions cost $3,200 to $8,500 a month, depending on the plan, with custom terms for larger companies.

The second version starts from a text description or sketch and produces drawings for:

- architecture;
- structural systems;
- mechanical systems;
- electrical systems;
- plumbing and sewage;
- interiors.

Users can also design and revise a building, which the first version did not allow. A change to one part updates the whole project, and the software checks it against local building codes. The second version is already in use with early customers; Dram says it will be publicly available this week. A third version is in development, adding structural calculations and electrical panel design.

## The human sign-off remains

The company says licensed architects will still review and stamp documents before permit submission. The final decision remains with a person. Blueprints AI is targeting both architecture, engineering and design firms, and developers and property owners. It sells through subscriptions and may add a one-time payment option for customers with a single project.

I think the most consequential detail is not the promise of faster drawings, but the scope of the review that remains. The company says its software checks plans against local codes, yet does not describe how reliably it handles those checks or what happens when a plan fails one. Human sign-off is a meaningful boundary, but it also leaves open how much of the time saved survives professional review.

Dram’s idea dates back nearly two decades, to a thesis about automatically producing school-building documentation. ChatGPT’s release in November 2022 made the concept seem viable to him; he launched Blueprints AI within the next six months. The company was formally registered in 2025 and was named to Startup Battlefield 2026’s list of 200 promising young companies. Its founders will present at TechCrunch Disrupt in San Francisco from October 13 to 15.

The product’s real test is whether it can make the routine work smaller without making the review burden larger. If architects still have to reconstruct the reasoning behind each generated plan, the software may speed up drawing production while leaving the harder part of the job untouched.
