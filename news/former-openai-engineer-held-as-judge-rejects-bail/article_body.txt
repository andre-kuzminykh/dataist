## The bail question

Prosecutors pointed to an offer from the Saudi consulate to contact a bail-bond company and help raise money. Tse asked whether Alfozan was an “asset” of the kingdom and said the offer looked like a blank check: the government appeared willing to pay any amount to secure his release. He said he would have been more reassured by bail backed by Alfozan’s relatives.

Defense lawyer William Pernick called the flight-risk concerns unfounded. But Tse said he was not convinced Alfozan intended to keep living in the United States and saw a real risk that the case could end if he left.

The prosecution also cited Alfozan’s $385,000 salary and $2.2 million profit-sharing package from OpenAI, along with income from Meta and other US technology companies, as evidence that he had the means to flee.

The defense offered a different account of the family’s travel plans:

- Alfozan’s wife needed to leave the United States to renew her student visa. Before his arrest, he planned to travel with her.
- The couple had a one-way ticket to Riyadh, which the defense said was not unusual because Alfozan sometimes had to return early from trips for work.
- Nas Yasin, another lawyer for Alfozan, said his wife’s brother, an employee of the Saudi navy, booked her ticket as a private individual. She had recently received a US visitor visa and expected to return to California from Saudi Arabia on Tuesday.

Alfozan’s wife has not been charged. Last week, the FBI declined to say whether she might face charges. The next hearing is set for November 6.

## What the case is about

Alfozan is a US permanent resident who was moving toward citizenship. Prosecutors say he exchanged frequent WhatsApp messages with a member of the Saudi royal family in 2025 and 2026. According to a court document, the unnamed royal tried to persuade him to return to Saudi Arabia and work on a future AI initiative. In May, the person wrote that Saudi Arabia needed him more than the United States; prosecutors say Alfozan replied “100.”

Authorities have not named a possible motive. They have noted that Alfozan worked at a Saudi venture-capital firm, advised Saudi companies and was believed to have friendly ties with senior Saudi officials. Pernick said there was no evidence of espionage for Saudi Arabia or any other covert activity.

OpenAI found a concealed audio-recording device in late September, hidden in a ball of toilet paper behind a television in an executive conference room used for confidential business meetings. The company linked it to Alfozan and fired him early this month. The FBI arrested him soon afterward.

He faces up to five years in prison on one charge of using a device to intercept conversations, and prosecutors say additional charges may follow. The government cited alleged messages with an AI chatbot in which he asked for help installing listening devices, and a photograph showing him looking behind the television after OpenAI had removed the recorder. Authorities said the device’s data was encrypted, so it is unknown whether it recorded anything.

Investigators also said they found recording-device components at his home and photographs on his phone of other OpenAI surveillance cameras and a second conference room. OpenAI had previously said it found another listening device in a bag Alfozan brought to the office and left unattended.

Pernick said the electronic components at home were for Alfozan’s own projects, including efforts to improve a smart litter box. He described his client as someone who had spent 15 years working on technology projects that blurred professional and personal life.

## The gap in the story

The government’s account combines alleged surveillance, Saudi connections and a possible return to the kingdom, but it has not stated a motive. I think that gap matters: the bail ruling turns on whether Alfozan might leave the country, while the public case against him still leaves open what the alleged recording was meant to accomplish—and whether it captured anything at all.

Alfozan appeared in court Monday wearing orange jail clothing and a medical mask lowered around his neck. After the hour-long hearing, Pernick said he was disappointed by the decision and would keep seeking its review.

The ruling leaves Alfozan in custody while the case proceeds, but the central uncertainty remains unresolved: the device was found, yet authorities say they cannot establish whether it recorded a conversation.
