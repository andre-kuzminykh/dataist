## What the report says

According to The New York Times, US authorities surveilled Alice Huang and concluded that she was passing information to Beijing and was close to Jensen Huang.

About a year ago, officials briefed President Donald Trump on their suspicions. He did not act, possibly to avoid disrupting Nvidia chip sales or worsening trade relations with China. Jensen Huang was not warned; officials then believed he knew nothing about the matter.

## The Megaspeed connection

The US government suspects Megaspeed of helping China obtain Nvidia chips. From June 2024 to February 2026, the company’s Malaysian subsidiary bought at least $4.8 billion in AI equipment containing Nvidia chips.

Nvidia said meetings with customers are part of its CEO’s job and that Megaspeed does not receive confidential information. Megaspeed announced an internal investigation. A Chinese embassy representative called the allegations “groundless attacks and slander.”

I think the reported purchase figure makes this more than a story about who met whom: it gives the allegations a substantial commercial backdrop. But the public account leaves the central question unresolved. It does not say what information Alice Huang allegedly passed, or what evidence links the equipment purchases to that alleged transfer.

That gap matters. Nvidia’s response addresses the boundary around customer meetings, while the report’s allegations concern information and access. Without details about either, the company’s denial and the government’s suspicions remain difficult to assess—and Jensen Huang’s reported lack of prior knowledge sits at the center of that uncertainty.
