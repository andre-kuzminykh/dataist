OpenAI has begun testing Private Inference, a feature that lets companies send requests to the company’s most powerful models without giving it access to their data. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said the pilot is underway with its first enterprise customers. In his view, stronger privacy will expand the range of work tasks for which AI can be used. For now, OpenAI is building up computing capacity to develop the service.

# OpenAI tests private company access to its most powerful models

## How Private Inference works

OpenAI employee Shekar, who leads the project, called Private Inference the first step toward “private intelligence for everyone.” For now, the feature is intended for enterprise customers: OpenAI does not use their data to train models even without it. ChatGPT users’ conversations, by contrast, are used for training by default unless users opt out.

Private Inference requests are processed on specially isolated hardware. OpenAI says its security can be independently verified.

Altman also described Private Safety Processing, a system that lets OpenAI detect abuse without retaining customer data. Content flagged by the system is encrypted and stored in the customer’s own cloud; only a protected review system can read it.

Google is developing a similar approach, called Private AI Compute.

Dataist’s take: Private Inference separates request processing from OpenAI’s access to the data, but is currently available only to enterprise customers. To protect against abuse, the company separately describes encrypting flagged content and storing it in the customer’s cloud.
