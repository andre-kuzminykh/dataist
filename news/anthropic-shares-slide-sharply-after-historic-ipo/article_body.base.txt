# Anthropic shares surged after IPO, then quickly fell

## Share price and company valuation

Anthropic’s shares put the company’s market valuation above $1.3 trillion. That is less than the $2 trillion expected ahead of the offering, but still a substantial sum for a company that remains far from profitable.

In June, Elon Musk’s SpaceX went public at a valuation of $1.75 trillion. The company is now investing heavily in AI, but it already had a successful rocket-launch business, making a direct comparison with Anthropic’s IPO difficult.

[INFO:facts] $83 :: IPO price | $111 :: post-opening high | $97 :: share price stabilisation | $1.3 trillion :: market valuation

## Costs and risks

Anthropic’s revenue grew twelvefold in 2025, but the company’s expenses remain far higher than its income. It lost $42 billion last year, more than in 2024.

In its IPO prospectus, the company listed the possibility that AI could lead to “catastrophic or existential threats to humanity” among its risks. Such a warning could make it harder for Anthropic to win shareholders’ trust.

The listing took place amid investor concerns about debt and data-center investment. Sentiment around the AI boom was also affected by a scandal involving Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang: last week, he resigned after becoming embroiled in a story involving affairs, an alleged Chinese spy and more than one employee. The scandal could draw regulators’ attention to possible violations of federal export laws.

Lawmakers who have opposed data centers are expected to take control of Congress early next year. The source describes the convergence of these circumstances as a “perfect storm” for the industry.

Dataist's take: Anthropic’s IPO shows that strong interest in AI alone does not guarantee sustained share-price growth: after a sharp start, the shares quickly fell, while the company continues to incur heavy losses. The risks listed in the prospectus and the possibility of greater regulatory scrutiny of the industry also matter when assessing the offering.
