Voyage Labs has raised $12 million in seed funding to develop a specialized AI assistant for trip planning. Atlas, the company’s main product, is already available in closed beta. It is designed to accompany travelers from their first idea to their return home: exploring options, taking into account correspondence over several weeks, tracking price changes, and booking flights and hotels. Kleiner Perkins led the round, with SignalFire and SV Angel also participating.

# Voyage Labs raised $12 million for an AI travel assistant

## How Atlas works

Voyage Labs expects Atlas’s narrow focus to help it compete with general-purpose AI assistants. Rather than responding to a one-off request for an itinerary, the service is designed to be a constant companion throughout the trip-planning process. It takes in ideas, researches each option suggested, and adapts its recommendations over time based on conversations with the user.

Voyage Labs co-founder and CTO Yogesh Jain sees long-term planning—over several days or weeks—as a good fit for AI agents that run for extended periods. Atlas is also intended to track price fluctuations in real time, then book flights and hotels. The company describes the product as an assistant that can plan and organize an entire trip, from the first idea to the return home.

## Who is behind the startup

Voyage Labs was founded by Manik Gupta, who previously served as director of product management at Google Maps. He then spent four years as Uber’s chief product officer. Gupta said that for eight years, his work focused on building maps.

Competition in the market for travel assistants is already intense. Trip planning is often used as a clear example of what general-purpose AI systems can do: users can try it, for example, in Muse and Gemini. Voyage Labs, by contrast, is betting on a dedicated product for travel and outdoor activities. Gupta believes the market is changing quickly and that it is still difficult to predict which formats of AI assistant will be in demand in the coming years.

The company will use the funding to expand partnerships with travel service providers and grow its engineering team. Gupta said Voyage Labs aims to bring trip planning and organization together in a single end-to-end service.

Dataist's take: Voyage Labs is betting that a dedicated assistant can handle the extended process of planning a trip better than a general-purpose AI agent. Atlas is still available only in closed beta, so it is not yet possible to assess how fully it can bring together researching options, tracking prices, and booking.
