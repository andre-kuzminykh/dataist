# Novartis CEO: AI will cut drug development time by four years

## Why the forecasts differ

Narasimhan is CEO of Swiss pharmaceutical company Novartis and a member of Anthropic’s board. His cautious forecast differs from the expectations of Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and Nobel Prize in Chemistry laureate Demis Hassabis, who founded DeepMind and its subsidiary Isomorphic, a drug discovery company.

As Anthropic prepared to go public, Amodei suggested that cancer, Alzheimer’s disease, heart disease and mental health disorders could be cured in less than ten years. Last year, Hassabis said AI would help defeat all diseases within a decade.

Narasimhan says current understanding of human biology is limited. As a result, drug development will still require lengthy trials in animals and people. In his estimate, AI will reduce the average development time from 12 years to eight over the next decade. The early stage, before testing in humans, could take up to two years.

Narasimhan also expects 12% of drug candidates entering clinical trials to succeed, up from the current 8%.

## What Anthropic and Isomorphic are doing

Anthropic is investing in its own drug research, has acquired biotech startup Coefficient Bio and is building a biology lab.

Last week, Isomorphic raised $5 billion. The company is developing new drugs with pharmaceutical companies, including Novartis.

Narasimhan sees AI’s practical benefits somewhere between bold predictions of diseases soon being eradicated and skepticism from the pharmaceutical industry.

Dataist’s take: AI could speed up individual stages of drug development, but Narasimhan’s forecast leaves room for lengthy trials in animals and people. His estimate is therefore about shortening timelines and improving the success rate of clinical development, not about curing all diseases soon.
