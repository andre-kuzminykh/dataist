Can AI agents do more than solve physics problems and actually carry out research?

The authors introduce a test built from real research challenges contributed by physicists. AI agents must review previous work, plan an investigation, run calculations or experiments, and write up their findings, with checks for both correctness and scientific quality. The results suggest that today’s agents are held back less by missing facts than by shaky reasoning and a lack of the instincts experts develop through experience.

In this review we look at how the test works, what the agents were able to do, and what still stands between them and credible discoveries in physics.
