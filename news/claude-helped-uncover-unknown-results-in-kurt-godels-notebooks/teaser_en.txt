Finnish logician Jan von Plato used Claude to work through 16 of Kurt Gödel’s notebooks: 1,009 pages of notes on mathematics and logic, much of them written in German Gabelsberger shorthand. Researchers had previously made progress at about a page a week; Claude prepared an overview of the entire collection in a few days. The notebooks yielded 663 theorems and sketches: about 400 were verified without changes, around 200 were refined, and roughly 50 proved incorrect. The work also revealed topics Gödel explored decades before they drew the attention of mathematicians.
