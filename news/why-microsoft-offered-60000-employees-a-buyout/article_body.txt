## Why Microsoft offered 60,000 employees a buyout

## How programmers' work is changing

At the DealBook Summit, Nadella said the buyout was meant to let employees choose their own next steps. He described a shift in thinking about AI and programming: at first, people thought AI would not be able to write programs; then came the view that it could build any software and programmers were no longer needed. Now, Nadella said, it is becoming clear that programming still matters and will continue to evolve, even as teams are structured differently and the work itself changes.

He acknowledged that this reorganization is difficult for some employees, but said he considers it necessary. As an example, Nadella pointed to the team working on Cowork, an AI agent in Microsoft 365 apps. It has just 11 developers, who have learned to use AI in their work. They are building a software factory that produces a product, rather than building the product directly.

## Employees as managers of AI agents

Nadella believes that, over time, employees across professions will direct AI agents rather than do the work themselves. In his view, this way of managing work will spread across all kinds of jobs.

But Microsoft, now 51 years old, was not founded as an AI-focused company. Nadella noted that an established business will need more time than many expect to reshape its work, workflows and output. Pay will change too. Some employees, he added, may decide they want to take a different path.

## Why the buyout was meant to ease anxiety

Last week, Nadella discussed the same subject on The Pragmatic Engineer podcast with Gergely Orosz. He said talk of an AI revolution had made people anxious and left them feeling they had no control over their own fate.

He linked that anxiety to speculation about the singularity — a hypothetical point at which AI surpasses human intelligence — and predictions that AI will take people's jobs. To make structural changes, a company needs to move employees into new roles and give them opportunities, Nadella explained. The buyout therefore offered employees a choice: join Microsoft's new program, which would substantially change their work, or leave and pursue a different life.

Dataist's take: the buyout is tied to restructuring roles around AI, not to a claim that programmers will be replaced immediately. But Nadella himself acknowledges that the changes will take time and may prompt some employees to leave.
