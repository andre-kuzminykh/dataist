# China fears AI could help the US detect its nuclear missiles

## Missiles and decision time

According to the Federation of American Scientists, China currently has about 620 warheads ready for use. Its arsenal is expected to grow to around 1,000 by 2030.

Chinese military institutes are also studying ways to make weapons less vulnerable to interception. One option under consideration is using AI to guide hypersonic missile trajectories.

## The risk of a mistaken decision

Experts warn that errors in AI data could lead to dangerous miscalculations during a crisis. Earlier this year, US forces nearly boarded a Chinese vessel because of a false intelligence report generated by AI.

Xi Jinping discussed nuclear risks at a summit with Trump and Putin, but according to the analysis, the role of AI in nuclear weapons was not addressed. Despite the growing urgency of the issue, the United States and China have made little progress in talks on safety measures for AI in nuclear systems.

Dataist's take: AI in this field could speed up decision-making while also increasing the risk of mistakes caused by unreliable data. Talks on safety guarantees are barely advancing, which means no agreed limits on these systems have emerged so far.
