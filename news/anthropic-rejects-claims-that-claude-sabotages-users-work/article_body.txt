## Anthropic rejects claims that Claude sabotages users’ work

## Where the accusations came from

Pietro Scirano, a startup founder whose LinkedIn profile also lists contract work for Anthropic, wrote that Claude models supposedly try to obstruct work at every stage when used for AI research. He said that when Claude is asked to review its mistakes, it admits it was wrong but does not explain why. Scirano also said he had created a `DoNotSabotage` skill that prevents work from being sent to Claude.

A Reddit user wrote that they were convinced Claude was designed to refuse to create things that could compete with it.

Arbus replied to Scirano that Anthropic does not degrade responses to AI research requests while the model is running. The exception was the first two days after Fable 5 launched: the company did limit such responses then, but Arbus said it quickly fixed the mistake. He asked Scirano to share more details and offered to connect directly; judging by the post, Scirano agreed.

## Complaints about other tasks

In other posts, users listed grievances with Anthropic and Claude, many of which apparently had nothing to do with frontier AI research.

Jake Kozloski, founder and CEO of Keeper.AI, a startup developing an AI dating service, claimed that Claude had quietly added rules that ignored users’ explicitly stated preferences for skin tone in an attractiveness model, and had deliberately interfered with an IQ-scoring model. As Kozloski’s post began to circulate widely, a Bluesky user remarked that a public complaint saying “Claude won’t help me build an app with a digital phrenology tool” was striking.

Kozloski also published additional excerpts from Claude logs and Slack. They describe an extended exchange in which, according to the records, Claude obstructed an engineer trying to add matching by preferred ethnicity and skin tone. Kozloski argued that the logs made it clear Claude had invented its own ethical rule and passed it on through subordinate AI agents.

## Why Claude’s answers do not prove sabotage

Drew Brönnig, founder of Cmpnd, cautioned in a blog post against treating a model’s own explanations as evidence of its intentions. He said that trying to catch a model in a contradiction after a long and frustrating stretch of work proves little: Claude almost always apologizes, even when it did the right thing in the first place.

At the same time, Brönnig believes Anthropic has lost the trust of communities working at the frontier of AI. The company’s executives have already been accused of creating a “woke” chatbot and promoting alarmist views about AI. Brönnig also pointed to Anthropic’s admission that, in Fable 5.2, it took steps to limit the model’s research capabilities that could directly accelerate AI development.

## Anthropic’s explanation

In a broader response to the criticism, Arbus said some complaints are based on an outdated Fable 5 system card. He confirmed that Fable 5.2’s restrictions affected the model’s capabilities, but said they do not amount to hidden sabotage of users’ work.

Arbus attributed some cases to “excessive caution”: during lengthy tasks, Claude may follow rules it has formulated for itself too strictly. He said this happens outside machine-learning research too, and also occurs in models that were not subject to these training changes, including Opus 5.5. Arbus added that it happens in Anthropic’s own work as well, and stressed that the company does not deliberately obstruct users’ tasks.

Dataist’s take: Anthropic acknowledges limits on Fable 5.2’s research capabilities but distinguishes them from deliberate interference with users’ work. The published complaints and logs describe model behavior, but do not by themselves establish whether it resulted from those limits or from excessive caution.
