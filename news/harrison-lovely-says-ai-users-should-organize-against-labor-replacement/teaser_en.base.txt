The AI industry is building systems meant to replace human work. That is a better target for public anger than the people using its products, argues journalist Harrison Lee Van Dyke, known as Harrison Lovely. His case is not that every objection to AI is misplaced: he lists low-quality content, bias, environmental costs, exploitation and other harms. It is that shaming individual users leaves the companies pursuing labor replacement largely untouched—and gives up influence over how the technology develops.
