Recursive’s first model, Flint, is both a coding system and a demonstration of the process used to build it: the startup says its “Eureka Machine” automated much of the development work. The claim matters because Recursive is pursuing recursive self-improvement—not just as a way to make better AI, but as a method it hopes to apply across science. Flint’s early benchmark results and a hands-on test make the approach look promising. They do not yet show how far the automation can go, or how much of the work still depends on people.
