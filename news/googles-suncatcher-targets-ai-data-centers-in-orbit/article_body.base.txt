## The scale of the proposal

Google manager Travis Beels estimates that an orbital fleet would need about 10,000 satellites to match the capacity of a single 1 GW ground-based data center.

The engineering obstacles are just as large:

- Space has no air for cooling.
- Radiation can disable chips.
- Launch costs would need to fall to about $200 per kilogram for the model to make economic sense.

[INFO:facts] 10,000 :: satellites for 1 GW | $200 :: launch cost per kilogram | 20 years :: Bezos estimate

## The economics are the test

Jeff Bezos estimates that orbital data centers could need up to 20 years to become cheaper than terrestrial facilities. That timeline turns Suncatcher into a long-range infrastructure bet rather than an immediate answer to AI’s data-center constraints.

I think the launch-price threshold is the proposal’s real center of gravity. Solar power may make orbit attractive, but it cannot compensate for a fleet that is too expensive to put there or too difficult to keep operational.

What I’d want to know is how Google plans to move from a 10,000-satellite estimate to a workable system. The available details establish the scale and the obstacles, but not a deployment path. Until that gap is closed, Suncatcher looks more like a test of orbital economics than a new data-center buildout.
