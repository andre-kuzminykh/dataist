# How individual investors can buy Anthropic shares and assess the IPO risks

## Price and shareholder rights

The prospectus says 825 million ordinary Class A shares will be offered in the IPO. The preliminary price range is $70 to $78 a share; the final price will be set on Wednesday.

Buyers will have voting rights. But even combined, the votes attached to all these shares will account for only 49.9% of the total. That will not be enough to force the company to change its governance.

## How to apply

In the UK, several investment platforms are accepting applications for shares:

- <strong>Robinhood and eToro</strong> — a minimum investment of $250 (£189).
- <strong>Hargreaves Lansdown</strong> — a minimum of £250.
- <strong>AJ Bell</strong> — applications require at least £1,000.

AJ Bell will close applications at 13:00 on Wednesday, while Hargreaves Lansdown and Robinhood will close at 15:00. All three platforms warn that they may stop accepting applications earlier without notice.

Once the IPO is complete and trading begins, shares will be available to buy at any time, including through platforms that did not take part in the offering.

## Anthropic is already in investment funds

Some funds already hold stakes in Anthropic. In the UK, these include the Baillie Gifford US Growth and Scottish Mortgage investment trusts. Fidelity has invested in the company through funds available to US investors.

After the IPO, Anthropic shares may eventually be added to index funds, though the timing will vary. Actively managed funds will also be able to decide whether to buy the shares. Investors may therefore gain exposure to Anthropic through a fund without buying shares directly.

## Risks to consider

Anthropic’s prospectus devotes 92 pages to potential risks facing the company and the industry. Recent events, the publication notes, have highlighted the vulnerability of US markets, particularly the technology sector.

The Guardian’s financial editor, Nils Pratley, questioned Anthropic’s governance structure, saying it concentrates an unusually large amount of power in a small group of people.

Jason Hollands, managing director of investment platform Bestinvest by Evelyn Partners, which is not taking part in the IPO, points to the economics of the business. For many individual investors, the offering will be the first opportunity to invest directly in a developer of advanced AI models. But Anthropic requires substantial capital investment and has yet to establish a reliable path to profitability. Investors will need to judge whether the company can turn demand for its models into sustainable revenue.

If demand exceeds the limited number of available shares, it could support the price in the first days of trading. But that interest guarantees neither a successful start to trading nor further gains. Hollands points to SpaceX’s June offering: after intense excitement and initial interest, its shares traded below the IPO price at times.

## What Claude said

When asked whether it was worth buying Anthropic shares, Claude warned of a conflict of interest: it could not give personalized advice, was not a financial adviser, and was created by Anthropic itself.

Claude then listed questions investors should consider before buying individual shares in an IPO: whether they are prepared to lose the money invested, what portion of their portfolio the investment would take up, and whether they already have exposure to Anthropic through an index fund.

<strong>Dataist's take:</strong> High demand and a headline-grabbing valuation do not, by themselves, resolve questions about the company’s profitability and governance. Individual investors should also consider indirect ownership through funds: buying shares directly is not the only way to gain exposure to Anthropic.
