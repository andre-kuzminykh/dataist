## The work already done

LVV says Google’s contractor clear-cut about 330 hectares in Muhos without carrying out the assessment required to obtain a permit. Another 200 hectares were cleared for the planned data center in Kajaani, though officials have not yet determined whether that work was authorized.

The regulator’s order tells Tuike Finland Oy to stop all preparatory work at both sites that could significantly change the environment. LVV says the work went beyond tree removal: the contractor also stripped topsoil, built roads, prepared material-storage areas and altered ditches.

[INFO:facts] 330 hectares :: Muhos | 200 hectares :: Kajaani

Google has a little over a week to respond to Finnish officials, according to The Guardian. The state investigation is only beginning, but the company has already expressed regret. Google spokesperson Sondre Ronander told AFP the company had failed to meet its own high standards and understood why the work had caused concern. He said Google would review LVV’s findings and follow the agency’s recommendations.

## The gap between standards and oversight

The Finnish Association for Nature Conservation’s chair told AFP that Google had developed natural areas that should have been preserved. That criticism lands alongside the regulator’s account of extensive physical changes to both sites, while one key point remains unresolved: whether the clearing in Kajaani was approved.

I think the most consequential detail is not Google’s apology but the sequence: large-scale site work happened before officials had established whether the required assessment had been completed. The company says it will follow LVV’s recommendations; the harder test is whether the regulator’s review can still meaningfully constrain projects after forests have been cleared and the ground reshaped.
