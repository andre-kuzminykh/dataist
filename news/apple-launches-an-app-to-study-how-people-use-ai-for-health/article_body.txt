# Apple studies how people use AI to address health questions

## How the app works

Users will be able to ask health questions, as they would in ChatGPT or Claude. The app will draw on clinical sources, including The New England Journal of Medicine, Journal of the American Medical Association, and Nature, and provide simplified answers to everyday medical questions. Participants will be able to rate the answers and leave feedback, as well as take part in monthly surveys.

Participants can choose to share HealthKit data, including resting heart rate, activity and sleep information, and lab results. Apple says this information will help the app provide more personalized and accurate recommendations. It will also be able to help users prepare for a doctor’s appointment and refer them to specific specialists if their question involves concerning medical symptoms.

Apple says the data will be de-identified before being sent to a secure cloud operated by a third-party provider. It will not be used to train any AI models. OpenEvidence deletes data about queries and responses after 24 hours.

Apple’s vice president of health and fitness, Sumbul Desai, said the company believes scientific rigor and clinical grounding are the foundation of responsible innovation. She said the study will help Apple understand how people interact with medical AI in their daily lives, understand its answers, and assess them. The findings are intended to contribute to scientific discussion and help Apple build tools that support people with health questions.

The app is available to download for users in the United States. It is not yet known whether Apple plans to add AI answers to the Health app or Siri. Earlier this year, OpenAI said users ask more than 300 million health questions a week.

Dataist's take: The study will give Apple data on how people use AI-generated medical answers, but it does not by itself mean such a feature will appear in Health or Siri. Participation is limited to users in the United States with eligible devices and settings, and the results will not be available for at least 12 months.
