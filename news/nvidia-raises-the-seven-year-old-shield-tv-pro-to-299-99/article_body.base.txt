## What changed

Shield TV Pro launched at $199.99. Nvidia later discontinued the non-Pro model, which had sold for $149.99, leaving the Pro as the only version still on sale.

Nvidia told Ars Technica the increase reflects higher component costs, including memory. A company representative said the new $299 price took effect on October 2 and that component costs have risen substantially across the industry.

The hardware dates to 2019 and runs on the Tegra X1+, an older chip that remains fast enough for a media player. Shield TV runs Android TV, uses AI to upscale video and supports hardware decoding for almost any media format.

## The cost of keeping it alive

Earlier this year, Nvidia’s Andrew Bell described Shield as a passion project. He said the company continues to make it and sells every unit it produces. But Nvidia has no stockpile of hardware components bought before the AI boom, leaving Shield exposed to the same supply constraints affecting modern phones and computers.

The broader market is already passing higher costs on to buyers. Nintendo, Microsoft and Sony have raised prices for current-generation consoles. The PS5 Pro, for example, rose from $699.99 at its 2024 launch to $899.99.

Nvidia is not just caught in that pressure: it is one of the central players in AI’s expansion, and its demand for components contributes to the strain. Its market-leading AI accelerators and graphics processors have helped make it one of the world’s most valuable companies.

## A harder sell for casual streamers

Shield TV Pro stock has been low for the past several months. One possibility is that rising component costs made new units too expensive to produce; at $300, Nvidia may have more room to make them in greater numbers.

I think that explanation matters less to a buyer than the trade-off: $300 is a steep price for a box whose appeal depends on features many streaming customers may not need. Nvidia’s wide codec support and the option to use Shield as a Plex server make a stronger case than Netflix playback alone.

What I’d want to know is whether the higher price will bring more reliable availability, or simply make a scarce niche product more expensive. Nvidia’s store still lists Shield as unavailable, while Best Buy sells it at the new price. The increase may help the product remain in production, but it also narrows the audience willing to pay for what it does.
