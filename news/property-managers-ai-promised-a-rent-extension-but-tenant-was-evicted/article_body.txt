# Property manager’s AI promised a rent extension, but tenant was evicted

## A letter sent on behalf of the property manager

In their exchange, Olivia first asked when Mack would be able to pay, then confirmed that November 30, the last day of the month, would work. A few days later, Mack received an eviction notice. When she contacted the office, an employee told her, she says, that the AI agent had mistakenly granted the extension.

In December 2025, the apartment complex told residents that a new AI assistant would answer questions about rent and maintenance. Olivia’s emails were normally supposed to be labeled as AI-generated, but Mack’s messages had no such label. The exchange also seemed personal: the assistant mentioned a hole in the bathroom ceiling downstairs and expressed sympathy. The emails were signed, “Best wishes, Colony Townhome Apartments.”

Mack assumed she was corresponding with an office employee: the email included her personal information and mentioned the bathroom. She told WRAL that she felt deceived because the messages gave no indication that AI was responding. She also said she had nowhere to stay.

## A bill requiring AI disclosure

The case angered local lawmakers. State Senator Jim Burgin told WRAL on Friday that people should be notified when they are communicating with AI, and that the question of liability deserves separate consideration. In his view, staff should have reviewed the documents before evicting Mack.

Burgin proposed a bill called the “Bot Act,” which would require people to be told when they are communicating with AI. But during the three years the senator has tried to introduce the measure, it has not advanced in the legislature.

Mack is now staying in a hotel while she looks for permanent housing or seeks to have the eviction reviewed in court. She said that if she had been promised an extension through November 30, the property management company should have kept its promise and taken responsibility for it.

Dataist’s take: This story raises a practical question: if a company assigns AI to communicate on its behalf, can it disavow the AI’s promises by calling them a mistake? In this case, residents were not told AI was involved, and the bill that would require disclosure has yet to advance.
