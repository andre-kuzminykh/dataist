Singaporean cloud services and AI infrastructure provider Megaspeed, led by Alice Huang for more than two years, is set to be added to the US Commerce Department’s Entity List, Semafor reported. The company has become central to an alleged espionage scandal involving Nvidia and its chief, Jensen Huang. Once listed, US companies will need a special federal license to do business with Megaspeed or sell it goods. According to anonymous sources cited by Semafor, the measure is intended to prevent the company from obtaining US-made semiconductors.

## Megaspeed may be added to the US export restrictions list

## What the restrictions would change

The US Commerce Department’s Entity List names foreign parties subject to export restrictions. Its purpose is to prevent sensitive technology from reaching parties that could threaten US national security or American citizens.

Megaspeed would be able to buy goods from US companies and do business with them only after obtaining a special federal license. Sources familiar with the situation told Semafor that the restrictions are intended to cut off the company’s access to US-made semiconductors.

## Investigations into Nvidia shipments

During the period when Nvidia chief Jensen Huang acknowledged being in a relationship with Alice Huang, Megaspeed was one of Nvidia’s largest chip buyers.

Last week, the US Commerce Department announced a criminal investigation into Megaspeed. US and Singaporean authorities are investigating whether the company violated export restrictions and illegally routed Nvidia chips to China. Megaspeed denies any wrongdoing.

TechCrunch requested comment from the US Commerce Department and said it would update its report if it received a response.

## Jensen Huang returns to lead Nvidia

On Monday, Jensen Huang returned as Nvidia’s chief after a brief suspension by the board amid the scandal. Before that, President Donald Trump had advocated for his return.

Dataist’s take: Adding Megaspeed to the list could limit its access to US technology, but would not in itself mean that investigations have established any violations. The company denies wrongdoing, and US and Singaporean authorities are continuing their review.
