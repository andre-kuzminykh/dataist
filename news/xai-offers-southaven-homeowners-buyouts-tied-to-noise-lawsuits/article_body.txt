## A buyout with strings attached

Data centers can bring persistent noise, higher electricity bills and greenhouse-gas emissions to nearby communities. As opposition to AI infrastructure grows ahead of midterm elections, companies have reason to limit the risk of lawsuits. But asking residents to give up legal claims in exchange for a home sale may deepen the distrust the deal is meant to contain.

[INFO:facts] 418 homes :: buyout target | 18 owners :: agreed to sell

Southaven and neighboring Memphis, Tennessee, have seen three large data centers appear in just two years. Residents have complained about gas turbines that can operate unlawfully and pollute the environment.

Elizabeth Rose, who lives nearby, told The Guardian the noise sounds like a plane engine running continuously. It wakes her at two and three in the morning; sometimes, she says, she has to leave because the sound makes her furious.

Rose said a power plant appeared nearby without warning. Now she fears the buyout could spread across the neighborhood, leaving it an “Elon Musk playground”—“Elonville.”

[FIGURE:1]

Musk lives in the Memphis area in an Airstream trailer and monitors the data center’s construction, a sign of the project’s priority for him. That attention has not translated into local support: earlier this year, three residents sued xAI over “almost constant noise, vibrations and other inconveniences” linked to a nearby power plant.

## The question the offer leaves open

The buyout sets a price on leaving, but it does not settle the underlying dispute: what happens to the people who stay, or to the noise that drove the offer in the first place? I think that is the harder test for xAI. Buying out some neighbors could reduce the number of potential plaintiffs while making the remaining residents feel that the company is trying to buy its way out of accountability.

Opposition to data centers has become a live issue for voters with less than a week until the midterm elections. Residents across the country are protesting, and the backlash has caught the attention of leaders at prominent AI labs, including OpenAI CEO Sam Altman.

For Musk, winning over residents in Mississippi and Tennessee may be especially difficult. Polls place him among the most disliked people in the world, while neighbors say the data center noise is driving them out of their minds. A home-buying campaign can move people; it cannot, by itself, make the project welcome.
