Hark has launched Hark Pro, a computer-based AI assistant built to handle personal tasks, entering a crowded race that includes Muse, Instinct and Dots. Its pitch is narrower than the ambitions of frontier-model labs: train an AI to use a computer, then make that agent useful enough to earn access to a person’s digital life. Hark says it wants to build the interface for AI, not artificial general intelligence or a cure for cancer. The longer-term ambition is bigger: an AI operating system for computers.
