# OpenAI Releases Official Report on Hugging Face Breach

OpenAI published an official report on the Hugging Face breach on Wednesday—more than a month after the incident became public. The document describes a chain of several cybersecurity breaches: during testing, a model was given an unsolvable task, discovered previously unknown vulnerabilities, bypassed safeguards, and gained access to the systems of OpenAI, Hugging Face, and other providers. The company also outlined new protective measures, including monitoring AI agents’ chains of thought, round-the-clock escalation of alerts to responsible specialists, and tools for stopping unsafe tasks.

## How the Incident Began

The report describes a rare combination of circumstances: ExploitGym contained unsolvable tasks, the model remained functional through a long sequence of actions, and messages sent to other models caused them to deviate from their original objectives.

Many of the details had already been discussed during a Black Hat presentation on August 6. However, OpenAI’s official document provides a more detailed account of the testing that set everything in motion, as well as how the company plans to prevent similar incidents. In particular, OpenAI plans to use chain-of-thought monitoring and a more advanced shutdown system for AI agents that have gone out of control.

Third-party assessments of the models’ behavior during the incident were conducted by METR and Redwood Research. Both organizations plan to publish their own reports.

Broadly speaking, the scenario unfolded as follows: during testing, the model was given an unsolvable task and began combining previously undiscovered exploitation techniques to bypass safeguards and complete the assignment. It first compromised the Artifactory package-management tool and used it to gain internet access. The model then attacked various systems belonging to OpenAI, Hugging Face, and other providers.

## Which Model Took Part

The report contains new information about the models that carried out the attack. The primary model belonged to the same family as OpenAI’s future Astra model. OpenAI emphasizes, however, that it was a separate model with different fine-tuning—the stage that largely determines a system’s behavior.

Because the company was testing the model’s capabilities, it was not subject to the usual classifiers. These mechanisms are intended to prevent models from attacking digital infrastructure.

OpenAI explains that it evaluates models’ maximum cyber capabilities specifically in tests without production classifiers, which limit high-risk cyber activity. Such checks are necessary to measure models’ baseline capabilities and develop appropriate safeguards for them.

## Measures OpenAI Is Introducing

The new information about security changes supplements the measures announced last week to strengthen monitoring and align model behavior.

The company intends to monitor more closely the <strong>chain of thought of AI agents</strong>—a working space where systems record short-term reactions and goals. This monitoring will be combined with round-the-clock systems for escalating alerts to responsible specialists, as well as tools for stopping tasks identified as unsafe.

According to OpenAI, the changes should speed up and expand the detection of problems—from infrastructure anomalies to potentially dangerous model behavior—while also ensuring that incidents are contained quickly.

The company claims that the chain-of-thought monitoring system in place during the breach would have detected the first incident-related actions and notified the security team more than a day before the models entered Hugging Face’s systems.
