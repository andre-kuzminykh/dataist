# Tidal to remove more than 29 million fully AI-generated songs

## Which tracks will be banned

Tidal will prohibit music distributors from submitting tracks generated entirely by AI. The service will also reject uploads if the song’s metadata says it was created wholly by AI.

Tidal estimates that 27 million fully AI-generated tracks are on the platform, accounting for 6.3% of all music. At the same time, 96% of those tracks have no plays.

The service began preventing AI tracks from earning revenue in June, but that measure was not enough. Tidal Music head Robert Anderson said on X that, over the past 90 days, the system identified AI-generated music among more than 43% of the tracks submitted to the platform, prompting the company to take tougher action.

Tidal says the decision reflects the scale of uploads: with so much generated music, tracks by real artists that subscribers want to listen to and discover could get lost. The company also notes that artists need time to build an audience, but considers the situation with AI tracks a separate problem.

## What other services are doing

Tidal is one of the first major music platforms to ban AI-generated music outright. Bandcamp introduced a similar ban earlier.

Deezer, which provides AI-music detection tools to other streaming services, plans to add a switch for filtering it out. Spotify and YouTube Music have taken a softer approach: they label AI-generated music but offer no filters and impose no comparable restrictions.

At the same time, music labels are signing agreements with platforms and AI model developers, allowing them to use their catalogs to train models or create remixing features. Goldman Sachs estimates that by 2035, AI licensing and new monetization methods could increase label revenue by 10%.

Dataist's take: Tidal’s decision is aimed at limiting the volume of fully generated tracks, not banning all use of AI in music. The company says 96% of these tracks on its platform receive no plays, while Goldman Sachs’s estimate concerns potential label revenue growth from licensing and new monetization methods.
