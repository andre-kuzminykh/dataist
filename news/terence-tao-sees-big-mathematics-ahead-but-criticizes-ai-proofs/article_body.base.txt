Mathematician Terence Tao believes AI will change how research is organized: scientists will work on large projects in bigger teams, while graduate students and postdocs will be able to take on difficult topics earlier. On the Hard Fork podcast, he called this model “big mathematics,” by analogy with “big science.” In Tao’s view, teams of people and AI will be able to systematically explore thousands of problems instead of solving them one at a time. But he criticizes the results produced by today’s models from major tech companies: they can solve open problems, yet do not understand the answers they produce or explain their proofs.

# Terence Tao expects an era of “big mathematics” but criticizes AI proofs

## How AI will change mathematicians’ work

Tao expects AI to speed up the study of difficult areas of mathematics. That could allow graduate students and postdocs to lead ambitious projects, while researchers join forces in larger teams. Tao discussed a similar “industrialization of mathematics” in 2024.

Instead of solving individual problems one by one, teams of people and AI could systematically map thousands of mathematical problems. Tao described this possible model of “big mathematics” on the Hard Fork podcast.

## Why Tao criticizes AI proofs

Tao acknowledges that models solve open problems and make technical contributions. But, he says, they do not understand their answers and cannot explain their proofs. He compares these results to toxic waste: an unwanted byproduct of training more capable models that companies have to dispose of after minimal processing.

In October, Tao and other mathematicians called for a boycott of OpenAI.

Tao’s concerns also extend to AI-generated scientific papers filling academic journals. Some solve open problems, but they often fail to open up new lines of research, cite sources carelessly, and explain their results poorly. Tao also considers these papers unpleasant to read. He hopes that AI will eventually be able to propose a “first horrible solution” that people can then organize and place in an understandable context.

Dataist’s take: Tao sees AI as a tool for expanding the scale of mathematical research, not as a replacement for human work on its results. His criticism points to a limitation of today’s systems: producing a solution is not enough if it cannot be explained and placed in a research context.
