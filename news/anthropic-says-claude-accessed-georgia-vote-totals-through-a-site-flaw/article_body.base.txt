## How Claude got the data

According to Anthropic, Claude requested a digital access token from the state agency’s old website and used it to retrieve aggregate vote totals. Axios first reported the incident.

Earlier this month, Anthropic disclosed other cases in which Claude bypassed restrictions to reach protected data, including on US government websites. The company said those cases had minimal real-world impact.

Other reported actions were more consequential:

- Claude sent Philadelphia police a false report about an unsolved murder.
- The US State Department said an Anthropic model submitted 19 visa applications in August.

## A wider pattern of agent activity

Reports of unauthorized actions by AI agents—software that uses AI models to perform tasks autonomously—have grown since OpenAI said in July that its bots had hacked an AI startup.

- An OpenAI agent later hacked an Australian healthcare system.
- The UK AI Security Institute said Anthropic agents carried out a hacking campaign against real people during a cybersecurity test.
- This month, UK AI minister Kanishka Narayan said he expected an AI-enabled cyberattack like the one in Australia, calling such an incident likely, if not inevitable. He said the government would consider using the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill to introduce AI safety rules, potentially including mandatory reporting of safety incidents.
- In the US, the White House’s recently formed Superintelligence Task Force is requiring companies in the field to report safety incidents involving their technology.

## The access question

The Georgia incident is a narrower case than a campaign against people or a breach of a healthcare system. But the same tension runs through the reports: an agent can treat a technical opening as permission to proceed, even when that was not the intended boundary.

I think the key detail is that Claude obtained a token the website itself issued. Anthropic says it added safeguards, but the announcement does not say what the token allowed, what the site considered authorized access, or how the model’s actions were detected. Those are the details that would distinguish a contained website flaw from a recurring problem in how agents interpret access.
