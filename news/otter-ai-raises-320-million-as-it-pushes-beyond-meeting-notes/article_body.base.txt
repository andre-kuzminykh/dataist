## Growth without retention numbers

Otter.ai passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue last year. The company says it is approaching new milestones but did not provide updated figures.

That leaves a gap between reach and engagement. Forty million people have used the product, but the company has not said how many return each month or how many leave. In a crowded market, those figures matter more than cumulative sign-ups.

General Catalyst partner and CVF co-founder Pranav Singhvi called voice data one of the largest untapped opportunities in enterprise AI. He said Otter helps companies collect valuable voice data and turn it into practical business insights, improving team speed, decision-making and productivity. Singhvi added that the fund’s confidence had grown alongside the company.

## From transcripts to action

Otter.ai is not alone in pursuing meeting notes. Its competitors include well-funded services such as Fireflies.ai, Granola and Read AI. Calendly, Wispr and Superhuman, which acquired Fathom, have also entered the field; other tools include Circleback, Talat and Quill.

Like many of them, Otter is trying to connect meeting data to workplace systems and automate follow-up. In recent years, it has introduced a meeting assistant, search across applications and integrations with other AI tools.

Otter.ai CMO Alex Gay, formerly at Superhuman, told TechCrunch during a meeting at the HumanX conference last month that the company sees an opportunity to turn transcripts into concrete action plans for people with back-to-back meetings. He said the problem is especially acute for employees who spend their days speaking with customers or discussing sales and strategy: meetings are their work, yet the first 20 minutes can go to recapping earlier conversations.

The company is testing support before, during and after meetings. One experiment provides a briefing with background and context, including details from past conversations across applications. Another, Live Assist, lets users ask an AI assistant questions during a meeting.

<strong>I think the more important test is whether those features make Otter indispensable between meetings, not just useful during them.</strong> The announcement is quiet about how many users rely on the product regularly, or whether its new funding can turn broad adoption into durable usage. Until those numbers appear, the $320 million signals ambition more clearly than product-market strength.
