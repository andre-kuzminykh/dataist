# Anthropic is developing a way to verify which model produced an answer

## How FlashProve works

FlashProve creates a verifiable proof that a result came from a specific model. The developers intend it to work like a digital signature on an email: a recipient can check who sent a message, while a user can check which model generated a response.

The system is designed to prevent one model from being substituted for another. It could, for example, stop a lab that built and runs a model from secretly replacing it with a cheaper one to cut costs, or a more expensive one to inflate its performance metrics.

FlashProve could also help verify claims by third parties about which model performed a task. A developer might say they checked a software library for vulnerabilities using a leading cybersecurity model. A company might say its business plan was approved by a suitable model. The system is intended to let users verify such claims.

## Fingerprints without revealing weights

To use FlashProve, labs will need to publish a “fingerprint” for each model they release. It can then be matched to a specific output and cryptographically verified.

A fingerprint does not reveal a model’s weights — the internal numerical parameters that determine its behavior. Anthropic and OpenAI, among others, keep those parameters secret. This would let labs publish fingerprints without disclosing weights and use the system with commercial models as well.

The developers are also designing FlashProve to be lightweight, so verification can run on a local device.

## Research is ongoing

Along with the announcement, the researchers posted a detailed technical specification and working code for different parts of the system on GitHub. The materials are available under an open-source license.

Development of FlashProve is ongoing. Anthropic stresses that the published results reflect work in progress, not a completed research program. The company has not announced plans to deploy the system itself or at other labs; the researchers hope other specialists will study the published materials and improve them.

Dataist's take: FlashProve offers a way to verify where responses came from without revealing the weights of commercial models. For now, it remains a research project: there is no deployment timeline, and its practical value depends on whether labs publish fingerprints for their models.
