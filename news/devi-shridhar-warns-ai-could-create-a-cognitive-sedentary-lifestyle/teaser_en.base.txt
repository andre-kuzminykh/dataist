Devi Shridhar, chair of global public health at the University of Edinburgh, is warning that AI could create a cognitive version of the sedentary lifestyle. At a university conference, she argued that systems designed to remove effort from work and learning may also remove the activity that keeps minds capable. The comparison comes from her own earlier mistake: in the early 2000s, she dismissed the social and health costs of streaming video, even as a consulting partner saw its commercial potential. Netflix’s global revenue later reached $12.6 billion (£9.5 billion) in 2026. Shridhar’s concern is that AI may repeat the pattern at a deeper level.
