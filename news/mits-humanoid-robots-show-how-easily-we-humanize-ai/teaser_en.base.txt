At MIT CSAIL, Norton shows two Unitree humanoid robots, each worth about $50,000. A graduate student says the manufacturer’s advertised $16,000 price is not real. The robots are usually seen in choreographed videos; here, one feels close enough to greet. The author knows it has no consciousness, but still takes Gabe’s gloved, flexible hand and says hello. That small reflex is the point: people can know a machine is not human and still respond to it as if it were.
