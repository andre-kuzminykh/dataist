A small AI model may soon be able to guide a robot through a task by writing and running code.

The authors introduce CaPtain, a way to train an open vision-language model to control a robot through simple commands for seeing, measuring, and moving. It first learns from example tasks, then improves through trial and error in simulation, where a separate evaluator helps identify which actions moved the robot closer to success. The result is a compact model that works across simulated tasks and can be adapted to a real robot without relying on a powerful closed AI or a pre-trained robot-control model.

This review walks through how CaPtain trains its robot agent, why learning from delayed outcomes is challenging, and what the results suggest about bringing capable robot control to smaller, open models.
