# Anthropic founders sold nearly $2 billion in shares as the company went public

## How much the founders received

Each of the seven founders sold about 3.4 million shares. The calculations suggest they chose the number of shares to receive about $250 million at $74, the midpoint of the IPO’s initial price range. After the price was raised to $83, each made about $30 million more.

Anthropic said the founders had pledged to donate 80% of their shares in the company to charity. The pledges are voluntary and not legally binding, however: each co-founder can withdraw theirs.

## How much money Anthropic raised

The company itself raised about $104.8 billion. That figure could rise to $117 billion if the banks buy all the shares allocated to them. In addition to ordinary Class A shares, Anthropic sold $26 billion in special non-voting Class N shares to major technology companies.

- Nvidia bought $10 billion in Class N shares.
- Google bought $10 billion in shares.
- AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC split the remainder.

For comparison, Facebook raised $16 billion on the day of its IPO in 2012, which was a record at the time.

## Google and Amazon stakes

At the higher IPO price, Google ended up with 13.4% of Anthropic. Amazon also holds a large stake: through earlier investments in the startup, it received promissory notes that could be converted into shares. As part of the IPO, Amazon converted some of the notes and received 14.6%. Had the company converted them all, its stake could have approached 24%.

Together, Google and Amazon, two major cloud providers, now own 28% of Anthropic. That figure comes from the company itself.

## Infrastructure commitments exceed $1 trillion

Even after raising nearly $105 billion, Anthropic has about $230 billion in available capital, including funds it already had. Its future spending, however, is significantly higher.

As of the quarter ended September 30, 2026, Anthropic needs about $70.7 billion for computing capacity and infrastructure over the next 12 months. It will need $451.6 billion for these commitments through the end of 2030. The company also has hundreds of billions of dollars in additional commitments, some of which depend on future conditions or have not yet been estimated.

Overall, Anthropic disclosed an estimated range of $1.05 trillion to $1.08 trillion in future infrastructure commitments.

Dataist’s take: The IPO gave Anthropic a large cash cushion, but the scale of its commitments shows how capital-intensive the race for computing infrastructure is. Nearly $230 billion in available capital does not cover even all of the company’s disclosed future spending.
