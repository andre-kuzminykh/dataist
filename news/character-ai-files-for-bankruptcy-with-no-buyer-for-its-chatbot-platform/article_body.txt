## A sale without a buyer

Adams said Character.AI hopes an offer will allow the platform to continue. If no such offer arrives, the service will shut down. The company expects the sale to close in late November or early December.

Disney will provide transition services through former Character.AI employees who moved to the entertainment company last month alongside former CEO Karandeep Singh Anand.

## From breakout growth to legal pressure

Former Google engineers Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas founded Character.AI in late 2021. Shazeer co-authored the landmark paper on transformers. The startup initially trained foundation models and built a chatbot platform where users could create characters to talk with.

By September 2022, Character.AI was valued at $1 billion after raising $150 million. The company said users had sent its bots more than 2 billion messages in five months, spending an average of more than two hours a day on the platform.

The mobile apps drew more than 1.7 million installs in their first week after launching in May 2023. In August 2024, Google struck a $2.7 billion deal to license the startup’s models, and Shazeer and De Freitas returned to Google. Character.AI’s general counsel, Dominic Perella, took over as interim CEO, and the company shifted its focus from training foundation models to growing the business. By October, the platform had 20 million monthly active users.

The legal pressure intensified after a 14-year-old Florida teenager died by suicide. His mother alleged that he had become obsessed with a Character.AI chatbot. Texas authorities later opened an investigation into whether the service complied with child safety and privacy laws. Character.AI settled some lawsuits, added safety features and barred minors from using the platform.

Anand, previously at Brex and Meta, became CEO in June last year. The company broadened its products with AI video creation and social feeds, then tried interactive stories and short drama series to attract users. The bankruptcy filing suggests those additions were not enough to make the business sustainable.

## Engagement has a cost

Sensor Tower data placed Character.AI among the apps where users spent the most time. That is an unusually strong engagement signal, but it cuts both ways: more use can mean higher AI costs, while the company still has to find a way to monetize its audience.

PitchBook says Character.AI raised more than $193 million across several funding rounds. Its investors included Lightspeed, SV Angels, Greycroft and a16z.

I think the sharper question is what a buyer could preserve: the audience, the characters users built, or the business around them. The announcement names no buyer and no offer, leaving the platform’s most visible asset without a clear path to continue.
