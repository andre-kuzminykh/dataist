## What moves to the factory

DSX POD shifts much of the work off-site. Partners will install computing and networking equipment, route cables, fit power and liquid-cooling systems, then test each module before delivery. At the destination, operators still need a prepared site and connections to utilities and networks.

Nvidia’s six-month-to-14-day claim is about launch time, not the time needed to build the facility from scratch. The company says earlier operation means earlier revenue for data-center operators, but the announcement gives no details on what the 14-day figure includes or how it was measured.

## The water claim has a caveat

Nvidia also says the DSX platform could reduce water use for cooling by up to 100%. In closed-loop systems, heat is transferred to the air without evaporating water. The company says some climates may still require additional cooling during periods of extreme heat.

[INFO:compare] Six months :: current launch time | 14 days :: claimed launch time | 100% :: potential water reduction

I think the most consequential part of this announcement is not the headline timeline but the gap between factory testing and a working data center. The claim depends on a prepared site and on the remaining connections being completed quickly; Nvidia has not said what conditions make that possible. The water figure, too, is a maximum with a stated climate caveat, not a guarantee for every deployment.

The first shipments are planned for early 2027. Until then, the central question is whether the factory-built modules can make the on-site work predictable enough to deliver the promised speed.
