## What drove the costs

Anthropic has faced a shortage of computing capacity since late last year. Since then, it has signed dozens of agreements with Amazon, Google, SpaceX, chipmakers and former cryptocurrency miners, The New York Times reports.

The company expects access to more than 3.5 gigawatts of electricity this year and plans to double that amount next year. The $5 billion in reserved capacity is therefore not just a one-off expense: it sits within a broader effort to secure the infrastructure Anthropic says it needs.

The other $2 billion went to a program that doubles employee donations. The company has named both costs as major reasons its expenses rose.

## The IPO overhang

The updated prospectus lists $895 billion in lease obligations for computing capacity and data centers. The New York Times reports that an IPO could raise around $100 billion, with Japanese investors expected to contribute up to $3.5 billion.

The gap between those figures is striking, but they describe different things: the IPO estimate is potential fundraising, while the $895 billion is a lease commitment. The announcement does not say how those obligations are spread over time.

I think the more important question is whether the infrastructure commitments will translate into enough usable capacity to support the company’s growth. Anthropic’s plan to double its access to electricity next year signals how much more it expects to need; the IPO, if it goes ahead, would be a funding event set against that long runway of commitments.
