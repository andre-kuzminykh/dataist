Pinterest has expanded its parental controls: parents can now regulate how much AI-generated content their children see. The new feature adds to controls for notifications and autoplay. Pinterest is among the first major social networks to let parents manage AI content shown in a teenager’s main feed and recommendations.

## What’s changing in the settings

The new setting applies specifically to AI content in the feed. On other platforms, parental controls more often restrict teens’ access to individual AI-powered features, such as chatbots and AI characters. There are still few options for controlling AI-generated material in recommendations.

Snap added a setting that prevents its AI chatbot from responding to teens. Meta said it was globally suspending teens’ access to existing AI characters while it reworked the product based on parents’ feedback. In WhatsApp, parents can limit teens’ access to Meta AI by blocking them from creating content with AI or getting answers to questions.

Parents and child advocates link the spread of AI content to harm to children’s well-being and to companies’ interests. In April, child advocacy group Fairplay sent a letter to YouTube CEO Neal Mohan and Google CEO Sundar Pichai. More than 200 organizations and experts signed it. They called for AI content to be removed from YouTube Kids and for AI videos not to be recommended to users under 18.

## Pinterest’s other measures

Pinterest introduced settings in 2025 to limit content altered with AI. However, that content still appears in users’ feeds, even when the controls are on.

The new parental controls arrived alongside other settings:

- turn off autoplay;
- adjust notifications, including push notifications, browser and app notifications, and email;
- manage personalized ads.

Pinterest also redesigned its parental controls page to make the features easier to find and configure. The new settings let parents decide what content teens see in the app.

Dataist’s take: Pinterest is adding parental controls at the feed level, an area where social networks still offer few comparable settings. But the platform’s earlier controls did not fully remove AI content from recommendations, so the new feature’s effectiveness will depend on how consistently it can change what teens are shown.
