## What happened at AGI House

AGI House is a Hillsborough mansion where a dozen or more people live together and regularly host parties. In December, a woman called the Hillsborough police station at 2 a.m. from a Lyft. She said a resident had prevented her from leaving and tried to pressure her into having sex.

According to the police report, the man told her she was old and would “be alone forever.” He repeatedly tried to persuade her despite her refusals. She eventually climbed over a gate and left the house unharmed.

The police had received 37 reports involving AGI House since 2022. At least 17 of those reports concerned parties held at the property.

AGI House was founded by Jeremy Nixon, a former Google researcher who had already launched other technology houses. He described the concept as a place where “20 AI hackers live in one epic house for three months.”

The house was originally called NeoGenesis. It took the AGI House name after OpenAI released ChatGPT and the AI boom began. Before NeoGenesis, Nixon co-founded another hacker house, Genesis, in 2019.

Genesis also had a troubling connection to parties. A 19-year-old technology entrepreneur told The New York Times that he had secretly entered parties there when he was 13.

## The experience residents describe

In December 2021, 30-year-old Ehon Zhu filed a police report accusing a former Genesis resident of rape and assault. Before that, Zhu had lived in four technology houses and viewed them as almost utopian spaces for entrepreneurs. The incident changed her view.

The investigation remains open. Zhu said the accused former Genesis resident had not left the house, but investigators were unable to find him.

Zhu later founded the startup Zette AI. She told The New York Times that she was far from the only person to experience something similar.

The pattern matters because these houses are not random groups of strangers. They can sit close to the industry’s formal power structure. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman advised Residency, another technology house, while Google co-founder Sergey Brin attended dinners at AGI House.

These communities shape the beliefs and relationships of people who may become future AI employees and executives. They also create networks that can affect careers. The next Sam Altman or Elon Musk may spend important years of early adulthood in one of these houses.

## The missing layer is accountability

I think the central issue is not that young technologists live together or throw parties. It is that a private social environment can also function as an informal talent pipeline, with access to influential people and career-making connections, without showing who is responsible when the environment becomes unsafe.

The organizers appear to understand the influence these houses carry. Rocky Yu, AGI House’s current manager and a venture investor, is involved in a legal dispute with the former manager over the rights to the AGI House brand.

The police were asked to check on people at AGI House at least four times. In one case, an ambulance was sent to the house.

What I’d want to know is who screens residents and guests, who can remove someone from the property, and how complaints are handled when the same network is also a source of status and opportunity. Without that structure, the houses risk turning access to the future of AI into a shield for the people already inside it.
