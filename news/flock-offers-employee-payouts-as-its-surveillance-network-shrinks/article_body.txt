## What Flock is offering

Flock calls the package the “most generous” voluntary compensation program in its history. The company says it gives employees more control over what they do next.

Applications opened Friday. Flock has not said how many employees have applied or how many requests it will approve.

- Several months of medical coverage
- Two years to exercise stock options after leaving
- Some offers worth tens of thousands of dollars

Employees are due to hear the decisions on October 9. Most approved participants are expected to leave by October 29.

## A network under pressure

The employee program is unfolding alongside a sharp rise in contract cancellations. Secure Justice says at least 214 cities and counties have ended their Flock contracts since 2021. In 2026, 90 contracts were cancelled or allowed to expire. During August, local administrations ended an average of three Flock contracts per day.

[INFO:facts] 214 :: cities and counties ended contracts since 2021 | 90 :: contracts cancelled or not renewed in 2026 | 3 :: contract endings per day in August

The pressure is not limited to criticism of the company. There are also rumors that Flock may sell part of its business or the entire company.

One person familiar with the situation told Wired that the direction became clear after a large number of vandalism incidents.

Flock’s product depends on scale: its services are built around broad surveillance coverage. When one municipality drops out, the network becomes less useful to the customers that remain. Some police departments have explicitly cited the loss of coverage after neighboring cities ended their contracts as a reason for leaving.

That creates a feedback loop. Fewer contracts mean less coverage; less coverage gives more customers a reason to reconsider their own contracts.

## The gap between reassurance and action

Less than a month ago, when Flock was asked about the cancellation data, a company representative said renewals were outpacing terminations. The new departure program makes that reassurance harder to read at face value.

My read is that the package is less a routine benefit than an attempt to give Flock a controlled way to reduce its workforce while uncertainty around the business grows. Calling it voluntary gives employees a choice, but it also lets the company avoid presenting the move as a conventional round of layoffs.

The more important unknown is not how generous the individual offers are. It is what Flock expects its operating footprint to look like after the contract losses. What I’d want to know is whether the company is preparing for a smaller network, a sale, or both. Until Flock answers that, the employee payouts look like a signal about the business’s future, not just a perk for departing staff.
