## How the limit works

Users will be able to check their remaining tokens and the reset time in settings. Conversations with Instant models will not count toward the limit.

That leaves the subscription price fixed, but not necessarily the experience. The article’s implication is that heavy users on cheaper plans may get lower-quality answers once their allowance runs low.

## A simpler interface, with a usage ceiling

OpenAI recently doubled limits on more expensive plans, possibly in preparation for the merger. The company is also facing a usability problem: ChatGPT currently offers two work modes and 11 model settings, a combination that can confuse even experienced users.

Anthropic has already combined Chat and Cowork in Claude. OpenAI’s move may therefore simplify how people choose a way to work, while making usage limits more central to the product.

I think the important trade-off is not just whether users can understand the settings. It is whether a shared limit makes the cheaper plans feel less predictable: the price stays fixed, but intensive use can still mean waiting, paying more or accepting weaker answers. What the announcement leaves unclear is how much usage each plan will include—and how often a typical user will hit the ceiling.
