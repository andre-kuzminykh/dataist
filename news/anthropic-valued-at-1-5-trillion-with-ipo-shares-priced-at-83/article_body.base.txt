Anthropic has set its IPO share price at $83, above the initial range of $70–78, according to media reports. At that price, the company’s valuation, including all diluted shares, reaches $1.5 trillion. Anthropic will begin trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on Thursday. The offering and first-day trading results will show whether investors remain interested in AI companies amid recent stock volatility and market upheaval, including after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s departure.

# Anthropic valued at $1.5 trillion with IPO shares priced at $83

## Valuation and trading outlook

Anthropic’s valuation has risen from $965 billion, which the company reached in May after raising $65 billion from private investors. But it fell short of $2 trillion, a valuation Anthropic had reportedly sought several months ago.

A strong first day of trading could signal that investors still expect AI to spread. The offering’s results will also set a benchmark for private AI companies, including Anthropic’s main rival, OpenAI. Its most recent valuation was $1.25 billion; the company has not announced plans for an IPO.

## Who stands to benefit from the offering

No venture investor owns more than 5% of Anthropic, but the IPO will bring substantial returns to many early backers. Sequoia and former Google CEO Eric Schmidt reportedly each own about 1% of the company. Singapore’s sovereign wealth fund GIC also controls about 1%, a source familiar with the situation said.

Other investors who could make billions include Iconiq, Menlo Ventures, Coatue, Sound Ventures and Spark Ventures. Sound Ventures was founded by Guy Oseary and actor Ashton Kutcher. Spark Ventures is the only venture firm known to have secured a seat on Anthropic’s board.

Sequoia, Iconiq, Lightspeed, Accel and Altimeter Capital Management, all of which have already invested in Anthropic, reportedly have such confidence in its prospects that they planned to buy billions of dollars’ worth of the company’s shares in the IPO.

But their stakes are smaller than those of Anthropic’s strategic partners, the company behind Claude. Amazon owns nearly 15% of Anthropic, while Google owns nearly 14%. A group of major technology companies also agreed to buy $26 billion in non-voting shares as part of a private placement alongside the IPO. The group includes AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners.

Each of Anthropic’s seven co-founders, including CEO Dario Amodei, sold nearly 3.4 million shares in the IPO. According to the S-1 filing, that brought each of them about $282 million.

Dataist’s take: The offering price above the initial range and Amazon’s and Google’s large stakes underscore the scale of interest in Anthropic. But market expectations will depend heavily on trading after the listing, which will show how willing investors are to support the company’s high valuation amid volatility in AI-related stocks.
