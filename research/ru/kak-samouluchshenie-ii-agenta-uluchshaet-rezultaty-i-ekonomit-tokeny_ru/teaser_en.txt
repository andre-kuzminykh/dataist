What if an AI agent could improve the way it works—not by changing its core model, but by redesigning the instructions, tools, memory, and workflow around it?

The authors propose a more disciplined form of self-improvement that helps an agent test and refine these surrounding components without simply memorizing the tasks it was trained on. The system limits how many changes it makes at once, explores new strategies, and removes edits that are costly, trivial, or useful only for a specific benchmark—leading to more reusable behavior and fewer tokens spent during operation.

In this review we look at how regularized self-improvement works, why unconstrained evolution can fail outside familiar tasks, and how the proposed approach builds leaner, more adaptable AI agents.
