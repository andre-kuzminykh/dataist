## A scientist who has worked with uncertain evidence

Stubbs is the Samuel C. Moncher Professor of Physics and Astronomy at Harvard. From 2018 to 2024, he was dean of the university’s Faculty of Arts and Sciences. In 1998, as part of the High-Z Supernova Search Team, he helped discover that the expansion of the universe is accelerating. The team later received the Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics for that work.

He was also the first science director of the Vera C. Rubin Observatory project. In June, the observatory began its ten-year survey of the southern sky, which includes studying the accelerated expansion of the universe.

More recently, Stubbs has written about keeping research reproducible when AI models can give different answers to the same question. He is also a member of JASON, an independent group of scientists that has advised the US government on defense and technology since 1960.

Anthropic co-founder and president Daniela Amodei pointed to Stubbs’s work on the universe’s unexpectedly rapid expansion. She said his team followed the data even when it conflicted with accepted ideas, and that the same honesty, rigor and commitment to truth should shape AI as it changes science.

## What the trust can do

Anthropic is a public-benefit corporation, a structure meant to balance commercial growth with a public mission. Its Long-Term Benefit Trust is designed to help preserve that balance over time. The trust can appoint a majority of the company’s board; its trustees also advise the board and leadership on major decisions, particularly those involving AI risks and its effects on society.

Stubbs joins trust chair Neil Buddy Shah, national security expert Richard Fontaine and former Federal Reserve chair Ben Bernanke. The trustees are independent of Anthropic’s leadership and investors.

I think the appointment’s significance is less about adding a prominent scientist than about the kind of judgment the trust says it needs: someone accustomed to following evidence when it challenges prevailing assumptions. That is a relevant credential for AI oversight, but the announcement does not say how the trust will use that expertise in practice. Its formal power over board appointments is substantial; the harder test is whether its advice can shape decisions before the company has made them.
