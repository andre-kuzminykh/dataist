# AI bots are overloading restaurant bookings in the UK

Muse has been downloaded millions of times in the US. Meta has now launched the assistant in the UK. With the user’s permission, it can access their email and book restaurant tables and make purchases on their behalf. For restaurants, however, automation brings extra work.

[FIGURE:0]

Dave Strauss, co-owner of Bouchon Racine, a French restaurant in London recently named the UK’s restaurant of the year, answers booking calls himself every day from 11 a.m. Recently, a bot with a human-sounding voice called him. When Strauss asked whether it was a robot, it said it was calling as an AI-based booking system.

Strauss argued with the bot and asked it not to call again. The conversation, he said, was unsatisfactory: the bot did not object.

Kimberly Coke is a private consultant for high-end restaurants. Celebrities pay her to help secure tables at popular venues. Coke believes that widespread use of bots could lead to serious problems. She makes bookings over WhatsApp and by phone, relying on personal relationships with restaurants.

According to Coke, there is plenty of information online about popular restaurants and places worth visiting, but getting a table is difficult. That is why clients turn to her. When a booking comes through an app, the restaurant does not know who will show up, or whether anyone will show up at all. Restaurants have an especially hard time in December, when tables are left empty.

## Why restaurants are turning away from automation

The problem is also apparent in New York, where getting a table at a hard-to-book restaurant is considered a status symbol. Popular venues include 4 Charles Prime Rib, known for its double wagyu cheeseburger, often served with a fried egg. Sources at Resy say bots tried to book tables there using multiple accounts, forcing the platform to block them.

Strauss says restaurants are constantly being pushed to automate to save money. Bouchon Racine receives many emails proposing that it hand bookings and phone calls over to AI, but this only reinforces the team’s decision to do things its own way. If Strauss could give up online bookings, he would.

Jeremy King, owner of Simpson’s in the Strand, Arlington and the Park, also plans to rely less on digital tools. He says visitors will find it easier to call if they want a good table at one of his restaurants. King is not opposed to new technology, but if it gets in the way of restaurants’ work, they will have to do without it.

## Meta’s deals and UK oversight

AI agents could become more commonplace in the UK. Meta has reached agreements with several local companies, including Marks & Spencer, Currys, Sainsbury’s and easyJet. These agreements make it easier for Muse to work with the companies’ websites and apps. If a company does not have an app integration Muse can use, the agent will use its website.

Before Muse launched, Meta discussed its responsibilities under UK law with the Information Commissioner’s Office and the Competition and Markets Authority. On Tuesday, the Information Commissioner’s Office, which oversees data protection in the UK, said the company had agreed to a voluntary monitoring and reporting scheme. The Competition and Markets Authority said the app’s terms of use had been changed after discussions to make them fairer.

Dataist’s take: Automating bookings is not just about whether an agent can find an available table. Restaurants need to know who is coming and whether the visit will happen. As Meta develops Muse and expands its access to UK companies’ websites and apps, some restaurateurs are choosing phone calls and personal interaction as a way to keep control of bookings.
