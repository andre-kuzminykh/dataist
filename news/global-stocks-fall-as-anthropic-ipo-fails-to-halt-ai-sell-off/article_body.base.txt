## Anthropic’s IPO fails to halt the sell-off in AI stocks

## Losses in Asia, Europe and the UK

Shares in major chipmakers continued to fall on Friday. Taiwan’s market lost 6.7%, hitting its lowest level since mid-April, while TSMC shares fell by about 6%.

Japan’s Nikkei fell 5%, and South Korea’s Kospi, which has a large share of technology companies, dropped 5.5%. SoftBank, an investor in technology companies, had its worst performance in more than two years: its shares fell 14.5% in Tokyo. The pressure on the company’s shares is linked to investor concerns about its large AI investments.

In Europe, the pan-European Stoxx 600 fell 1.3% in the first 90 minutes of trading. Markets in the region were heading for their worst week since March. The UK’s FTSE 100 fell 4.6% over the week, potentially marking the index’s biggest weekly drop since the start of the war in Iran.

## Anthropic’s rise does not dispel doubts

The global market sell-off followed Thursday’s decline on Wall Street, when all the main indexes lost more than 2%. Trading took place as Anthropic held its IPO: the company’s shares jumped 17% by the end of the day, and briefly rose by nearly 40% after trading began. On Friday, however, they fell 6% to $91.30, drawing closer to the IPO price of $83.

The offering did not ease concerns about AI investments. Market participants are increasingly questioning whether companies can generate returns that justify the billions of dollars invested in AI development.

According to Daniela Hathorn, senior market analyst at Capital.com, the IPO showed that interest in AI investment remains, but it was not enough to outweigh concerns about company valuations, profitability and the sustainability of investment spending.

Market sentiment, Hathorn added, has shifted from buying shares at lower prices to asking whether the correction will continue. Declines in equal-weighted indexes indicate that selling pressure has spread beyond a handful of the largest technology companies. The recent outperformance of European markets has also faded.

Dataist’s take: Strong interest in Anthropic shares was not enough to lift the sector as a whole. Investors are still weighing not only AI’s prospects but also whether companies can justify the scale of their investments. Continued declines in broad indexes show that doubts have spread beyond the largest technology companies.
