North Korea has unveiled a chatbot called Fountain of Wisdom, according to the analysis center 38 North. At an information technology exhibition, its interface resembled ChatGPT, but users are allowed to ask only questions about science and technology. A warning also prohibits political and “uncultured” queries and makes users fully responsible for the consequences of malicious use of the service.

# North Korea launches Fountain of Wisdom chatbot with a ban on political questions

## What users are allowed to ask

North Korean state television reported the chatbot’s launch. According to 38 North’s translation, an on-screen warning says users may ask only questions related to science and technology. Users are asked to avoid topics involving public and political life, as well as “uncultured” questions.

The same notice says users bear sole and full responsibility for the consequences of maliciously using the chatbot. The narrow range of permitted topics may point to concerns that the system could produce an answer that diverges from the state’s official position.

## The internet under state control

North Korea is an isolated country where the authorities exercise almost complete control over citizens’ access to information. The media are state-owned and strictly censored, while most residents are barred from accessing the global internet. Instead, they use the internal network Kwangmyong, which is cut off from the outside internet and also heavily censored. Citizens face severe penalties for violating the strict censorship laws.

38 North suggests that the state-approved chatbot is likely subject to significant censorship as well. The analysis center compares it with Chinese chatbots, which are barred from discussing topics such as the Tiananmen Square massacre. The separate ban on political queries to Fountain of Wisdom may reflect the authorities’ concern that the system could say something contrary to the official line. If the chatbot spoke negatively about the country’s leader, Kim Jong Un, the consequences could affect both the user and the engineers who built the system.

## AI and other North Korean projects

As AI becomes one of the main tools for finding information online, other authoritarian countries may also develop their own state-controlled AI services.

Fountain of Wisdom is not North Korea’s first AI-related project. According to 38 North, skilled North Korean hackers have used the technology in state-backed cybercrime and theft. AI is also believed to have helped them carry out an attack on the cryptocurrency exchange Binance. The heist could turn out to be the largest in history by the amount stolen.

Dataist’s take: The strict limits on questions suggest the North Korean chatbot is designed to operate within a controlled information environment. The source provides no details on how the system answers permitted queries or what censorship mechanisms it uses.
