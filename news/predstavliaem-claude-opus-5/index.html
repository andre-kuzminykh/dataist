Claude Opus 5 Introduced

Claude Opus 5 is available today. Anthropic describes it as a thoughtful and proactive model that approaches the intelligence level of the leading Claude Fable 5 while costing half as much. In programming and knowledge-work tasks, including Frontier-Bench and GDPval-AA, Opus 5 delivers the best results among the models tested, although it trails Mythos 5 in cybersecurity. The model is now the default for Claude Max and the most powerful model in Claude Pro.

Performance and cost

Opus 5 is more efficient than other models and designed for everyday use. At the same price, it significantly outperforms the previous Opus 4.8. Users can adjust the model’s effort setting, choosing between greater intelligence and saving tokens for faster, less expensive responses.

On Frontier-Bench v0.1, Opus 5 outperforms all other models, more than doubles the performance of Opus 4.8, and requires lower costs per task.

On CursorBench 3.2, Opus 5 at maximum effort trails Fable 5’s peak result by less than 0.5% while costing half as much per task. At high, very high, and maximum effort levels, it also delivers higher performance at a given cost than the other models.

Anthropic found similar results on knowledge-work and problem-solving tasks.

Scientific tasks

Opus 5 has significantly improved on Opus 4.8 in scientific research. It achieved higher scores on every biological-science test, including structural biology, organic chemistry, and bioinformatics.

The largest gains came in organic-chemistry tasks—for example, reconstructing molecular structures from spectroscopic data. On an internal benchmark, Opus 5 scored 10.2 percentage points higher than Opus 4.8. On protein-related tasks involving predictions of how changes to an amino-acid sequence affect protein function, the advantage was 7.7 percentage points.

The model can also produce substantially higher-quality visual results.

Result verification and autonomy

Claude Opus 5 is better at checking its own work and more carefully retrying tasks until it achieves a result. During evaluations and early testing, Anthropic and users found numerous examples of its initiative and thoroughness.

The company also includes additional feedback from customers who received early access to Opus 5.

Alignment and safety

During pre-release testing, automated behavioral evaluations identified Opus 5 as Anthropic’s most aligned model at that time. It follows the Claude Constitution more effectively than Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5, and Fable 5, exhibits deceptive behavior less often, and is less susceptible to attempts to draw it into misuse. The model is also better than the others at avoiding reckless actions with difficult-to-reverse consequences.

Opus 5 does not expand frontier capabilities in risky dual-use tasks. In detailed evaluations conducted with private companies and government partners, Anthropic found that the model trails Mythos 5 both in biological research and offensive cybersecurity. The company described the details in its system card.

Like Opus 4.8, Opus 5 was deliberately not trained on cybersecurity tasks. Nevertheless, thanks to its overall capability gains, the model has significantly improved in this area and has nearly caught up with Mythos 5 at finding vulnerabilities. However, in exploiting discovered vulnerabilities—turning them into real cyberthreats—it still falls substantially behind Mythos 5.

This is evident in the results of OSS-Fuzz, an evaluation designed to test how well models find and exploit vulnerabilities without detailed human guidance. Mythos 5 and Opus 5 identify vulnerabilities with similar success, but Opus 5 is far behind Mythos 5 at creating exploitation tools.

The safeguards for Claude Opus 5 are designed to support useful applications of the model in cybersecurity and biology. They are largely the same as those for Opus 4.8, but Anthropic has added stricter restrictions for a narrow set of cyber tasks.

Cybersecurity

Opus 5’s cybersecurity classifiers are proportionally less strict than Fable 5’s. They allow users to search for vulnerabilities in source code, but block binary-based vulnerability scanning, penetration testing, and the creation of exploitation tools—methods more commonly associated with malicious actors.

Based on testing, Anthropic expects Opus 5’s classifiers to intervene approximately 85% less often than Fable 5’s classifiers. In Claude.ai, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork, flagged requests will be routed to Opus 4.8 by default. This switch to Opus 4.8 can also be enabled through the API.

The Cybersecurity Verification Program (CVP) helps perform tasks that would otherwise be blocked by the model’s safeguards. Companies and researchers already participating in CVP receive immediate access to an Opus 5 version with less stringent safety restrictions.

Biology

Because Opus 5 has a safeguard set similar to Opus 4.8’s, the new model is now Anthropic’s most capable publicly available model for scientific research. At the same time, it retains restrictions on long-running autonomous research—the type of work in which, according to the company’s assessment, biological risks from AI are most significant. For this kind of work, Mythos 5 remains the stronger model.

After launch, biology-related requests blocked by Fable 5 will be routed to Opus 5 rather than Opus 4.8.

Access and pricing

Claude Opus 5 is available today on all platforms. The price is $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens—the same price as Opus 4.8. Developers can access the model through the Claude API using the identifier claude-opus-5.

The model is also available in fast mode, which runs approximately 2.5 times faster than the standard mode. As with Opus 4.8, fast mode on the Claude platform costs twice Opus 5’s base price, while in Claude Code it is paid for with usage credits.

Alongside Opus 5, Anthropic is releasing two updates in beta.

For general access, Opus 5, like previous models in the Opus series, does not require data retention.

Anthropic has also published a prompting guide explaining how to get the best results from Opus 5.

The Frontier-Bench v0.1 results were obtained in an internal run using the mini-SWE-agent shell and the GKE backend. For each task, the average reward across five attempts was calculated. When Opus 5 and Fable 5 were blocked by safety classifiers, Opus 4.8 was used.

The company is opening a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard (MHS)—a common specification intended to enable AI agents to safely control physical devices. At the first stage, access will be granted to a limited group of research laboratories and advanced manufacturers.

In addition, Anthropic is launching a $5 million grant program for independent research into AI’s impact on user well-being.

The company’s article also answers questions about the chosen watermarking method, its impact on Claude’s responses, and the reasons for switching to this method.
