Chinese developers Z.AI and Moonshot AI are postponing the release of their next flagship models and looking for new sources of training data. According to Reuters, Z.AI will likely move the release of GLM-5.5, a model with around 3 trillion parameters, from November to the first quarter of 2027. Moonshot AI, facing a shortage of overseas computing capacity and restrictions on data collection, may delay Kimi K4 until mid-2027. The company is now using Google’s and Meta’s latest models for training.

# Chinese AI developers seek new data and computing capacity

## New timelines and interim models

The delays are tied to restrictions on Nvidia chip supplies and access to computing resources, as well as US companies’ efforts to crack down on model distillation. After the US closed a loophole that allowed Nvidia chips to be obtained through cloud services, Nvidia halted shipments to Southeast Asia.

As Z.AI prepares GLM-5.5, the company released GLM-5.4 on Monday, with 750 billion parameters. The developers say its new architecture enables the model to compete with systems containing between 2 trillion and 3 trillion parameters.

Without access to overseas computing capacity, Moonshot AI’s development cycle could double from six to 12 months. This will likely push the release of the company’s next major model, Kimi K4, to mid-2027.

## Seeking data and computing resources

After OpenAI and Anthropic cut off access to data collection for distillation, Moonshot AI turned to Google’s and Meta’s latest models as sources of training data. According to Reuters sources, the company is also stepping up its search for computing capacity in Europe.

Dataist’s take: The restrictions affect two resources Chinese developers need at once: training data and computing capacity. Moonshot AI is seeking alternatives to both, while Z.AI releases an interim model as its next flagship is delayed.
