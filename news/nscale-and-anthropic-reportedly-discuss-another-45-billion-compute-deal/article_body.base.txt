## A huge commitment, with the economics still unsettled

TechCrunch said it had asked Nscale and Anthropic for comment and would update its story if it received a response. The reported deal is not confirmed in the source account, and its $45 billion figure sits against a broader investor concern: that companies are spending too much on computing power for too little profit.

Anthropic is expected to hold an initial public offering this year. Nscale is also planning to go public, but Bloomberg reported today that investors pushed back against the company’s proposed $30 billion valuation.

[INFO:facts] $45 billion :: reported deal | $30 billion :: Nscale valuation

The contrast matters. A reported commitment of $45 billion in computing capacity is larger than the valuation investors resisted for Nscale. That does not make the two figures directly comparable, but it makes the deal’s scale hard to ignore.

## The unanswered part is the return

I think the most important missing detail is not how much capacity the companies might secure, but what the agreement requires them to pay and when. The report gives no terms, so there is no way to tell how much risk sits with Anthropic, with Nscale, or across the life of the deal.

Anthropic is making these commitments on the expectation that demand and returns will catch up with spending. With a possible IPO this year, that expectation will be harder to keep abstract: public investors will want to see how infrastructure commitments translate into economics. Nscale faces its own test, after investors resisted its proposed valuation. For both companies, more compute may support growth—but it also raises the price of proving that growth is worth funding.
