At Labour’s party conference, several senior figures were worrying less about killer robots than about who might control powerful AI—and whether Britain is moving fast enough to manage the risks. In the days that followed, AI dominated a cabinet meeting, the head of MI5 warned about its use in cyberattacks, and ministers discussed tougher cyber-security legislation. The shift matters because Labour is now debating two risks at once: that AI could enable serious harm, and that excessive caution could leave Britain behind.
