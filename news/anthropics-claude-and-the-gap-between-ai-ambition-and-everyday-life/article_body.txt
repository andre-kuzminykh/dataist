## The joke behind the claim

The researcher also described feeling like an outsider at times among his colleagues. He allowed that Anthropic could be called a cult, while stressing that this was an extreme assessment.

The company openly entertains the possibility that AI models may already be conscious. Some competitors consider that position marginal. Taken together, these details suggest a workplace where questions about machine minds can feel more immediate than the everyday ways people might actually use them.

That gap is not unique to Anthropic. Meta introduced its Muse assistant and brought AI agents to a broad audience, promising they could book flights and buy groceries. But the company did not explain how much people want those tasks automated.

## The ordinary-use test

Elon Musk was mocked not long ago for saying chess was uninteresting to an intellectual like him because it would one day be “fully solved.” The parallel is not that humor and chess are the same problem. It is that both claims can mistake a difficult intellectual challenge for a problem people urgently need solved.

I think the more interesting question is not whether Claude can produce a joke, but whether the people building these systems understand which parts of ordinary life people actually want to hand over. An announcement about capability does not answer that.

The distinction matters as AI agents move toward tasks such as shopping and travel. If companies focus on what their systems can automate rather than what people want automated, technical ambition may keep outrunning everyday usefulness.
