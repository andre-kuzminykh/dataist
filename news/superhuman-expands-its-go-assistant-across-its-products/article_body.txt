# Superhuman expands AI assistant Go to email, documents and third-party services

## Personalized suggestions and email

After rebranding last year, Superhuman began investing more heavily in Go. The assistant will now be able to draw on information from different apps and offer personalized prompt suggestions. When users first launch the updated setup flow, they can connect the services they need and preload context about their work.

Go works directly in the input field, like Grammarly, and helps manage other agents. The assistant can also act on its own: check schedules, monitor email and prepare replies. The updated Go also takes a user's writing style into account and adapts messages in email and Docs to match it.

## Go in Docs and Superhuman Mail

Superhuman is adding AI features to several products, including Docs and Superhuman Mail. In Docs, Go agents work directly in the app and can handle different tasks in the background. Users will also be able to configure workflows that run when specific events occur.

In Superhuman Mail, Go replaces the previous central AI tool. The assistant can search company knowledge bases and connected services, use that context to draft messages, and organize incoming email in Google and Outlook accounts.

In September, Superhuman acquired note-taking service Fathom to expand the context available to its products. The company is now introducing collaborative, real-time note-taking with shared access for teams. After a meeting, Go will be able to make decisions and, for example, prepare a first draft of a proposal.

## Users and competitors

CEO Shishir Mehrotra says Go has more than 1 million active users. A range of agents are competing for users' attention, for both personal and work tasks. Mehrotra says Go is more work-focused, but can also help with personal email and scheduling. In a conversation with TechCrunch, he attributed the popularity of these assistants to their ability to take the whole person into account and cover both work and personal tasks.

Go may not compete directly with Instinct, Muse and Fo, but Superhuman expects users to choose it over products such as OpenAI's Dots or Claude Duet.

Dataist's take: Superhuman is expanding Go from a writing assistant into an interface that brings together data from email, documents and connected services. The reported 1 million active users indicate the scale of its usage, but on their own do not show how often people will entrust Go with personal tasks.
