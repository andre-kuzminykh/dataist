OpenAI has opened access to Daybreak, a cyber defense tool built on the company’s models, to NATO and several European governments. Specialists can use it to check software, find vulnerabilities and address security gaps. NATO’s Communications and Information Agency is already actively using these models. At the Crossed Swords 2026 exercise, participants used AI agents for cyber operations under human supervision for the first time.

## Where the models are already being used

The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity, ENISA, used OpenAI models to find four vulnerabilities in software used by EU institutions. A joint report by ENISA and CERT-EU reviewed 27 software projects: 95% of the 88 reported issues turned out to be real flaws.

CERT Polska found six vulnerabilities in MikroTik routers that attackers were already actively exploiting. Ukraine is using Daybreak to protect civilian infrastructure. France, Germany and Poland have also received access to the tool.

Dylan Brown, head of NATO’s Communications and Information Agency, called the use of models a new stage in cyber defense. Dataist’s take: the ENISA and CERT Polska examples show that models are being used to find specific vulnerabilities, including ones already exploited by attackers. At the Crossed Swords 2026 exercise, however, AI agents operated under human supervision; the report does not specify how autonomous their actions were.
