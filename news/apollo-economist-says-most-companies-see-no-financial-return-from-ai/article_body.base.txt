## The hard part is converting intelligence into returns

Slok says AI may work well as a calculator, but business intelligence is largely the ability to make decisions. Those decisions depend on factors that models do not understand, including:

- Responsibility and trust
- Corporate culture and unwritten rules
- Ethical trade-offs and fairness

These considerations can shape reputational risk, which ultimately affects revenue growth and profit margins. Lower-cost AI, in other words, does not automatically translate into better business results.

Recent research also suggests AI has not yet produced a significant increase in productivity.

## The missing link

I think the more important question is not whether AI can perform useful tasks, but whether companies can change how they make decisions around it. Slok’s argument points to a gap between cheaper cognitive tools and the human, organizational judgments that influence financial outcomes.

That leaves a difficult test for AI adoption: if productivity gains remain limited and responsibility-sensitive decisions still depend on context, falling costs alone may not be enough to lift profits.
