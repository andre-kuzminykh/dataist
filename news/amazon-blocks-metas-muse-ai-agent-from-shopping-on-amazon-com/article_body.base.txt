## What Amazon blocked

As GeekWire reported, Amazon said Muse could no longer access the site because its use was unauthorized. Muse is therefore unable to complete purchases on Amazon.

Amazon is not required by law to open its website to Muse. It also has its own foundation models and one of the most popular inference platforms on the internet, so the company may see little reason to give an outside agent a privileged route into its commerce system.

## Why Amazon can wait

The practical risk is straightforward: if Muse places an order incorrectly, Amazon may have to deal with both an unhappy customer and an unhappy seller.

Muse has one of the lowest hallucination rates among AI models, but that rate is still not zero. For an agent handling purchases, a small error is not merely a bad answer. It can become the wrong product, the wrong quantity or a transaction that someone must unwind.

Even if Amazon sees a future for AI-agent shopping, it may prefer to wait through several more update cycles before introducing those capabilities.

## The control point is the platform

I think the more important detail is not Muse’s model quality but Amazon’s ability to decide who gets to act on its marketplace. An agent can be capable enough to navigate a site and still have no right to transact there.

Amazon’s message is also quiet about whether the company plans to support agent shopping on its own terms. That absence leaves outside agents in a weak position: they must prove reliability to the platform whose rules determine whether they can operate at all.

The next stage of agent commerce may therefore be constrained less by whether agents can shop than by whether marketplaces are willing to let them.
