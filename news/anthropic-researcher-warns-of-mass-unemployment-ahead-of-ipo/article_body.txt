## Anthropic researcher predicts mass unemployment ahead of the company’s IPO

Lin described how he thinks events could unfold: first, people will lose their current jobs and move into other professions for a short time, then lose the ability to find work there as well. In that scenario, the researcher believes, they will have to rely on government financial support. He also said AI will soon become more capable than any person and able to beat anyone in a one-on-one contest.

## What is known about AI’s impact on employment

There is little evidence so far of an impending mass disappearance of jobs, the article’s author notes. It is difficult to separate the slowdown in hiring from the broader economic downturn that followed the pandemic. Moreover, even leading AI models still perform poorly on work tasks, in the publication’s assessment: their nonsensical answers create extra work instead of reducing it.

## Why the prediction came ahead of the IPO

Lin’s remarks came shortly before Anthropic’s expected initial public offering, which is projected to become the largest in history. The company’s offering documents do not disclose the stakes held by individual employees or divisions. However, Anthropic employees, including Lin, receive compensation in shares that convert into common stock. As Reuters put it in the headline of one article, this could “create thousands of millionaires.”

Lin therefore has a personal financial interest ahead of the offering in having investors view Anthropic’s main product as more powerful: higher expectations for AI could mean stronger demand and, in turn, a rise in the company’s share price after it goes public.

Bloomberg columnist Parmy Olson previously called this approach a paradoxical marketing strategy. In her view, fearmongering has worked especially well for companies and industries seen as vulnerable to change: fear has proved an effective way to sell.

Dataist’s take: Lin’s prediction should also be considered in the context of the upcoming stock offering, since Anthropic employees could benefit financially from an increase in the company’s value. But the information cited in the article does not prove that the IPO prompted his statements. The prediction is limited by the lack of convincing evidence that AI is already causing mass job losses.
