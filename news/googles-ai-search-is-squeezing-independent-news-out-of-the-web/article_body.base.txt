## Google moves the audience

In 2024, Google began placing AI Overviews at the top of its search results. That pushed established news sites lower as the search engine prioritized interaction with a new technical format.

Google controls almost all web search, so this is not a minor change in referral traffic. It changes the route by which readers encounter reporting. Meta launched its own AI assistant in the same year, further moving social-media users away from news sites and toward chatbot summaries, which can contain serious factual errors.

Direct traffic to the 100 largest media sites has fallen by 25% in two years. Meanwhile, 40% of desktop visitors no longer scroll through web pages. For publishers funded by ads or subscriptions, that behavior cuts into the part of the business that turns attention into revenue.

## AI clicks are not replacing search traffic

Traffic from AI chatbots is growing, partly through disputed agreements between commercial chatbot developers and traditional media companies. But the scale remains small: referrals from AI chatbots number only in the hundreds of thousands, compared with tens of millions of visits previously sent by ordinary search engines.

That gap is the central economic fact. AI referrals can be increasing and still fail to compensate for the collapse of conventional search traffic. A rising percentage is not meaningful if the underlying volume is shrinking much faster.

I think the more important change is not that chatbots sometimes get facts wrong. It is that they remove the visit itself—the page view, subscription opportunity and advertising impression—while still drawing on the work that made the answer possible.

## The replacement market

As independent news sites close for lack of audience, AI-focused entrepreneurs are buying the assets they leave behind. Last week, Futurism reported on a global content-farm network organized by Brown Brothers Media. The company acquires closed media properties and turns them into platforms for producing large volumes of AI-generated material.

Brown Brothers Media says the model generates tens of millions of views. It has also created numerous fictional author profiles, making it difficult for ordinary readers to distinguish reported fact from material fabricated by AI.

The announcement is quiet about the part that matters most to journalism: who is responsible when the system produces false information, and what remains for publishers whose original reporting is summarized elsewhere. The traffic numbers suggest that the market is not simply shifting from one distribution channel to another; it is weakening the economic base that supports the reporting in the first place.

Content farms offer a plausible direction for that market. Click economics can reward writing optimized for search algorithms even when the material is low-quality and AI-generated, while professional journalism loses ground worldwide. The web may end up with more pages competing for attention and fewer organizations able to establish what deserves it.
