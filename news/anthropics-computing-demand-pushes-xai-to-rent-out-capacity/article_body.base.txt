Anthropic has signed a deal to rent computing capacity from xAI for about $3.1 billion a month, becoming Elon Musk’s company’s largest customer. The New York Times reports that xAI has been renting out capacity to competitors since May, and that revenue growth became even more important after the company went public in June. Google pays xAI $800 million. Either contract can be terminated with 90 days’ notice. Renting out capacity now accounts for more than half of xAI’s revenue, and the company is reshaping its own projects to fulfill customer orders.

# Anthropic becomes xAI’s largest customer for rented computing capacity

## Computing matters more than proprietary models

According to sources cited by the publication, xAI was supposed to finish building new data centers by October to meet Anthropic’s orders. Elon Musk has also said that Claude’s creator wants to rent even more capacity.

In August, Musk urged engineers in a group chat to increase “revenue per GPU.” The publication reports that he was also frustrated that xAI’s own models could barely compete with rivals. Musk reportedly renamed the internal chat “Sell computing capacity because we can’t compete.”

## xAI winds down some AI projects

The company is cutting back or restructuring its own development work to adapt to its new priority. In November, division head Mike Nichols told employees that the 60-person team working on Grok Imagine, an image and video generator, would be disbanded. A company memo described the decision as driven by business-critical work.

In September, xAI shut down its AI avatars. OpenAI also discontinued work on its Sora video model because of computing capacity constraints.

Dataist’s take: Renting out computing capacity already brings xAI more than half its revenue, but growth depends on large customers and contracts that can be terminated with 90 days’ notice. The project changes show that the company is prioritizing this demand while its own models struggle to compete with rivals.
