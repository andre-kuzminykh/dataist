## Two bots, one candidate

Zip first drew attention last month with a video in which he fed convincing-sounding nonsense to a virtual AI interviewer. The bot lost track of the conversation, scolded him and changed its expression to something unsettling.

This time, the satirical blogger brought Victor to an interview with an employer’s chatbot. Victor introduced himself as a partner in the conversation and praised Zip as a marketing star suited to a marketing manager role. The company bot responded that working with Victor was “incredibly impressive and easy.”

Zip had to correct his assistant twice: he was a marketing coordinator, not a manager with direct reports, and Victor was being “too nice.” After the bots repeatedly interrupted each other, Zip pulled Victor aside and told him that while he liked the flattering presentation, the assistant was going too far. Victor could embellish his experience by about ten percent, Zip said, but not invent things that had never happened.

Then Victor volunteered that Zip would accept $60,000. Zip objected, and later briefly left the call, leaving the two bots to negotiate without him. When he returned, the employer’s chatbot said it was prepared to discuss a range of $85,000 to $95,000.

## The joke points both ways

The setup extends the old image of young applicants bringing their parents to interviews: now the chaperone is an AI assistant that answers on the candidate’s behalf. I think the sharper point is not that these bots make a mess of a hiring call. It is that employers and applicants are being pushed toward a contest between systems that imitate human interaction, while the person whose job is at stake becomes the least active participant.

The video also turns the hiring process back on employers. Using AI for interviews or initial screening can signal that finding the right person is not worth much human attention. But Zip’s prank complicates the implied answer that applicants should respond in kind: a flattering assistant can misstate credentials, invent experience and bargain away salary.

What the announcement leaves unanswered is how much of this is already routine outside a comedy video. The more hiring is mediated by bots, the more candidates may feel they need a bot of their own — and the less anyone in the conversation is actually speaking for themselves.
