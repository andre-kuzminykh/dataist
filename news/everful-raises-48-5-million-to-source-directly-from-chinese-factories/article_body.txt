# Former Alibaba executive’s Everful raises $48.5 million to source directly from Chinese factories

## How Everful handles sourcing

The round was led by Vertex Ventures Southeast Asia & India and January Capital. Existing investors Square Peg and Lightspeed also participated.

Cici Song, who previously worked at Alibaba, and entrepreneur Kevin Han founded Everful in 2024. Before that, Han sold one of his companies to Amazon. Everful started as a wholesale jewelry business and later became a platform that helps businesses source products across categories directly from manufacturers.

Small retailers and brands often work through intermediaries, face high minimum order quantities and wait a long time for deliveries. They also have to invest in inventory before they know which products will be in demand. Song saw a gap between fast-changing consumer trends and traditional wholesale while working as an investor at Bessemer Venture Partners and researching cross-border sourcing through meetings with industry startups. That observation shaped Everful’s goal: to make direct factory sourcing easier with an AI-powered platform.

Competitors include Alibaba, which connects international buyers with manufacturers and suppliers, and San Francisco-based wholesale platform Faire, where retailers buy products directly from brands. Everful’s co-founders say the company’s difference is that it handles the entire sourcing process rather than just facilitating a transaction.

Everful is designed for small shops and brands without their own sourcing teams. The company also vets its factory partners. Other players offer quality checks, logistics and AI-assisted sourcing, but Everful aims to combine these services into a single process so retailers have less work to do themselves.

The platform uses AI agents to automate labor-intensive tasks. They track consumer trends, find suppliers, coordinate orders across factories and manage logistics.

## Pricing and expansion plans

Because Everful sources products in China, trade disputes between the US and China and tariffs directly affect its business. Song said the co-founders had considered geopolitical risks from the outset. Her experience at Alibaba and Bessemer Venture Partners influenced the company’s strategy.

As an example of changing trade policy, Song cited the Trump administration’s suspension of the de minimis rule, which had allowed eligible shipments worth no more than $800 to enter the US duty-free. In Song’s view, the changes could support a model in which sourcing happens abroad and sales take place locally. She said Everful helps local retailers and brands, and that a long-term cross-border trade model would combine international sourcing for businesses with local sales.

Everful will use its Series A funding to expand in the US and Europe, especially the UK, France and Italy. The company also plans to invest in AI to automate custom product configuration and other sourcing processes. It will also deepen its integration with e-commerce platforms, building on its existing Shopify integration. Over time, Everful wants to become an end-to-end infrastructure platform for global sourcing.

The company has about 70 full-time employees worldwide, including 45 in research and development. Everful has also built an AI operations team that connects its engineering and business units.

Dataist’s take: Everful is trying to compete with large marketplaces by handling the entire journey from finding a factory to delivering the goods. That model depends on international trade conditions: the company itself says tariffs and changes to import rules directly affect its business.
