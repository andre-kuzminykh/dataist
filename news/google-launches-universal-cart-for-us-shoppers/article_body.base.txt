## A cart that follows the shopping process

Universal Cart is designed to keep potential purchases together, even when they come from different retailers. Google says it can notify users when a saved item gets cheaper or returns to stock.

The tool can also factor in credit-card benefits when suggesting a payment method and show relevant promo codes. Checkout runs through Universal Commerce Protocol, which Google introduced earlier this year to help AI agents and merchants interact across stages of a purchase.

## Google is building beyond search

The cart joins Google’s other AI shopping features: conversational product search through AI Mode in Search, and Let Google Call, which has an AI agent contact nearby stores to check whether an item is available.

Amazon, OpenAI and their competitors are also adding agentic-commerce features, pushing chatbots beyond answering questions and toward helping with transactions.

## The missing measure is completed purchases

I think the interesting question is not how many products Universal Cart can collect, but how often it gets a shopper from saved item to completed order. The announcement describes price alerts, stock notifications and checkout infrastructure, but gives no results showing whether shoppers use those features or buy more as a result.

That gap matters because a shared cart is only useful if it reduces friction across the retailers involved. Google is laying out the connective tissue; the announcement leaves the actual shopping outcome unmeasured.
