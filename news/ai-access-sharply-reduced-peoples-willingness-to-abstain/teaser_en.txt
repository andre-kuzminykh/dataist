AI access nearly erased people’s willingness to say “I don’t know,” a new study finds. Across experiments involving questions about movies, participants with AI available answered more questions but got far fewer right than those without it. The models usually handled the easier questions and sometimes failed on harder ones; their advice was mostly wrong, so the result cannot be explained as people sensibly handing work to a reliable tool. The finding points to a risk beyond accuracy: confident AI answers may crowd out people’s own uncertainty.
