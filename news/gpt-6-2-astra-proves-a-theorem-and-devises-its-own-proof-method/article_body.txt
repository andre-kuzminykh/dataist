## GPT-6.2 Astra proves a mathematical theorem and develops a new method

The Smoktunowicz theorem describes how a mathematical system grows when its basic elements are multiplied: growth is either slow or fast, with no in-between. Instead of following the approach used in published proofs, Astra used “capacity,” a measure from existing research that estimates the minimum number of elements added at each computational step.

## The Autoform-Hard test

Autoform-Hard is part of Meta FAIR’s AutoformBench, assembled by Vals. It contains 56 problems, five of which were corrected. In Meta’s study, ten models failed to solve these problems within the allotted budget.

Astra tried to stop after 47 minutes, having spent $8, but the system rejected the attempt to finish: unsolved attempts could not be submitted until spending reached $20. The model then spent nine minutes without entering code as it worked out a different strategy. At the 94-minute mark, it produced a proof 1,800 lines long; total spending was $21.58.

- <strong>GPT-6.2 Astra</strong> — 49 problems solved.
- <strong>GPT-6.2 Sol</strong> — 48 problems.
- <strong>Claude Fable 5.2</strong> — 48 problems.

Dataist’s take: the result shows that the model can not only write a verifiable proof but also choose a method different from the one published. However, Astra was tested on a specific set of 56 problems, and its success on that test alone says nothing about its performance beyond it.
