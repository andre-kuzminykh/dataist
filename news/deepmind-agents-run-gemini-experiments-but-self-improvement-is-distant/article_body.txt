# DeepMind AI agents run some experiments for new Gemini models

## One algorithm, changing models

Kavukcuoglu described DeepMind’s change in approach as a strategic shift. Previously, the lab formed separate teams for specialised projects, including AlphaFold and fusion research. Now, he said, both scientific work and products rely on one general-purpose algorithm.

Each generation of Gemini is developed manually as a separate system, rather than assembled on an unchanged architecture. Meanwhile, parallel teams are working on the model’s next generations.

## How AI agents run experiments

The agents manage some computing resources themselves and launch experiments. But this approach does not yet amount to recursive self-improvement: in Kavukcuoglu’s assessment, that capability is still a long way off.

For safety, DeepMind does not reward agents for their internal chains of reasoning during training. This keeps their intentions visible and available for review in natural language before they take action.

## The limits of measuring intelligence

Today’s AI can visually depict physical processes with precision, such as fluid motion and collisions. But, Kavukcuoglu noted, models do not yet understand these processes accurately.

He believes there is no test that could show when models will bridge this gap and achieve artificial general intelligence (AGI): no benchmark captures the essence of intelligence.

In Kavukcuoglu’s view, the transition to AGI will begin when useful everyday products earn enough trust for people to delegate decisions to them, just as they would to other people.

Dataist’s take: DeepMind is already handing AI agents some of the work involved in running experiments, but the company draws a clear line between this kind of automation and recursive self-improvement. Another limitation is the lack of a test that could determine how close models are to artificial general intelligence.
