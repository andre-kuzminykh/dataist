# Airbnb to launch an AI trip-planning agent in 2027

## Why Airbnb is building a dedicated agent

Airbnb CEO Brian Chesky has previously said that ordinary chatbots are poorly suited to planning trips. In his view, people want not only to get a ready-made option but also to explore destinations, compare accommodations and discuss their choices with travel companions. In an interview with TechCrunch last month, Chesky said an AI agent should not take away travelers’ inspiration or the pleasure of searching.

On the company’s third-quarter earnings call, he repeated that point. Chesky cited data showing that 80% of travelers enjoy spending time exploring trips. He also compared trip planning with simple routine tasks, such as booking a table or scheduling a meeting: agents like Muse and Instinct are suited to those tasks, he said, while travel calls for a different approach. Chesky believes general-purpose agents are not ideal for the job.

## The interface will adapt to each request

Airbnb describes the product as a personalized interface built specifically for travel. Instead of responding with text alone, the agent will assemble a view suited to the request from a library of hundreds of components: maps, photos, comparisons, calendars and itineraries. It will combine them in real time based on what the user is looking for.

Chesky called the approach “similar to a generative interface”: the system should offer the right format at the right moment, based on the traveler’s specific task.

In developing the product, the company is drawing on nearly 20 years of experience with interactions between hosts and guests. Airbnb says the agent will take into account how people book stays and host guests on the platform.

## Help will continue after booking

The agent will be able to assist travelers throughout their trip. Airbnb listed tasks including changing a booking, resolving payment problems, helping with a refund or rebooking, and providing round-the-clock support with safety issues. The company says an external agent would struggle to carry out these actions independently.

Over time, Airbnb plans to extend the agent’s help to other parts of a trip. In addition to homes and hotels, it is expected to work with services and experiences—areas the company has focused on more since last year’s redesign.

Airbnb previously said its AI agent would arrive within the following year. The company has now given a more specific launch window: the first half of 2027.

## Revenue growth

The announcement came alongside strong third-quarter results. Airbnb revenue rose 19% year over year to $4.87 billion, exceeding Wall Street analysts’ expectations. The company also raised its revenue growth forecast for 2026: it now expects growth closer to the high end of the “teens” range rather than the midpoint.

Chesky called the quarter Airbnb’s fastest revenue growth in more than three years and told analysts the company was accelerating growth again.

Dataist’s take: Airbnb is building an agent around what its platform can do. It is meant not only to make recommendations but also to help with bookings, payments and support. That sets it apart from general-purpose assistants, but the scope of its promised capabilities remains to be tested after launch.
