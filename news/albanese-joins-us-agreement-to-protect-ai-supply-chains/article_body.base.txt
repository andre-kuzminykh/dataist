Australia has joined a US agreement aimed at protecting the technologies and critical infrastructure that underpin AI supply chains. The move was announced ahead of the Quad leaders’ summit in Manila, Donald Trump’s first since becoming president. On Tuesday, Anthony Albanese will meet the US president. Critical minerals, maritime security and energy security are among the summit’s topics. Canberra is seeking to keep Washington engaged in the Indo-Pacific without worsening relations with China.

# Australia backs US agreement to protect AI supply chains

## Agreement on technology and infrastructure

The Quad summit is taking place in Manila, the capital of the Philippines. Trump is attending a leaders’ meeting of the grouping for the first time. After a heavy defeat in the US midterm elections, he is also expected to take part in talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

The Quad is meeting for the first time since 2024. The Australian government is trying not to provoke China while joining an agreement with the US, Japan and other countries to protect key AI technologies.

The San Francisco Declaration is part of the international Pax Silica agreement. It is intended to strengthen supply chains for chips and components made from critical minerals, and to foster cooperation as China seeks control over certain areas of AI development. Government representatives say the agreement should help the US and its partners set standards in AI rather than cede leadership to others.

Participants commit to protecting sensitive technologies needed for AI supply chains. These include goods, software, materials, knowledge, components, the raw materials used to produce them, and production equipment.

The declaration also covers critical infrastructure: ports, terminals, transport, communications and logistics networks, as well as information and communications technology infrastructure and the energy systems on which they depend. The list also includes digital platforms used to coordinate and manage these systems.

Along with Australia, the US and Japan, the agreement was signed by the UK, India, Israel, South Korea and Singapore.

## Australia’s task: keeping the US engaged

Speaking at the ASEAN-Australia summit on Monday, Albanese said the region needs friendship, mutual trust and optimism to prosper. Cooperation, he said, would help countries address shared challenges at home and across the region amid global instability.

Federal government sources told Guardian Australia that Albanese’s attendance at the APEC leaders’ meeting in Shenzhen at the end of the week is part of an effort to maintain contact with Trump during the final two years of his presidency and prevent the US from retreating from global leadership.

Premesha Saha, a senior fellow at Asia Society, expects the Quad talks to cover critical minerals, maritime security and energy. In her view, US engagement in the Indo-Pacific is a key strategic interest for Australia, and the Quad remains one of the few forums that connects the US president to the regional agenda alongside three close partners.

Saha said Trump’s attendance at a regional Quad meeting matters to Australia after a year in which Washington focused elsewhere and established its own channel of communication with Beijing. She said Albanese’s goals include keeping the US engaged and developing a Quad that can deliver results even as US policy and interests change.

Saha said Beijing would likely criticize the meeting and portray it as bloc politics, but cautioned against overstating how much the summit would anger China. The Quad is not a military alliance, and Washington has repeatedly stressed that the grouping is not directed against China.

Jennifer Parker, a nonresident fellow at the Lowy Institute, called criticism of Albanese’s participation in the annual run of international summits short-sighted. She said Australia’s ties with countries in the region are necessary for security, which underpins national prosperity. Australia, she added, needs to maintain an international profile and stand up for its interests.

Parker disagreed, however, that Australia has to keep the US in the Asia-Pacific: Washington has enduring economic and strategic interests there. Still, she said, it can sometimes be useful for Australia to remind the US of that.

## Digital economy and bilateral meetings

On Monday, Albanese announced a new ASEAN-Australia digital economy program. It is intended to support AI safety, AI standards and digital trade. Albanese said people should remain in control of AI and shape the technology rather than let it shape them.

The Australian prime minister also held a bilateral meeting with Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.

In a conversation with Lao Foreign Minister Saleumxay Kommasith, Albanese again expressed serious concern about the deaths of Australian tourists Bianca Jones and Holly Morton-Bowles. They died in 2024 after drinking methanol-laced alcohol at a hotel in Vientiane.

Australia is concerned about the seriousness of the allegations in the case. Investigators said they could not make further progress because of a lack of evidence: no autopsies were conducted. Albanese called what happened a tragedy and stressed that the case remains a serious concern for Australia.

Kommasith said the Lao government attaches great importance to the case and is working to bring those responsible to justice.

Dataist’s take: The agreement formalizes Australia’s cooperation with the US and other partners to protect technologies and infrastructure critical to AI supply chains. The Quad meeting itself does not ease tensions over China: participants emphasize that the grouping is not a military alliance and is not directed against Beijing.
