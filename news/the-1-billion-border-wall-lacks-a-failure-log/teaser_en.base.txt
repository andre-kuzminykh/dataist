The “virtual wall” along the U.S.-Mexico border is expanding without a reliable way to show whether it saves lives or misses people in danger. A year-long investigation by MIT Technology Review and the Times of San Diego found that more than 1,050 people have died near surveillance towers, though the true number is likely higher. By 2034, the U.S. plans to spend $1 billion to triple the system’s size. The immediate need is not another tower. It is a way to determine what the existing network sees, records and fails to do.
