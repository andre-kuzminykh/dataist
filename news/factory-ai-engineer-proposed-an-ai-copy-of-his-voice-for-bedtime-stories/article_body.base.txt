## A voice, not a bedtime story

In a since-deleted post on X, Factory AI engineer Sng said his wife had asked him to read a book to their newborn. He suggested playing an audiobook on his phone. When she pointed out that the voice would not be his, he replied that he could train a voice model. He added that parenting feels different when you work in AI.

That distinction is the point. An audiobook can deliver the words; a synthetic copy of a parent’s voice imitates one part of the relationship. Sng’s post did not explain whether he intended to use such a model, or how often. But the proposal itself raises a question about what technology is being asked to replace during a child’s first months.

The episode recalls Sam Altman’s comments about relying on ChatGPT to help raise his son, and his suggestion that an AI podcast could replace the usual conversation with a child on the way home from school. Those remarks drew sharp criticism. Sng’s idea pushes the same instinct into an even more personal setting.

## The handoff keeps widening

AI companies are promoting tools that take on more of the small tasks that fill daily life. Meta is pushing its Muse AI agent, which can send messages and emails on a user’s behalf. Other proposed uses include planning days and vacations, making bookings and reservations, reading, and even friendship when human friends are unavailable or disagree.

Parents are already using AI in more direct ways, too:

- Some let their children talk at length with ChatGPT’s voice mode.
- One mother made an AI copy of herself so her son could talk with it while she travelled for work.
- Meta is developing StoryKit, an app that creates personalized stories for children and reads them aloud.

[INFO:chips] messages | planning | reading | friendship

These examples are not all equivalent. A tool that drafts an email does not stand in for care in the way a voice meant to soothe a baby might. My concern is that the convenience framing makes that difference easy to miss: saving time is not always the same as improving the task.

## What the proposal leaves out

Users responded to Sng’s post with disbelief. One asked whether a father’s first impulse should be to read to his child himself and spend more time with them. Another called it one of the saddest things they had read on social media.

Sociologist and author Jess Calarco said it was telling when men preferred to invent technology to replace childcare rather than share parenting responsibilities equally. That criticism shifts the focus from whether a voice model can perform the task to who is expected to do it in the first place.

Sng had posted a hospital photo with his wife and newborn before the controversy, captioned: “After creating so many agents, it’s time to create a human.” The joke now sits beside a harder tension: the more AI can imitate the signals of care, the more important it becomes to distinguish imitation from time spent caring.
