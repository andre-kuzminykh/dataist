## Why Israel is watching the leadership change

Israel is home to research teams from Intel, Microsoft and many local startups. But Nvidia has particular significance because of its leading role in AI: the company’s GPUs are widely used to train and run large language models. A leadership crisis could therefore affect both Nvidia’s plans and expectations across Israel’s tech sector.

In 2019, Nvidia acquired Mellanox for $6.9 billion and made it the foundation of its largest research center outside the US. The company also planned to build a campus in northern Israel costing several billion dollars. Israeli cybersecurity entrepreneur Pavel Gurvich told TechCrunch that the Mellanox acquisition brought Nvidia to the country and fostered positive sentiment toward the company and Huang in the local tech community.

Nvidia employs 6,000 people in Israel. The central question now is whether the company will remain as committed to the country under a new leader. Both those hoping Nvidia will stay the course and those who would prefer a change are watching to see who succeeds Huang.

In 2023, Nvidia’s interim chief Colette Kress said in an interview now circulating again on X that, as CFO, she would usually start her day with meetings about Israel before turning to business in California.

Two Israelis are among the possible candidates for the permanent role: Apple’s Johny Srouji and Nvidia’s Michael Kagan. But the list is preliminary, and neither is considered a leading contender. Kress may not remain at the helm for long either.

## What a new CEO could mean for competitors

Whoever takes the job, Huang’s successor will probably have less influence within Nvidia and across the tech industry. Investors, however, do not expect the leadership change alone to reduce demand for the company’s products.

Yanay Oren, general partner at Vertex Ventures Israel, says slower decision-making at Nvidia could create an opening for competitors. But if uncertainty also slows AI spending, competitors could win a larger share of a market that is itself shrinking.

Under Huang, Nvidia invested heavily across the AI industry, backing companies and developing new generations of its own chips. The company accounted for a substantial share of global AI spending. Huang’s departure does not by itself undermine Nvidia’s technology or ecosystem, but it could give other players more room to shape the market’s direction, says Adi Levanon, founder and managing partner of Selah Ventures.

Companies around the world are bringing increasingly demanding AI agents into their workflows. Against that backdrop, interest and venture funding are growing for developers of AI-specific chips and inference software that can compete with Nvidia. Levanon says these companies now have time to show that their approach is more cost-effective, but a change in Nvidia’s leadership alone will not be enough to attract customers.

By Dataist’s assessment, a change at the top could weaken Nvidia’s influence over industry decisions, but it does not erase the company’s technological and ecosystem advantages. Competitors have an opening to prove that alternatives make economic sense, but slower AI spending could also shrink the market they are counting on.
