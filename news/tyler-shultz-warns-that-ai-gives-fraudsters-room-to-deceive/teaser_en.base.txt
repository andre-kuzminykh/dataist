Tyler Shultz, a Theranos whistleblower, sees a familiar risk in the AI boom: investors may be backing companies without knowing what their technology can actually do. In an interview with The San Francisco Standard, he warned that AI gives people who want to deceive others “a great opportunity.” The comparison is rooted in his experience: Theranos reached a valuation of about $10 billion without making its blood-testing technology work, while patients received dangerously inaccurate results.
