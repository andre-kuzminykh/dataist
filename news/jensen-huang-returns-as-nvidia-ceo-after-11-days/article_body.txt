# Jensen Huang immediately returns as head of Nvidia

## Why the board changed its decision

Huang’s return was announced on Monday. Board chair Harvey Jones, who was recently appointed to the role, said the board believes Huang’s return is in the interests of Nvidia’s shareholders, employees, partners and customers. The company also said it was satisfied with Huang’s candor and ability to lead. In making its decision, the board considered the views of senior US government officials.

Huang stepped down on November 12. Five days later, he publicly said he was prepared to return: employees, investors, business partners and customers had urged him to lead the company again, he said. He added that he would agree if the board decided it was in the interests of shareholders, employees, Nvidia and the US.

On the day he resigned, company employees rallied behind Huang: according to WIRED, they left more than 1,600 comments under a post in the internal “suggestion box,” calling on the board to bring him back. A public petition soon followed. At the time of publication, it had gathered more than 200,000 signatures; most signatories identified themselves as Nvidia shareholders. Entrepreneur Elon Musk and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan said they planned to sign the petition or had already done so.

Huang’s reinstatement marked a reversal by the board. At a reporting meeting on November 17, board representatives said they did not intend to return him to the CEO role. WIRED also reported that the board was concerned about what one of the publication’s sources called Huang’s “lies” over the past year or so.

Trump, who worked closely with Huang during his second term, had previously left open the possibility of his return. On November 16, the president told reporters he would not object to it, adding that he thought Huang had learned an important lesson. On Monday, Trump wrote that Nvidia’s board had made the right decision.

## What preceded the resignation

Huang’s departure followed several days of intense scrutiny of his extramarital romantic relationships. They included a relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese entrepreneur who is not related to him, a relationship with an Nvidia employee who reported directly to him, and a relationship with another company employee.

The relationship with Alice Huang drew particular attention on November 6. The New York Times and the Financial Times reported that US intelligence officials suspected her of having ties to China’s Ministry of State Security. Both publications cited an intelligence memo circulated among senior Trump administration officials last year. In the following days, some reports and administration officials questioned the accuracy of the information. The Chinese government has repeatedly denied that Alice was its spy.

Alice led Singapore-based AI company Megaspeed. She was accused of helping Chinese tech companies use Nvidia’s most advanced chips outside China in violation of US export restrictions. Media reports also said she had worked as a television reporter, in HSBC’s private banking division in Hong Kong, and at a Chinese state-backed investment bank.

## How Nvidia’s role has changed

Huang founded Nvidia in 1993 with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem. According to available accounts, their goal was to create realistic 3D graphics for personal computers. For a long time, the company was known mainly in the gaming community. Nvidia became widely known during the recent AI boom, as its graphics processors became a key part of the infrastructure used to develop advanced models.

As of September 2026, Nvidia controlled about 90% of the global graphics processor market. A year earlier, it became the first company valued at more than $4 trillion. Nvidia’s share price fell sharply after Huang’s resignation.

Dataist’s take: Huang’s return shows how closely Nvidia’s leadership is tied to his presence: the board reversed its earlier decision after public support from employees, investors and customers. At the same time, the reasons for his resignation involve allegations and information whose accuracy was disputed, so his return does not resolve the questions that preceded his departure.
