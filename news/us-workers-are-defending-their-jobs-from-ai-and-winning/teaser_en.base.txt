In Oakland, California, 94.1% of city workers’ union members voted to authorize a strike on October 22, worried that AI would intensify surveillance and lead to unfair performance evaluations. The strike, scheduled for November 5, has not gone ahead: the city and the union reached a tentative three-year agreement. It requires employees whose jobs are displaced by AI to be retrained or reassigned rather than laid off. Unions across the country are seeking similar protections as automation spreads: according to an Ernst & Young survey conducted from July to August 2026, nearly 70% of state and local government agencies have already adopted or are adopting generative AI.
