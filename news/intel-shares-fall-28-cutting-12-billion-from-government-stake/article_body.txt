## The pressure is showing up in PCs

Intel also warned that gross margin would fall from 43% to 37%. With large investments under way, the company does not expect positive cash flow.

The company’s difficulties extend beyond its forecast. PC sales are slowing as the AI industry absorbs memory and other components, pushing up computer prices. Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan said on a conference call that customers were already reporting a slower PC market because of memory prices and availability. He added that prices for complete computer systems had risen substantially since the start of the year, putting more pressure on already constrained IT budgets.

## Data centers offer growth, not an easy exit

Intel’s data center revenue rose 78% from the same quarter a year earlier. But the company is constrained by limited factory capacity and a shortage of external customers, while competing with major manufacturer Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co.

One new Intel factory in Arizona is already operating. The next is not expected to launch before 2028.

[INFO:compare] Data center revenue :: up 78% | Gross margin :: down to 37%

## A rally still exposed to execution

I think the contrast matters more than the market’s one-day reaction. A 78% increase in data center revenue is substantial, but it has not removed the constraints on capacity or brought enough outside customers to make the business a clear counterweight to the PC slowdown.

The announcement is quiet about how Intel plans to bridge that gap before its next factory comes online. For now, investors are being asked to value a company whose growth is real, but whose manufacturing limits and investment needs still weigh on cash generation.
