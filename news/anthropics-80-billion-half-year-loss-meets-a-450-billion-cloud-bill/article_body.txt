## The gap is widening

The reported net loss was $13 billion in the first half of 2025. A year later, it had reached $80 billion. The Wall Street Journal called it one of the largest six-month losses ever recorded.

Revenue growth looks modest beside that change: first-half 2026 revenue was $16.3 billion, only $1.1 billion higher than in the same period last year. The figures sharpen a broader concern about the scale of AI spending and whether the returns can arrive fast enough to support it.

The story also points to a risk beyond Anthropic. The Russian report argues that the US economy is now heavily dependent on a cycle of AI investment and data-center development. If that cycle weakens, the financial consequences may not stay within the companies building AI.

## A long cloud bill

Anthropic faces another major commitment: $450 billion for cloud infrastructure over the next seven to ten years. The Wall Street Journal described the available time to address the problem as limited, and the spending as perhaps the most expensive effort to defer a reckoning the world has seen.

I think the striking part is not simply the size of the loss, but the mismatch between that loss and the reported revenue increase. The company’s cloud commitments make this more than a question of whether it can post a better quarter: they create a long runway of costs that must be financed while the business grows.

What the announcement does not tell us is how much of the $450 billion is fixed, or what revenue growth would make those commitments sustainable. Without that detail, the figures show the scale of the bet, but not how Anthropic plans to make it pay.
