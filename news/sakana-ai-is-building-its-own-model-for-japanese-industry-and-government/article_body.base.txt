# Sakana AI is building its own model for Japanese industry and government

## What Sawara is being built for

Sakana AI’s plans for its own foundation model come amid interest in potential uses for Japan’s defense and cybersecurity, as well as concerns about dependence on American and Chinese technology. Ha said Sakana AI’s long-term goal is to apply AI in industry and manufacturing. Japan also needs homegrown solutions for security tasks, particularly if access to foreign models becomes restricted.

Sakana AI will begin work on Sawara this month. In 2027, the company plans to train a small model and a larger version. Ha expects the smaller model to be particularly useful for local deployment.

The company’s own model is intended to complement products it has already developed:

- <strong>Fugu</strong> is an orchestration system that calls on other large language models, including those developed by American AI labs, and assembles AI agents for specific tasks. Sakana AI introduced it about five months before announcing Sawara.
- <strong>Namazu</strong> is a model focused on Japanese. It fine-tunes open models from Meta and DeepSeek to better account for the Japanese language, culture and business needs. Its use cases include translation, questions about politics and running AI agents.

Ha explained that open models do not always suit Japanese companies without modification: their answers may reflect their creators’ views, and the models may refuse to discuss Japan or Japanese politics, or respond in ways Japanese users do not want.

Developing Sawara alone will not end Japan’s dependence on foreign technology. Ha described pretraining the model as the last missing piece that would give Sakana AI a full technology stack. The company plans to apply Sawara in industry and manufacturing.

## Local deployment and government requirements

Foreign models will remain part of Sakana AI’s work: Fugu, for example, uses OpenAI models. But they are not enough for some tasks. In cybersecurity, Ha said, Japan needs adapted models that can be deployed on its own premises.

Japan will continue using American systems, but it also needs domestic alternatives. Ha said he respects Snowflake, Palantir and Datadog, but that Japan will need its own systems for national defense.

In July, Sakana AI signed a research contract with Japan’s Ministry of Defense. The two sides are studying how AI can analyze and combine information from different sources. The company has also worked with the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications on systems for detecting disinformation.

Working with government agencies has influenced how Sakana AI plans to develop Sawara. Unlike commercial customers, government agencies often need to inspect training data, customize models and run them on their own premises. Ha believes many governments have similar requirements.

Commercial customers can use Fugu, Namazu and foreign models together. In government projects, Sakana AI is currently focused on two areas:

- detecting and analyzing disinformation;
- multimodal systems that combine data from different government sources.

## Plans for a public listing

The idea of building its own foundation models emerged amid reports from Nikkei and Bloomberg earlier this month about a possible Sakana AI IPO.

Ha said the company has no plans to go public in the near future. The timelines and potential valuation mentioned in those reports were speculation. He does not know when Sakana AI might list, but expects that it will eventually go public in Japan.

Ha sees Sakana AI’s future growth as tied to the international expansion of Japanese manufacturers. The company wants not so much to sell its own models as to help local businesses apply AI and compete in global markets. Sakana AI plans to enter overseas markets alongside the government and industrial companies.

Dataist's take: Sawara is intended to become part of Japan’s AI infrastructure for industry and government, but it will not replace foreign models: Ha himself acknowledges that Japan will continue using them. The project’s practical value will depend on whether Sakana AI can combine local deployment and data inspection requirements with models that work on real-world tasks.
