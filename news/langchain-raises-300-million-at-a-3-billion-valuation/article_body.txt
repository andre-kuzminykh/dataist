The startup LangChain, which develops tools for building and managing AI agents, raised $300 million in a Series C round. The round was led by CapitalG, with Singapore’s GIC, DFJ Growth, IVP and Sapphire Ventures also participating. It valued the company at $3 billion, more than double its previous valuation of $1.25 billion, set about a year ago after it raised $125 million.

# LangChain raises $300 million at a $3 billion valuation

## From open-source project to AI agent management

LangChain emerged in 2022 as a popular open-source project. It helped large language models perform tasks they struggled with at the time: searching the internet, calling APIs and working with databases.

As leading AI labs developed tools that let models handle these tasks, LangChain shifted its focus to managing AI agents. The company offers businesses a way to maintain control over the AI systems they use.

Its software product, LangSmith, is designed for building and managing agents. The company says it does not lock customers into a particular model, cloud platform or ecosystem, unlike offerings from major cloud providers and leading AI labs. LangChain also says LangSmith helps customers monitor agent behavior and is easy to use.

## Customers and competitors

Other startups offer similar capabilities and are also finding success. The source gives emerging competitor Mintive as an example. LangChain is counting on an advantage from entering the market earlier and reports that LangSmith has 7,000 customers.

They include startups Harvey, Rippling and Clay, as well as large companies Nvidia, Toyota and Salesforce.

<strong>Dataist's take:</strong> The new valuation suggests investors see AI agent management as a promising market, but it does not by itself prove that demand is durable. LangChain reports 7,000 customers, but the source provides no data on how much they spend or how extensively they use LangSmith.
