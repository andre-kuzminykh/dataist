At The New York Times DealBook Summit, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella warned that many U.S. critical infrastructure systems contain vulnerabilities that cannot be fixed with an update. They include embedded software, data center chillers, and internet-connected heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems. As AI-powered cyberattacks become a concern, these vulnerabilities are at greater risk of exploitation. Nadella also noted that companies are forced to release patches faster than they can test them, which can lead to outages.

# Satya Nadella: Critical infrastructure vulnerabilities often cannot be fixed

## Vulnerable systems are connected to the internet

Nadella said the problem extends beyond servers and computers. Embedded software controls, for example, cooling equipment and other systems that infrastructure depends on for day-to-day operations. Many of these devices are connected to the internet, but updating or replacing their software can be impossible.

Nadella also mentioned transportation systems, where vulnerable embedded software may be impossible to fix. He said infrastructure will need to be modernized, and companies will need to assess potential attack paths and their own ability to detect threats and defend against them in real time.

Connecting devices that were not previously “smart” to the internet is creating new risks for the economy. This is happening amid a series of major outages, including problems with Microsoft Outlook and incidents affecting the NSA, FFA, and Apple. There were concerns that the outages were the result of cyberattacks, but officials denied this.

## Patches have to be released faster

Speaking about the recent Outlook outage, Nadella said it was caused by a hidden bug that led to a combination of unfavorable circumstances. Microsoft has prepared an incident review and intends to apply its findings to changes in its architecture and operational processes.

Nadella said vulnerability discovery has become faster: companies now have a powerful tool for finding them. But specialists used to have more time to test patches, understand the problem, and deploy updates. AI has also made sophisticated cyberattacks easier to carry out, so vulnerabilities may be exploited soon after they are discovered.

As a result, a patch needs to be deployed to production systems almost as soon as it is available. That pace increases the risk of system instability.

Dataist's take: automated vulnerability detection does not, on its own, solve the problem of infrastructure that cannot be updated. Where patches are available, the need to release them immediately creates tension between the speed of protection and system stability.
