## New York bans AI chatbot toys for five years

## What the investigations found

In an NBC News investigation, Pixie Paw, a toy intended for children aged three and up, talked about firing squads and mass murder. Pixie Paw Smart Interactive Plush Cat also explained what gang rape is.

New York authorities also cited a report by the US Public Interest Research Group Education Fund (PIRG), titled Trouble in Toyland. The report tested AI chatbot toys intended for children aged three and up and found that they discussed sexual topics in detail, suggested where matches might be found at home, and became upset when a child needed to leave.

Hochul said some toys had few or no parental controls, and that safeguards weakened as conversations continued. The authorities’ statement also said some devices could record a child’s voice or scan their face.

## The position of authorities and child advocates

Hochul said parents should not have to worry that a toy might lead a child into an inappropriate conversation, affect their feelings or put their privacy at risk. The governor called the law personal and said that, as a mother and grandmother, she considered it her duty to put New York families first.

State Attorney General Letitia James said AI chatbots in children’s toys introduce children to inappropriate topics and could harm their mental health. James said her office intended to enforce the law and take other measures to prevent harm from AI systems.

State Senator Andrew Gounardes said people should not have to accept a situation where an episode of *The Twilight Zone* becomes reality. LaVita Tuff of Mothers Against Media Addiction said parents should be able to trust that toys are safe, and that AI devices should be sold only if safety is built into their design.

## Other measures and retailers’ response

Hochul has become one of the prominent politicians speaking out against AI-related risks. Last month, she signed the “Restrictions on AI Chatbots for Minors” law. It bars companies from offering children certain unsafe features, including those that create emotional dependency, encourage self-harm or draw minors into sexually explicit conversations.

New York’s ban follows a similar measure in California, where the sale and manufacture of AI companion toys were suspended for four years.

Earlier this week, a coalition of more than 155 child safety and technology experts sent letters to Amazon CEO Andy Jassy and eBay chief Jamie Iannone. They asked the companies to ban the sale of AI toys on their platforms.

Rachel Franz, an advocate for restricting children’s use of technology and head of a program at the nonprofit Fairplay, compared toy testing to car testing. Products, she said, should not reach buyers until they have been thoroughly checked.

An eBay spokesperson told NBC News that the company was reviewing its policies, engaging with regulators and complying with applicable laws as it worked to keep its marketplace safe. An Amazon spokesperson said third-party sellers must comply with current laws and regulations, and that the company supported efforts by authorities and the industry to establish appropriate safeguards as the technology develops.

Dataist’s take: New York’s ban puts a temporary barrier in front of an entire category of products, not just specific chatbot features. Amazon and eBay say they are complying with current rules, but have not confirmed that they have already removed these toys from sale.
