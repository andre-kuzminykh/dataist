# Solar and storage could change how AI data centers are powered

## Power is in short supply

Powering data centers has become one of the biggest debates of the AI era. In just a few years, the US power system has shifted from almost no growth to adding several gigawatts of capacity each year. Demand is growing faster than many expect, Porter told TechCrunch: data centers need physical power sources, not just computing resources.

Scaling up supply quickly is difficult because supply chains take time. Companies are therefore trying to secure electricity by any means, including buying, leasing and installing gas-fired power plants. Porter believes operators of major cloud platforms and data center developers should consider not only the options available today, but also ways to scale capacity quickly in the future.

G2VP supports this approach with an analysis of the data center power market. Porter said global natural gas generation capacity grows by about 60–70 GW a year, while solar power is already adding well over 1 TW. After 20 years of working with companies, G2VP has learned to apply Wright’s law: producing the same product repeatedly helps scale its output.

Solar panels and most types of long-duration energy storage are made up of small modular components that can be manufactured at scale. Porter said this creates an opportunity to speed up deployment, expand capacity and reduce costs at the same time.

## Two options for new data centers

G2VP has experience tracking the development of AI data centers: the fund led Crusoe’s Series C investment round. Porter told TechCrunch that this gave the firm a view of how Crusoe changed and how different waves of data center construction unfolded.

G2VP estimates that investment in data centers will exceed $4 trillion by the end of the decade. The fund sees two scenarios for the next stage of development:

- Small facilities connected to the grid and equipped with their own power sources.
- Large data centers powered by solar energy and long-duration storage.

The second scenario involves building facilities outside the shared grid. That would make it possible to use renewable energy independently of the grid and reduce reliance on constraints related to permitting, such as access to the grid or permits for atmospheric emissions. Porter said this approach could significantly speed up data centers’ time to market.

## Storage is getting cheaper

In recent years, energy storage has mainly been associated with lithium-ion batteries. Their cost is too high to compete with fossil fuels for round-the-clock electricity supply.

That is changing with a new generation of long-duration storage systems. Porter named Form Energy and Antora Energy, a G2VP portfolio company, among the firms developing these solutions. He said they have significantly reduced the cost of storing a megawatt-hour.

These technologies may not gain broad acceptance among data center developers for several years, but Porter advises taking them into account now. He compared the challenge to trying to catch a big wave at Mavericks in Northern California: to catch it, you have to move in advance and react quickly; if you stay put, the wave will pass you by.

Dataist's take: The bet on solar power and storage is primarily about building large facilities faster by sidestepping some grid and permitting constraints. But long-duration storage technologies have yet to gain broad acceptance among data center developers, so G2VP’s proposed approach still needs to be scaled.
