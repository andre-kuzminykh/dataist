# Radar Makes Podcasts Searchable and Accessible to AI Agents

Particle, a startup founded by former Twitter engineers, has launched Radar — a podcast search engine that transcribes audio and understands the content of conversations. The service already indexes more than 130,000 podcasts, including all 200 of Apple’s most popular shows across 135 categories, and adds 20,000 episodes every day. Radar extracts quotes, identifies people, companies, and topics, tracks their mentions, and provides data through a web interface, API, and MCP. Its main customers are hedge funds looking for information unavailable to ordinary AI agents, which are primarily focused on text.

Searching Conversations

Particle was founded by Sarah Baeup, who previously worked at Twitter. The company is now changing direction: instead of developing a news-reading app, it is focusing on technology for indexing spoken conversations from podcasts.

According to Baeup, hedge funds currently generate the largest volume of API requests, connecting the service directly to their own systems. Radar’s tools could also be useful to journalists and researchers. Other well-paying customers include AI-powered search platforms and data resellers. Radar’s partners include Exa, a search API provider for AI agents.

The idea grew out of one of Particle’s most requested app features. Through its API, the app found interesting podcast excerpts and added them to related news stories in the feed.

The Particle team saw the value of the technology but realized that its use inside a news app was limited. As interest in AI agents grew, the company decided to focus on an API for podcast analysis.

Baeup describes the concept as an API for analyzing new media and audio. Most services and agents with APIs scan web pages and work with text, while Radar adds audio to that data. Typically, AI agents cannot see the content of audio recordings until someone transcribes them.

What Radar Can Do

Radar transcribes more than 130,000 podcasts — the company calls it the largest podcast transcription service. The index includes all 200 of Apple’s most popular podcasts across 135 categories, with 20,000 new episodes added every day.

The transcripts identify speakers and include detailed metadata. Radar recognizes the entities being discussed: people, companies, brands, products, and topics.

The service tracks mentions of these entities across different podcasts and sends notifications. Users can receive them immediately after a mention or as a daily or weekly digest.

Notifications can be delivered by email, through Slack, or via webhook. Filters are available — for example, users can receive messages only when a particular guest discusses a specified topic. Searches can also be limited to popular podcasts.

Radar highlights self-contained excerpts with timestamps. Users can listen to them and read the corresponding section of the transcript at the same time.

Baeup explains that the team preselects notable excerpts. This makes it possible to quickly understand what was discussed in an episode when users do not have time to listen to the entire podcast and do not want to read a summary.

Radar also tracks episode topics, the people and objects mentioned, when they appeared, listener ratings and reviews, advertising, and other information. A dedicated podcast ad search engine finds every episode in which a specific company was advertised and shows how often such placements appeared over time.

Additional capabilities could involve analyzing political bias, podcast rankings, audience estimates, sponsorship placements, and brand suitability.

API for AI Agents

All of these features are available through Radar’s web interface, but the company considers its API and MCP to be the main products. They allow AI agents and other companies to access the same data and analytical capabilities programmatically.

Radar pricing starts at $29 per month for one user. The business plan costs $399 per month and includes 20 users. API access is priced on an individual basis.

In the future, Particle plans to add other audio and video sources to the index, including YouTube, as well as short news clips. The company hopes this will help AI agents work not only with the text of web pages, but also with the content of conversations, interviews, and video materials.
