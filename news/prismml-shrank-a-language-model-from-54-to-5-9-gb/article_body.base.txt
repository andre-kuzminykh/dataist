PrismML reduced a language model from 54 to 5.9 GB

Startup PrismML has released Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B, an updated language model based on Qwen3.8-27B. It takes up 5.9 GB of memory, compared with about 54 GB if its weights were stored in the standard 16-bit format. The compression comes from ternary quantization: each weight can take only one of three values — −1, 0 or +1. The new version is tuned to work more reliably on tasks where an AI agent makes repeated, extended use of tools.

## Benchmark results

Bonsai 2.5 requires fewer passes and tokens than Bonsai 2, and is less likely to get stuck in loops or engage in prolonged reasoning. Its score on SWE-bench Verified rose from 60.8 to 75.6%. The base Qwen model scored 80.6%.

On AppWorld, a benchmark that tests AI agents, Bonsai’s score rose from 51.2 to 90.5%, nearly matching the base model. On LiveCodeBench v6, the new version scored slightly below its predecessor.

## Speed and availability

On an RTX 4090 GPU, the model generates about 204 tokens per second using multi-token prediction. Its weights are distributed under the Apache 2.0 license. The model runs through llama.cpp on Nvidia and Intel GPUs, and through MLX on Mac, iPhone and iPad.

PrismML grew out of a research group at the California Institute of Technology. The company’s investors include Khosla Ventures, Google and Samsung. The startup introduced its first Bonsai 27B model in July; it was built on an older Qwen model. The most compact version required less than 4 GB of memory and could run directly on an iPhone.

Dataist’s take: The results suggest that aggressive compression does not necessarily rule out strong performance on agent benchmarks: Bonsai 2.5 came close to the base Qwen model on AppWorld and SWE-bench Verified. However, the new version scored below its predecessor on LiveCodeBench v6, so one set of tests is not enough to judge its suitability for every task.
