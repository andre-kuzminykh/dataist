## The missing measure is economic value

At GTC Berlin on October 21, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said companies need to produce AI, not just consume it, to participate in the AI economy. The “AI factory” idea, however, now extends beyond computing capacity.

- HPE is betting on managed APIs and integration with the Model Context Protocol (MCP).
- VAST announced the Agent Sandbox Foundation Stack with Nvidia, designed to preserve agent state, data access and audit logs as available compute changes.
- Nebius’s security team highlighted confidential computing on GPUs, which can protect data and model weights during processing and verify an environment before encryption keys are handed over.

Infrastructure choices increasingly involve integration and security as well as compute. For agents handling longer tasks, teams also have to decide how they access company knowledge, retain task state, use different models and recover from errors.

The survey points to a more basic gap: companies are planning platform changes without a financially validated measure of what their current infrastructure returns. Across all 102 respondents, 63% planned to adopt a new, additional or replacement platform within a year. Just 10% said their AI infrastructure return metric had been reviewed and approved by finance. Among those planning a platform change, 91% lacked such a measure. Among those not planning one, the figure was 89%; the difference was not statistically significant.

Respondents whose primary platform was in production rated overall satisfaction at 4.0 out of 5.0, ease of implementation at 3.9 and value for money at 3.6. Those scores can describe a platform that works day to day. They do not establish whether its output is worth its cost.

For agent applications, a more useful measure may be the cost of a successfully completed task. That calculation should include inference, information retrieval, storage, retries and human review, then compare the total with the value delivered against an agreed baseline. Success also needs a quality bar: a support ticket closed incorrectly is not a successful task, and the cost of fixing a code change belongs in the cost of completing it.

## Architecture changes the cost of a task

Respondents cited integration with existing cloud and data stacks (48%), performance (42%) and GPU availability (35%) as factors in infrastructure selection. The difference between integration and performance was not statistically significant.

The practical question is where a workflow loses time, repeats work or spends resources without improving the result. A more capable model may help, but so might better information retrieval or context, a different model-serving configuration, or a clearer division of work between models and tools.

That makes “memory” an architectural question with three distinct parts:

- Institutional memory: the organization’s durable knowledge, including rules, records, procedures and past decisions.
- Agent task memory: the state needed to continue a job, including what has happened, what remains and the results of earlier actions.
- Inference memory: the resources and cached computations used to serve models.

These are not interchangeable. Company knowledge needs to be current, relevant and permissioned. Task memory should preserve useful progress without turning temporary output into official organizational knowledge. Inference-memory techniques need to be tested on the workloads they are meant to serve.

Anthropic’s context-engineering guidance describes structured notes outside the context window as one way to preserve information during long-running work. Automatic prefix caching in vLLM operates at a different layer: it reuses computation when requests begin with the same prompt. The vLLM documentation says this reduces prompt-processing work, not the time needed to generate new tokens.

In the September survey, 41% of respondents said they used or were evaluating Nvidia for inference memory, and 39% said the same of Dell; the difference was not statistically significant. Open-source key-value cache tools and model-level efficiency techniques each drew 21%. Another 16% said they were not working on inference-memory constraints or did not know about them.

## What companies are not measuring

The survey suggests infrastructure plans are advancing faster than financial validation. My read is that GPU utilization alone cannot resolve that tension: even a well-used system might be expensive per successful task, while low utilization does not by itself show that replacing the platform will improve the economics.

The same problem applies to model selection. Small models may suit bounded tasks, while harder stages need a different model or a person. The test is whether a model placed in the right part of a workflow meets the quality requirement at lower total cost—not whether its token price is lower. The September report named token price as a platform-selection factor for 23% of respondents, up from 11% in August; the difference between the two monthly samples was statistically significant. Total task cost still depends on token use, retries, tools and human intervention.

Responses also show a divide between leadership and individual contributors. Total cost of ownership was cited as a selection factor by 52% of vice presidents and directors, compared with 4% of individual contributors, a statistically significant difference under the report’s test for small independent groups. Individual contributors most often chose integration, at 58%.

A shared measure could connect those priorities: cost per successful task. It gives finance a way to assess value and gives technical teams a way to test whether changes to retrieval, model choice, caching or task-state management justify their expense.

Before buying new infrastructure, teams can start with a specific agent workflow:

- Have the application or product owner define successful completion and acceptable human intervention.
- Have the data team identify authoritative sources and access rules.
- Instrument latency, utilization, cache behavior and total operating costs.
- Agree on a baseline with finance and check the value delivered.
- Change one architectural component at a time and measure the effect on cost per successful task.

I think the consequential question is not whether companies should build AI factories, but what they will count as output from one. More capable models widen the range of tasks agents can attempt; without a quality threshold and an agreed cost measure, a new platform can add capacity without establishing that the work is worth doing.
