Stroke rehabilitation depends on repeated movement practice, but about 5 million people a year are left with long-term impairments after a stroke, while a shortage of physical therapists limits access to care. Johannes Lachner, a former MIT postdoc and now an associate professor at Purdue University, and Noah Geiger, a former visiting student at MIT, have built a robotic system that combines generative AI with real-time force feedback. It learns from human therapists and adjusts how much support patients get as they reach, lift their arms and practise everyday tasks.
