Sierra, a company that automates customer service for businesses, raised $1.75 billion in a Series F round. Its valuation reached $30 billion, twice what it was six months ago. Nearly half of the companies on the Fortune 50 list are Sierra customers. The company also said it had doubled its annualized revenue to $400 million in five months.

# Sierra doubled its valuation to $30 billion in six months

## Who invested in Sierra

The round was led by Greenoaks and Lightspeed Venture Partners. Previous investors also participated: Benchmark, Sequoia, Greenoaks, GV, and Tiger Global.

Sierra was founded by former Salesforce co-chair Bret Taylor and Clay Bavor. Its customers include BNY, HSBC, Singtel, Wells Fargo, JPMorganChase, Pearson, Next, and Cox Automotive. Sierra says it works with nearly half of the companies on the Fortune 50 list.

Later last month, the company said its outcome-based pricing model had helped double its annualized revenue to $400 million in five months.

## Competition in customer service automation

Business automation is one of the few areas where AI is being adopted quickly. Sierra follows a model similar to other companies in customer service: it develops a platform for building AI agents and partners with model developers to offer businesses a choice.

As personal and workplace AI agents begin interacting with corporate systems, Sierra is also developing protocol specifications for these agents with Meta, Stripe, and Walmart.

The company will use the new funding to develop its technology stack for AI agents that serve customers and expand its sales team. Sierra aims to sign deals with large companies and support them faster than its competitors.

As demand grows, customer service startups are raising large investments and reaching high valuations:

- Voice AI developer ElevenLabs is valued at $22 billion.
- Decagon is valued at $4.5 billion.
- Wonderful is valued at $5 billion.
- Parloa is valued at $3 billion.

Several other startups have raised more than $100 million.

At the HumanX conference in Amsterdam in September, Sierra co-founder Clay Bavor said that, contrary to the common view that AI is replacing contact center employees, most of the company’s customers are redistributing responsibilities among their existing staff.

Dataist's take: Sierra’s growth shows that enterprise customer service automation has become a major investment market. But the company operates in a competitive environment, and its plans to expand sales and its technology platform do not, by themselves, guarantee that it will maintain its edge.
