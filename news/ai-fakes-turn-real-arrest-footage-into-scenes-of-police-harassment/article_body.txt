## How the videos are made

Copyleaks found AI-generated clips built from real arrest footage, some apparently taken from reality shows such as TLC’s “D.U.I.” The footage is altered to make officers kiss people they have detained and touch them sexually.

The people in the clips are often handcuffed beside, inside or leaning against police cars. The fabricated sexual scene appears at the start of a longer arrest video, apparently to catch the attention of people scrolling through their feeds and keep them watching.

A few details make the clips especially damaging. Some women and men appear to smile while being kissed or groped, potentially making it look as though they consent to sexual contact from officers arresting them. Some videos are labeled as AI-generated and carry a disclaimer saying they are “for entertainment purposes only and do not have harmful consequences.” That wording does not change the fact that the clips sexualize real people without their consent.

Copyleaks co-founder and CEO Alon Yamin called the videos a troubling example of AI being used to make fake versions of real material for attention and engagement on social media. The clips, he said, depict real officers and civilians as doing things they did not do. Yamin added that the situation raises concerns for everyone shown in or connected to the videos.

## TikTok removes flagged clips

TikTok removed the material journalists brought to its attention and cited its rules requiring clear labels on realistic AI-generated content.

That response addresses the platform’s labeling and removal rules, but the more troubling issue is the material being altered: real footage that can falsely implicate both officers and civilians. I think the disclaimer is a distraction from that harm. A label can identify a fake; it cannot undo the use of someone’s real arrest to stage an intimate act they never agreed to.
