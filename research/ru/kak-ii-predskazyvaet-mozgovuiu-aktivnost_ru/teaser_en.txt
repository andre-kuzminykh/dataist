Can an AI model learn to predict how the brain responds without being rebuilt from scratch?

The authors show that a small set of trainable adjustments can adapt large, pretrained AI models to predict brain activity from what people see and hear. This makes better use of scarce brain data, while revealing how the model combines different kinds of sensory information.

In this review we look at how the approach works, what it reveals about brain responses, and why adapting many models efficiently could lead to stronger predictions.
