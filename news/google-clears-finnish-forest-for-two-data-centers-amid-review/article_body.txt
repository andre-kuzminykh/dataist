## Four sites, two data centers

Satellite images show four separate areas cleared for the projects. Contractors began felling trees before the assessments were complete, prompting criticism from the conservation association.

Its chair, Hanna Halmenpää, said Tuike Finland Oy, Google’s Finnish project company, may have acted through gross incompetence, disregard for Finnish law, or both.

The dispute follows reporting last month by Finnish newspaper Kaleva. It also comes as Google plans a $15 billion investment in Finland, its largest single investment in Europe.

## What the review must establish

The competing claims are specific: Google says the work followed forestry law and that broad environmental assessments are finished; the conservation association wants work stopped until current inspections conclude. The source of the concern is not only the cleared area but its place in the landscape: the forest connects animal habitats, and the association’s chair fears local bird species may have been harmed.

I think the central issue is whether the assessments can still meaningfully guide the project after clearing has begun. The announcement gives no findings from the ongoing checks, so it remains unclear what regulators are examining or whether the work will pause.

Google’s planned investment makes the tension harder to ignore: Finland is a major destination for the company’s European spending, while the local environmental review is still unresolved. The projects’ scale is visible on the ground; the consequences for wildlife, and the regulator’s response, are not yet established.
