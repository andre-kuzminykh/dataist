Microsoft Declares Code Red After Major Outage and Turns to More AI

## What happened during the outage

The Exchange Online outage was part of a series of major disruptions to technology services. Jason Henderson, president of the M365 Core division, told employees it was the largest incident in Exchange Online’s history by number of customers affected: never before had so many users been left without email for so long.

Microsoft vice president Amanda Silver, who oversees M365 Core and Work IQ, the company’s enterprise AI agent platform, compared conditions for engineers to “the fog of war.” She said employees exchanged more than 11,000 messages across 11 Teams chats as they tried to understand what had happened. The data needed for diagnosis was spread across different internal tracking systems.

Charles Lamanna, Microsoft’s executive vice president of Copilot, agents and platform, described the scale of the problems as roughly a decade’s worth of outages that could be considered acceptable.

## Why Microsoft needs AI agents

Code Red is the highest-priority mode, mobilizing employees to resolve critical incidents. Microsoft declared it to change how the company prevents and responds to major outages. Its goal is to resolve global incidents within an hour.

To speed up diagnosis, Microsoft plans to bring in more AI agents to help engineers find the cause of problems and analyze data faster. But using such systems in enterprise infrastructure carries risks: Meta and Amazon have already experienced serious incidents caused by AI agent failures or careless use.

## Cuts and automation

Over the past year, Microsoft has laid off or pressured thousands of employees to leave. The company has also offered buyouts to another 60,000 employees—more than a quarter of its remaining workforce.

Dataist's take: Microsoft’s plan links faster incident response to automated diagnosis, even as the company continues to cut staff. It remains unclear how much AI agents can make up for the loss of employees.
