## A different kind of compute cloud

General Compute has signed a $400 million agreement to buy new chips from SambaNova, the first debt financing for inference chips, and later added Positron and Cerebras as suppliers. A customer with $100 million in annual recurring revenue helped lay the groundwork for the Series A.

While many infrastructure companies have focused on GPUs, General Compute is building around chips designed for inference. It rents space in other companies’ data centers, uses smaller power sources and converts former bitcoin-mining sites into AI model-serving centers. The company supplies servers without software installed; cloud providers use them to run inference services for developers.

The approach gives General Compute places to deploy chips without waiting to build data centers from the ground up. But it is unlikely to make the company one of the largest neoclouds or a near-term rival to hyperscalers.

Puklowski expects the company to deploy 75 megawatts of compute capacity in 2027. He estimates it could access 200 megawatts of power, more than it is likely to fund and equip.

## Funding is part of the operating model

The company’s post-money valuation rose from $60 million after its seed round in May to $800 million after this round. That is a sharp increase, but the infrastructure plan still depends on raising money as well as securing power and hardware.

Puklowski told TechCrunch that finding capital through both debt and equity will become a constant part of his job.

Blackbird’s conviction also reflects its investment in Baseten, an inference cloud platform. James Palmer, a Blackbird partner and one of the round’s leads, said the firm had seen Baseten grow quickly over the past 10 months, alongside demand for open-weight inference and systems companies can own. He said Cerebras, Positron and SambaNova need distribution partners that can help them scale and manage the physical complexity of infrastructure.

Executives at all three chipmakers spoke positively about General Compute to TechCrunch, pointing to its focus on the economics of AI cloud computing. SambaNova CEO and co-founder Rodrigo Liang said that while everyone in the market sells speed, the central question is whether it can make money.

## The bet behind the round

General Compute CEO and co-founder Finn Puklowski is not worried by Elon Musk’s predictions of a chip glut and a market collapse. He argues that Musk has an interest in lowering prices for the GPUs he wants to buy. Puklowski says companies contact him every week looking for more capacity on TPUs, GPUs and other chips. He also believes Anthropic’s optimistic prospectus ahead of a potential IPO has temporarily eased investor concerns.

<strong>My read is that the round is less a verdict on the end of the neocloud boom than a bet on a particular bottleneck: getting inference chips into usable facilities.</strong> General Compute’s model avoids some construction delays, but does not remove the need to finance the hardware, power and sites. The company’s available power may exceed what it can equip and pay for; its growth will depend on closing that gap as much as on demand for inference.
