# Delivery Robots Removed from Sheffield Streets

Autonomous delivery robots from Starship Technologies have been removed from the streets of Sheffield just five months after the launch of a pilot project. The six-wheeled machines appeared in selected parts of the city in March as part of a partnership with Uber Eats. During the trial, the robots repeatedly had their signal flags snapped off, were covered in graffiti, and prompted complaints that they were obstructing pedestrians. Starship said it had “paused the service” after the trial ended naturally, and that vandalism had not influenced the decision.

The robots, which stand about 30 centimetres tall, transported orders from supermarkets and restaurants. Devices of this kind have already become a familiar sight on the streets of major UK cities, including Leeds and London.

Opinions about them are divided. Some praise the robots for their manoeuvrability and convenience, while others believe they block pavements and are equipped with a “sinister” number of cameras. In some parts of Leeds, the robots have become a permanent feature of the urban environment, but in Sheffield Starship has so far halted the service.

In addition to the damage inflicted on the robots themselves in March, unknown individuals vandalised a charging station and placed road cones behind the machines so they could not drive away.

Ryan Case, Starship’s head of public relations and communications, said the company had completed its Sheffield trial and paused the service to analyse what it had learned. According to him, there had been several incidents of vandalism, which was particularly frustrating because local businesses rely on the robots for deliveries. However, Starship described the suspension as a commercial decision and the natural conclusion of the pilot phase, rather than a response to damage to the robots.
