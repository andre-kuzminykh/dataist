# Lovable wants to help users turn their interests into businesses

## From apps to income

Generative AI has changed software development: instead of learning programming languages, people can describe a task in plain language and let a system generate the code. Lovable CEO Anton Osika got involved early. In 2023, he released a viral open-source tool that was a precursor to the current platform. Osika says his goal is to lower the barriers to building software.

The idea of “vibe coding” caught the technology industry’s attention: people without specialized skills began building software for everyday tasks. Parents could make an app to schedule their children’s sports activities, food lovers could create menus from what was in the fridge, and people in polyamorous relationships could build a tool to manage their relationship hierarchies. Users could also put together other niche apps to meet their needs.

Now Osika wants Lovable customers to make money from the projects they build. According to the startup, about 80% of users arrive with a financial motive, such as creating an online marketplace. So far, however, only 35% earn income from their projects.

## How Lovable helps small businesses

Next year, the company plans to offer tools for starting and running a small business, such as a bakery. Co-founder and CTO Fabian Hedin said Lovable is developing automation for different stages, from initial business strategy and website creation to customer retention and marketing plans.

A bakery owner will be able to get a chatbot-style morning briefing in the Lovable dashboard. It will analyze how many cake boxes were sold during a holiday promotion, which customer emails the system answered automatically, and remind the owner about financial payments. The user sets goals, and AI helps them work toward those goals.

Content creators already use Lovable to build businesses around their audiences. Osika said he recently had dinner in New York with creators, one of whom sells a book through the platform; others use it to sell merchandise.

## Security as a condition for growth

The trust of aspiring entrepreneurs matters to Lovable’s new direction, but platforms built with vibe coding have had security problems: projects could expose user data. Several similar services came under criticism this year for allowing apps with data exposed to the public internet to be published. Earlier this month, Lovable revised its approach to security, aiming to make websites built on the platform more secure by default.

Lovable’s head of security, Igor Andryushchenko, said security is built directly into how the platform works and remains one of the company’s main investment priorities. When real people start using a product, he said, security becomes part of the product itself.

Osika will consider Lovable successful next year if it helps a new generation of entrepreneurs launch sustainable businesses. Dataist’s take: moving from app creation to business-process automation broadens Lovable’s mission, but customer trust will also depend on data protection. The company already has security plans, but there is no information yet on how many users will be able to turn their projects into a sustainable source of income.
