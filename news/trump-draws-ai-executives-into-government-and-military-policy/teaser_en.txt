Over the past week, Donald Trump has tied his administration more closely to AI companies, the Pentagon and the intelligence services. The moves put executives inside the process of setting AI policy while giving intelligence chief Jay Clayton a second role overseeing the administration’s AI initiatives. Together, they suggest that AI is becoming part of how the US government defines its power: in regulation, military planning and the use of government data. That shift matters beyond the United States, especially for countries that depend on American technology.
