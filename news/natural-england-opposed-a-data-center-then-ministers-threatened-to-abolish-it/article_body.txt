# Nature regulator opposes data center near Bristol

## Risks to nature and people

Earlier this month, Natural England warned the city council that the data center could disturb rare and vulnerable bird species and drive them from their habitats. The Severn Estuary Special Protection Area is 600 meters from the site. Another protected area, Hallen Marsh, is 270 meters away; according to Natural England, the developers did not account for it in their assessment of noise impacts.

The Environment Agency pointed to a flood risk: the project is in a high-risk area, and the developers have not explained how people would find safe shelter during severe flooding. The agency also said it had received no information about projected water use or the risk of pollution to controlled waters from contaminated soil.

More than 4,000 people have opposed the construction. Bristol City Council says the project could have significant environmental effects and has called for a full environmental impact assessment.

## Dispute over regulators’ powers

Louise Haigh, the de facto deputy prime minister, accused quasi-autonomous government bodies, including Natural England, of obstructing construction. This week, she said both agencies could be abolished, with their responsibilities transferred to ministers or regional authorities.

Government representatives said that neither the objections to the Avonmouth project nor Natural England’s call for caution in building data centers had influenced Haigh’s statement about the possible abolition of the agencies.

Craig Bennett, chief executive of the Wildlife Trusts, called the threat to abolish Natural England an alarming sign of pressure on environmental regulation. Independent bodies are needed, he said, to enforce laws and ensure decisions about nature are based on science, not on the interests of companies or whichever groups a minister has spoken to most recently.

Mary Stevens, a Friends of the Earth activist, said Natural England had done its job well as an independent adviser. She added that she would not trust a minister to do that job without the agency’s independence.

## The scale of the plans and their environmental impact

The government sees building AI data centers as one way to accelerate the UK’s economic growth. To reduce the influence of local democracy on such projects, developers have been allowed to use the process for projects of national significance, in which the decision is made by a minister.

Natural England has warned ministers that large-scale data center construction requires caution. In its response to the draft national policy statement on data centers, the agency noted that these facilities are a relatively new type of development and that their full environmental impact is still being studied. It cited risks including excessive water extraction from water bodies to meet data center demand and potential harm to rare and vulnerable species.

Hundreds of AI data centers are planned in the UK. More than 300 projects are waiting to connect to the power grid; their combined demand will be 73 GW, almost twice the country’s peak winter electricity demand. Beyond their high energy use, data centers can pollute the environment, affect protected habitats and increase emissions from fossil-fuel power stations, which some developers want to use while waiting for a grid connection.

The Wildlife Trusts has launched a petition against abolishing Natural England. The organization said the current agency and its predecessors have protected England’s wildlife, land, water bodies and seas since the 1940s.

## The developer’s position

Rivington Energy, the company building the data center, said it would take the Environment Agency’s and Natural England’s comments into account and continue working with all consultation participants. It said a substantial amount of environmental and technical research had already been completed, with additional materials being prepared in response to the comments received. The company is also preparing a detailed environmental report for the next stage of the project review.

A government representative said data centers are essential to the economy, but their construction must take account of environmental protection and local residents’ interests. Developers are required to meet planning requirements, the representative said, while local authorities must assess environmental impacts and decide on local applications.

Dataist’s take: the dispute shows how difficult it is to combine faster data center construction with an independent assessment of its impact. Questions remain open for the Avonmouth project: the developers are preparing additional research, while the city council is calling for a full environmental assessment.
