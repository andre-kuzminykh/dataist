# Bharat Semi raises $24 million to develop AI image sensors

## A sensor with processing on a single chip

New Delhi-based Bharat Semi combines image capture, processing and AI on a single chip. This could eliminate the need for devices to send visual data to separate processors. Co-founder Vrinda Kapoor says this architecture could cut system costs by at least 70% compared with the traditional setup, which uses a separate image sensor, signal processor and system-on-chip.

Kapoor previously worked in medical technology and was a fellow at South Park Commons. She founded Bharat Semi in late 2021 with Vinayak Dalmiya, the company’s managing director, who had previously worked at McKinsey and in private equity. The startup’s CTO, Boyd Fowler, previously held the same role at image sensor maker OmniVision.

The company is targeting several markets:

- smart homes;
- industrial automation;
- surveillance systems;
- aerospace.

Kapoor gave the example of air conditioners that could detect when a room is empty and automatically adjust their settings. Drones, she said, could process images without more expensive computing equipment elsewhere in the system.

Bharat Semi’s first sensors are already in the prototyping stage. The second generation, with more advanced AI capabilities, is expected to reach that stage by mid-2027. The startup expects to deliver the new sensors to customers before the end of that year.

## Investment and growth plans

The $24 million round was an equity investment led by Info Edge Group, South Park Commons and Indus Bridge Ventures. Valour Capital, Micromax, Gaingels and individual investors also participated. The company will use the funds to expand its engineering team, complete product development and bring its sensors to market.

Bharat Semi has about 75 employees in India and the US. Its US team has 10 to 12 employees working on engineering and business development in Santa Clara. By mid-2027, the startup plans to grow its US headcount to around 20. The company has also started hiring engineers in Europe.

## A separate factory project

In 2024, Bharat Semi announced plans to build a semiconductor manufacturing facility in India through a technology partnership with the US Space Force. The project is still in development: the startup is looking for local partners to build and operate the facility.

The project is separate from the image sensor business. Bharat Semi plans to remain fabless, outsourcing chip production and focusing on semiconductor and software development. The $24 million it raised will not go toward building the facility.

Dataist’s take: Bharat Semi is developing a product for several international markets while seeking partners for a separate manufacturing project in India. Its sensors are still in development, with commercial production set to begin in 2027.
