# IMF chief warns AI boom could drive up interest rates

## Inflation and interest rates

Speaking at the 2026 APEC summit in Shenzhen, Georgieva linked rising consumer prices to increased demand for AI equipment and other technology products. The energy shock caused by the war in the Middle East is adding further pressure. Georgieva said the combination of these factors calls for tighter monetary policy in many countries.

Georgieva expects global inflation to reach 4.7% this year. At the same time, AI adoption is helping economic growth in some countries exceed expectations, despite the effects of the war with Iran.

Central banks have already raised rates this year since the war began in February: global oil prices topped 100 dollars a barrel, renewing inflationary pressure. The European Central Bank raised its rate in June for the first time since 2023. The US Federal Reserve followed in September, and the Bank of England did so earlier this month.

Financial markets expect borrowing costs to rise further next year. Those expectations reflect the boost rapid AI adoption is giving to economic growth amid instability caused by the conflict.

In recent weeks, however, global oil prices have fallen following the US midterm elections and an increase in crude oil supply. Business investment could weaken amid concerns about an AI bubble and turmoil in global stock markets. Economists have also warned that demand could slow: high inflation is reducing households’ ability to spend.

## AI is reshaping global trade

The energy shock will eventually pass, while AI will remain, Georgieva said. In her view, the technology will have a significant impact on global trade, competitiveness, productivity and economic growth.

AI equipment and related technology products already account for more than one-tenth of global trade in goods. That share is growing rapidly, but countries are not all benefiting equally from investment.

Georgieva urged governments to prepare for AI adoption. That means investing in digital infrastructure, labor markets and workers ready for new technologies, she said, as well as taking steps to reduce risks — particularly threats to financial stability and cybersecurity.

## Warning about financial system risks

A day before Georgieva’s speech, the European Central Bank voiced concern about rising debt among AI companies, which are borrowing to fund their expansion.

The ECB pointed to historical parallels with the dot-com bubble, which ended in a technology market crash around the turn of the millennium, and the subprime mortgage bubble, which led to the 2008 financial crisis. In its financial stability review, the regulator warned that a collapse in the AI market could spill over into the eurozone’s financial system.

For now, the sector continues to grow. On Thursday, Anthropic went public in the US. At an offering price of 83 dollars a share, it raised more than any other initial public offering. Anthropic develops the Claude chatbot; its valuation is expected to reach 1.5 trillion dollars, although the company reported losses of 80 billion dollars in the first half of 2026.

Dataist's take: The AI boom is supporting economic growth while adding to inflationary pressure, forcing central banks to factor it into interest-rate decisions. But rapid company growth does not resolve questions about the sustainability of their debt or the risk of shocks to the financial system.
