## The programmer’s job is moving up

Hansson describes the transition as the start of a new career for programmers. They will become “professional creators of things” who manage intelligence that, until recently, belonged only to science fiction.

That framing puts less emphasis on code as the primary product. The programmer’s value shifts toward deciding what should be built and directing the systems that build it. The source of that intelligence is clear in Hansson’s account: AI agents that change code rather than merely assist the person typing it.

## The architecture is unsettled

Traditional abstractions lose value when AI agents modify code, Hansson says. He does not present a replacement architecture. Instead, he says there is no ready-made plan for how the new arrangement should work, leaving developers to shape those methods now.

That uncertainty is the most concrete part of the announcement. I think the “new career” language is persuasive as a direction, but not yet as a job description. A programmer can be told to manage machine intelligence; the harder question is how teams will judge the results, divide responsibility and keep software understandable when the implementation is constantly being changed by agents.

Hansson calls the shift “the biggest event in the history of computing.” That is a sweeping assessment, and it remains his assessment. What makes it notable is that he is making it while conceding that the operating model is still being invented.
