# Jensen Huang may return as Nvidia CEO

## Campaign for a return

According to the Financial Times, Huang has discussed a possible return with people he trusts. One supporter told the paper that influential Silicon Valley executives are assembling a “war room” for the campaign. They support Huang’s return and want to secure backing at the highest levels of government. The supporter said the effort is being made with Huang’s consent.

## Circumstances of his departure

Huang acknowledged having an affair with Alice Huang, who is not related to him. The US government suspects her of spying for Beijing. Alice Huang held a senior position at Megaspeed, a Singaporean cloud computing company that bought Nvidia products. The company was investigated over suspicions that it had diverted US AI chips to China.

It remains unclear whether Alice Huang was actually connected to intelligence services. The paper notes that US authorities have previously wrongly accused people of spying for China. Reports of numerous affairs with Nvidia employees also damaged Huang’s reputation. His supporters acknowledge that these circumstances complicate efforts to bring him back: one campaign participant told the Financial Times that an affair with a subordinate could be grounds for dismissal.

## Support in Washington

Winning the White House’s favor will be difficult: Donald Trump’s administration includes influential critics of China, among them Secretary of State Marco Rubio. According to a source, Rubio’s misleading memo to Trump set off the scandal over the spying allegations; the document later made its way into the press.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, considered a supporter of a hard-line policy toward China, called for a “radical overhaul” of Nvidia’s leadership after Huang’s departure.

But Huang also has an influential supporter: Trump himself. When the scandal first broke, the president called him “a decent man.” After reports emerged that Huang might return, Trump told reporters before a trip to Manila that many people believe he should lead the company again, and that the idea is “certainly a good one.”

Dataist’s take: Huang’s return depends not only on support from Silicon Valley allies. Administration officials oppose him, and the circumstances of his departure remain disputed. The source does not say whether Nvidia’s board has considered his candidacy or when a decision might be made.
