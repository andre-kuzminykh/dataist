Apple appears to be working on an AI agent that could carry out tasks on a computer for the user: opening websites and apps, running commands, writing and debugging code, accessing services and choosing the right tools. Bloomberg found a job posting for a senior engineering manager to lead the development. The role says the agent will operate in line with Apple’s privacy and security standards. The project is still at an early stage, and Bloomberg reports that a launch is not being considered before the end of next year.

# Apple is developing an AI agent to control Macs

## What the agent is expected to do

The job posting describes an agent that can switch between tools and handle multi-step tasks across apps, browsers, the command line, code and APIs. Apple is looking for a leader to oversee the system’s development.

TechCrunch asked Apple for more information, but the company did not respond.

## Competition for computer agents

Over the past year, agents that can use computers have become a prominent area of the tech industry. Anthropic and OpenAI have released similar services. Meta has also entered the field: the company recently introduced its Muse line of agents.

Apple is also expanding its AI offerings in a new era associated with John Ternus. At the same time, Bloomberg reports, the company is developing its own digital assistant while making it harder for third-party agents to gain full control of Mac computers.

## Restrictions on third-party apps

In macOS 27.3, Apple plans to add warnings discouraging users from giving third-party agents full access to their computers. In October, the company told developers it planned to introduce further restrictions: users will have to take an explicit action to grant that level of access.

Bloomberg reports that Apple considered launching the service as early as the end of next year, but its own agent is still at an early stage of development.

Dataist's take: Apple is moving toward building its own agent while restricting similar third-party tools’ access to Mac. The company has not yet disclosed details about the product, and the projected launch timeline does not mean development is close to completion.
