## The hardware and the price

Bee Eclipse competes with Plaud Note and Pocket. Plaud Note sells for $159, Note Pro for $189 and Pocket for $129, making Eclipse the cheaper option at $99.99.

Bee Frontier comes in Onyx, Lavender and Sage. Eclipse is available in Silver, Midnight, Honey and Blush.

## From conversation to follow-up

Bee devices use AI to turn conversations into notes, then surface details as tasks, summaries and takeaways. If a user arranges to meet friends, for example, Bee can remember the date and time and create a calendar invitation. The company also presents lectures and care plans discussed with a doctor as potential uses.

All Bee devices create short conversation summaries for free and retain memories for 14 days. Bee Pro costs $11.99 a month and adds unlimited summaries, integrations with Amazon.com, Alexa+ and third-party apps, plus suggested tasks, daily memories, a personal chat assistant and conversation templates.

The devices also work with Gmail, Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook. Bee can draft emails, schedule meetings and flag items that need action. Users can upload photos or connect Apple HealthKit to include information about their overall well-being in takeaways.

## Privacy meets Alexa

Bee says its devices process audio in real time and delete the original recording. Text transcripts are encrypted with keys created and controlled by the device. According to Amazon, nobody—including its employees—can access those files unless the user deliberately shares them.

Connecting Bee to Alexa lets an Echo device use Voice ID to identify when the Bee owner is speaking, then record conversations to prepare takeaways. The feature is optional, and Amazon says transcripts and summaries can be deleted at any time. Bee’s Amazon.com and Alexa integrations also let users shop on Amazon and ask Alexa hands-free for Bee takeaways, summaries and tasks.

I think the central trade-off is not the $99.99 price but how much convenience users will grant a device that listens for useful details. The announcement describes encryption and deletion, but leaves the practical boundary around recording less clear: when does an Echo decide a conversation is worth capturing, and what does the user see before that happens?

## What Frontier adds

Both devices have one button to turn them on and off, and a light indicates when a conversation summary is being prepared. Frontier is rated IP67 for water and dust resistance, runs for up to seven days on a charge, charges over USB-C and has two microphones with improved noise filtering.

Bee devices come with a mobile app, whose interface has been updated, as well as a new desktop app. Bee Frontier is available now on Amazon; Bee Eclipse will go on sale later.

The lower-priced hardware makes Bee easier to try, but its longer-term proposition depends on users trusting the recording flow—and finding enough value in the summaries to pay $11.99 a month for the fuller service.
