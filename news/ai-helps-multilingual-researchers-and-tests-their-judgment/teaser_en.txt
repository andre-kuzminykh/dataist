On a train from Liverpool to London, moving at more than 100 miles per hour, I found roughly half the passengers around me having focused conversations with ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. I was on academic leave, supposedly a rare stretch for slow and careful thinking. Instead, I was watching people outsource questions, problems and drafts to machines. That scene captures the broader shift: one American study found that half of surveyed adults use an AI chatbot, while my own relationship with these tools moved from proofreading an email to shaping research, finances and daily decisions.
