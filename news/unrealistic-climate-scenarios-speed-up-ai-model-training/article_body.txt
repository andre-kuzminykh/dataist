# Unrealistic climate scenarios speed up AI model training

## From long simulations to fast forecasts

Climate research typically relies on large-scale physical simulations. A single scenario can take months to calculate on a supercomputer. AI models trained on those calculations can reproduce their results in seconds and help plan power-grid operations, public health measures and agriculture.

Previously, training these models required six to eight runs of large-scale simulations. They used realistic future scenarios, evenly distributed across possible conditions. This took so long that the models were often already outdated by the time training was complete. Co-author Noelle Selin also noted that they often performed poorly in unfamiliar situations.

## How synthetic data helps

The algorithm developed by the MIT, ETH Zurich and Imperial College London team trains models on scenarios with abrupt changes in climate indicators. Examples include a sudden spike in CO₂ in 2050 and a drop in methane levels in 2037. According to lead author Christopher Womack, these targeted changes help AI learn how the climate system works more accurately.

A single simulation run is enough to train the model. The team also reports that the AI reliably calculates the effects of local environmental measures.

Dataist's take: Reducing the number of simulations required could speed up the development of climate models and reduce the amount of training data they need. However, the source gives no quantitative results for accuracy tests and does not specify how the method performs beyond the scenarios described.
