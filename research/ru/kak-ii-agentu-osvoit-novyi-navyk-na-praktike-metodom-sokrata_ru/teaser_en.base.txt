A task can succeed once and still leave an AI agent with no reliable way to repeat it.

The authors propose a Socratic learning method in which an expert guides an AI agent through questions and advice, while the agent does the hands-on work: testing steps, writing its own reusable instructions, and revising them when they fail. This matters because a useful skill must work in new situations, not just explain one successful attempt. The team also finds that focusing expert review on unanswered questions can save time while improving how well those skills transfer.

In this review we look at how the method teaches agents to build and refine reusable skills, and how well those skills hold up on unfamiliar tasks.
