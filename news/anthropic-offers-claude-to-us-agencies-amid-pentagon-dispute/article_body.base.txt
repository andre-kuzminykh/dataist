## A return through civilian agencies

The Pentagon declared Anthropic a supply-chain risk in March after the company refused to remove restrictions on mass surveillance and autonomous weapons. Donald Trump then ordered all agencies to stop using Claude. The military kept using it during the war in Iran, but later took it out of service.

The legal fight has not settled the dispute. In late August, a judge in San Francisco ruled that one of the two classifications was an unlawful act of retaliation. In late September, an appeals court in Washington upheld the other.

Anthropic’s government offering includes:

- The same features available to corporate customers
- Usage-based payment within set limits
- Budgets and model access controls for each department
- Audit logs and two-person approval for sensitive actions
- Conversation storage on agency devices

## The unresolved question

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei’s dinner with Trump on Sunday may signal that tensions are easing, but the change could be temporary. I think the more important test is whether agencies can keep using Claude through this political and legal uncertainty. The offer provides administrative controls and local storage; it does not settle the disagreement over which uses Anthropic will permit.

That leaves a distinction between access and trust. Agencies can buy the same service as corporate customers, but the Pentagon dispute shows that government access can still turn on questions a procurement contract cannot resolve.
