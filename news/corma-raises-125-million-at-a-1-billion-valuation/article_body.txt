# Corma raises $125 million at a $1 billion valuation

[FIGURE:0]

## Surge in interest after attacks

Corma emerged from stealth in August. Interest in its product rose sharply after a series of security incidents prompted chief information security officers to urgently seek ways to protect their systems on October 21.

That day, a critical vulnerability was discovered in PHP, a popular web programming language. Another was found in Node.js, an open-source JavaScript runtime. LiteLLM, a popular open-source tool that routes requests among several large language model providers, also reported a security issue. Other incidents were disclosed the same day.

These events showed the scale of the burden AI’s spread is placing on security teams.

## AI-based defense systems

Corma is not the only company using AI for defense. Others include Armadin, a new startup founded by Kevin Mandia, as well as Anthropic, OpenAI, Microsoft and many other young companies. The market is still young but, according to the source, necessary.

Corma says it differs from competitors by operating as an advanced AI research lab focused on defense.

Dataist’s take: The round shows that investors are willing to back AI cybersecurity tools at an early stage of the market. Corma has so far only outlined how it differs from competitors; the source provides no data on the results of its products.
