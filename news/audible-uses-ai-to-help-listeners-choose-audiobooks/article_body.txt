## Audible is using AI to help listeners choose an audiobook

The new feature helps listeners decide what to play when they do not have time to browse reviews and ratings. Audible also draws on public reviews, synopses and metadata. The company says reader reviews will be part of each audio overview, alongside comments from editors and authors.

The approach resembles an Amazon feature that uses AI on product pages to summarize customer reviews and help shoppers assess a product’s pros and cons. But if Audible relies mainly on positive reviews, listeners may see less criticism. That remains to be tested.

The launch of audio overviews follows the introduction last month of several Audible AI features: they let listeners interact with characters, ask questions about the plot and recall events in a book through brief recaps. Recaps and the interactive “Ask a Question” feature will now be available for more books in the US, with wider availability in December. They can be used by people who have bought the book and Audible subscribers who can access it through their membership.

The new features arrive amid competition with Spotify. Last month, Spotify expanded access to audiobook listening through paid subscriptions to more than 180 markets.

Andy Tsao, Audible’s director of product and analytics, said audio overviews should feel like a recommendation from someone you know: quickly explaining what might interest you about a book and helping you discover less well-known works.

Dataist’s take: Audible is turning reviews and other material about books into a short audio format that could make it easier to choose something on the go. But the quality of the recommendation depends on which reviews make it into the overview: the company has yet to explain how it will account for negative opinions.
