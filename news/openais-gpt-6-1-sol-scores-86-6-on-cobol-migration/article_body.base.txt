## Two tests, different signals

OpenAI built the benchmarks to measure model progress. MultiLangBench asks models to create a SQLite-compatible database engine from scratch across 20 programming languages.

At the highest reasoning level, GPT-6 Astra passes an average of 87.4% of the tests. GPT-5.6 Terra scores about 38%.

[FIGURE:0]

GPT-6 Luna scores 40.3%, slightly above GPT-5.6 Terra’s 37.9%, at significantly lower cost. That comparison makes the benchmark more than a raw capability result: for this task, the newer model’s small score gain comes with a price advantage.

## COBOL migration is the sharper result

The COBOL migration benchmark asks models to translate COBOL into C++, Java, Kotlin, Python or Rust. GPT-6.1 Sol reaches 86.6% accuracy at low reasoning; GPT-5.6 Sol scores 5.4%. Raising the reasoning level lifts GPT-6.1 Sol to 97.9%.

I think the gap is more compelling than the headline’s broad claim about older languages. It shows a dramatic improvement on one migration task, but the announcement gives no detail on how the benchmark handles code that compiles yet behaves differently, or how representative its examples are of real legacy systems. That is the harder test: high benchmark accuracy matters only if it carries over to software that organizations still depend on.
