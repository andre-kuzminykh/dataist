Victoria’s Liberal Party confirmed that it used AI and search tools to prepare a condolence message for music journalist and producer Molly Meldrum. The statement was published in the name of Opposition Leader Jess Wilson shortly after Meldrum died on Monday morning, aged 83. Pangram identified the 177-word text as entirely AI-generated. The party said a staff member checked the facts and edited the text before sending it to Wilson for approval.

## How the statement was prepared

In her condolence message, Wilson called Meldrum “a true Australian icon who will never be forgotten”. The text mentioned his famous cowboy hat, colourful personality and long-standing love of the St Kilda Football Club, as well as his generosity, warmth and the affection Australians felt for him.

A Liberal Party spokesperson told Guardian Australia that the party uses search and AI tools to prepare materials and gather information. The first draft of the statement was put together with these tools while collecting information about Meldrum’s long career.

According to the publication, the original AI-generated draft did not mention the TV show Hey Hey It’s Saturday or the two occasions when Meldrum was crowned King of Moomba, Melbourne’s festival. A member of the team added those details. After being checked and edited, the text was sent to Wilson for approval.

Pangram rated the 177-word statement as 100% AI-generated. The company says its algorithm incorrectly flags human-written text as generated about once in every 10,000 checks.

## Other statements and the election campaign

Pangram identified Victorian Premier Ben Carroll’s condolence message as entirely human-written. The Victorian Labor leader recalled how viewers watched Meldrum’s program Countdown on Sundays, then went to a music shop the next morning to buy the song he had recommended.

Carroll also noted that Meldrum helped establish a St Kilda Football Club supporters’ group before Countdown began. Supporting others, he said, came naturally to the presenter. Carroll said Meldrum would receive a state funeral.

Major parties, One Nation and third-party participants made extensive use of AI in Victoria’s election campaign. Labor released a deepfake video in which Wilson celebrates Halloween and goes trick-or-treating with Warren Pickering, One Nation’s leader in Victoria. The video drew widespread criticism, after which the Greens called for the practice to be banned outright.

Last month, Australian Electoral Commissioner Jeff Pope told a federal parliamentary inquiry into artificial intelligence that requiring political communications created with AI to carry labels would be difficult. If every such message had to be labelled, he said, all political communication could be labelled by the next election or shortly afterwards — raising the question of how effective the labels would be.

Dataist’s take: The case shows that AI is already being used not only to create campaign materials but also to prepare official statements. The party says a human checked and edited the text, while the missing details underline that such a draft needs further work.
