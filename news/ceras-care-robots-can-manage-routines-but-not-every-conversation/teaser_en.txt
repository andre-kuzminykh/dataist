The robot in Vera’s home can remind her to take medicine, check in on how she feels and offer a conversation. But when the 74-year-old wants to talk about knitting, it often misses key words and steers her toward biscuits, music or television instead. Cera says at least 1,000 people in England use its robots. Their promise is practical: take on routine care tasks and give human carers more time. Whether a machine can also meet the need for companionship is less clear.
