## The money is the message

The poll, reported by Inc, was conducted by Guardrails Action, an organization that advocates for AI accountability. It found that AI has become a politically toxic issue in these key states. Even a hint that a candidate took money from major technology companies could hurt their image.

A quarter of respondents said they would consider switching parties to vote for a candidate who took a strong stand against AI companies and data-center construction.

Charles Blettler, political director of Guardrails Alliance, which is part of Guardrails Action and opposes spending in support of AI, told Inc that AI super PACs were more toxic to voters than:

- Wall Street
- Cryptocurrency
- AIPAC

Blettler said voters’ resistance to AI-industry money reflects both anxiety about AI itself and growing disgust with corruption, special-interest influence and money in politics.

## The industry is reinforcing the concern

AI companies and prominent figures in the industry often issue stark warnings: AI could destroy jobs, undermine civilization or perhaps become conscious. Anthropic and OpenAI have also warned that their AI systems could launch cyberattacks on their own.

I think the striking part is not just that candidates may pay a price for accepting AI money. The industry’s own warnings give voters another reason to distrust the companies backing those candidates. The announcement is quiet about which candidates or races were tested, so the findings do not establish how the money will affect any specific contest. But in close Senate campaigns, the perception of industry influence may be a liability before a candidate says anything about AI policy.
