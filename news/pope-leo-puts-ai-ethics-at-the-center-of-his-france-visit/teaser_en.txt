Pope Leo opened his three-day official visit to France with a warning that artificial intelligence could erode human dignity if society treats computation as intelligence and efficiency as the measure of a person. Speaking at the Élysée Palace and later at UNESCO’s Paris headquarters, the 71-year-old head of the Catholic Church argued that ethical education is the only way to keep machines serving people rather than becoming instruments of domination and injustice. His intervention places AI inside a broader critique of technology, power and the treatment of human life.
