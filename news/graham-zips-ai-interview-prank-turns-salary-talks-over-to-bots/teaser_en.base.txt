Graham Zip’s latest job-interview prank puts two AI assistants in charge of a conversation about his qualifications and pay. His own bot, Victor, speaks for him; the employer’s chatbot speaks for Brightwell Home. The exchange turns chaotic, and Victor even offers to accept a salary of $60,000. The joke lands because the setup is already familiar: some employers use AI for initial candidate calls, while job seekers increasingly face bleak employment prospects shaped in part by AI.
