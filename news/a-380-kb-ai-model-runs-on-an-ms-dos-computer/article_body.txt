## A 380 KB AI model runs on an MS-DOS computer

## A model for an old computer

The project was covered by Hackaday. The model was written in Turbo C 2.0 and runs on MS-DOS, Microsoft’s operating system, widely used on personal computers in the 1980s and early 1990s. According to the project’s creator, Anthropic Fable 5.2 was used for development and Opus 5.5 for training. In other words, a compact model was created with the help of larger models.

The model has 367,000 parameters — far fewer than the several billion typically found even in “lightweight” models. Its weights take up 380 KB and are stored in an 8-bit format. It requires 540 KB of memory to run.

## Correct facts and an invented king of France

The model has a retro interface. In one example, it correctly answers that whales are mammals and that Alexander Graham Bell invented the telephone. But when asked to multiply 17 by 23, it gives the answer 96.

Asked “Who is the king of France?”, it answered “Antarse Newton” with 100% confidence. The authors of the piece say they do not know whether anyone by that name ever existed.

Dataist’s take: the project shows that a language model can fit into the space available on a floppy disk and run on an MS-DOS-era computer. But a small size and low memory requirements do not guarantee reliable answers: the model was trained on just 3,500 facts and often makes things up.
