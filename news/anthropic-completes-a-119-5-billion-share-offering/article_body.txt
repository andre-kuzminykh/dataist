# Anthropic completes $119.5 billion share offering

## Trading and private placement

Anthropic’s Class A shares began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19, 2026, under the ticker `ANT`.

The IPO included 1,103,351,354 Class A shares owned by Anthropic and 23,648,646 shares owned by several shareholders. The price was $83 per share. The underwriters exercised their option in full to buy another 147 million shares from Anthropic at the same price. Gross IPO proceeds, before underwriting discounts and commissions and other expenses, totaled about $93.5 billion. Anthropic received about $91.6 billion of that amount; proceeds from the sale of shareholders’ shares went to them, not the company.

In a separate private placement, Anthropic sold 313,253,007 Class N shares at $83 each. They were bought by:

- Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.
- Broadcom Inc.
- Google LLC
- Micron Technology, Inc.
- Microsoft Corporation
- NVIDIA Corporation
- TSMC Partners, Ltd.

The total price of the transaction was about $26 billion. The sale of Class N shares was not registered under the Securities Act of 1933, as amended. Combined with the IPO, including shares sold by shareholders, the private placement brought in about $119.5 billion before expenses.

BTIG, Cantor, Capital One Securities, Macquarie Capital and Societe Generale served as co-managers.

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission declared the registration statement relating to the offering effective on November 18, 2026. A related filing under Rule 462(b) of the Securities Act became effective on the day it was filed, also November 18. The shares were offered only by means of a prospectus.

Anthropic’s announcement is not an offer to sell these securities or a solicitation of an offer to buy them. The company also said the sale would not be made in any state or jurisdiction where such an offer or sale would be unlawful before registration or qualification under local securities laws.

Dataist’s take: The completed transactions give Anthropic access to a large pool of capital, but some of the announced gross IPO proceeds belong to shareholders who sold shares, not to the company. The terms of the private placement and the list of buyers show major technology and semiconductor companies participating in the transaction; those details alone do not clarify how Anthropic will use the funds.
