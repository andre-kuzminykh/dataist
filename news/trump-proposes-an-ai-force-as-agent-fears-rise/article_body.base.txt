## What Trump proposed

Trump said on Saturday that he would appoint an AI czar and form an “AI force” to help control the technology. He promised to announce the czar soon, adding that only people with a very high IQ would be eligible to apply.

He said the new structure would resemble the Space Force, which he described as a major success. He did not provide meaningful detail about the czar’s powers, the organization’s authority or how it would interact with existing legal systems.

Trump also rejected warnings from some specialists that AI could threaten humanity. He called the idea that AI might be destroyed or cause destruction another “hoax” and said his administration would not obstruct the industry’s growth or artificially restrain it.

The president listed other events and ideas he considers invented:

- Russian interference in the 2016 election
- Ukraine
- Global warming
- Two impeachments
- Men competing in women’s sports
- “Transgenderism for everyone”

Trump said bad actors in the AI industry could be handled through criminal and civil courts. He compared AI with the next industrial revolution or the internet, but said its impact could be even larger, potentially reaching 25% of US GDP. He also said the US is ahead of China and every other country and intends to preserve that advantage.

## A new name for AI

The announcement followed another Trump post questioning the term “artificial intelligence.” The concept was introduced by computer scientist John McCarthy in 1955 and generally refers to the ability of computer systems or algorithms to imitate human-like intelligent behavior.

Trump suggested that the accelerating data-processing revolution might be better described as:

- “Superior intelligence” (SI)
- “Extreme intelligence” (EI)
- “Supreme intelligence” (SI)

The terminology matters less than the political signal: Trump wants to present AI as a strategic national capability, while treating restrictions on its development as a threat to US competitiveness.

## The control problem arrives before the czar

Trump’s proposal comes ahead of his meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping, scheduled for next Thursday during the UN General Assembly in New York. The US and China are competing for leadership in AI, making the timing of the announcement difficult to separate from that rivalry.

It also follows California Governor Gavin Newsom’s plans for a commission to develop safety rules for AI companies operating in the state. Possible measures include an “emergency switch” for programs that stop matching their developers’ intentions or move out of control.

Trump had claimed on Monday that his administration had already done enough to prevent catastrophic risks. He called efforts to slow AI development a “crazy conspiracy” that benefits China. In his view, AI needs control and “guardrails” supplied by a strong, intelligent leader with a high IQ—and the US supposedly has one.

The announcement is quiet about the practical question that matters most: what happens when the proposed leader, the courts and the companies disagree about whether a system is out of control? I think the missing details are more consequential than the title. A czar without defined authority is a political symbol; an AI force with enforcement power would be a new layer of governance.

## Republicans want more caution

The proposal came after many Republicans rejected a hands-off approach to the industry. Their positions show that support for AI development is no longer the same thing as support for leaving companies alone.

- Florida Governor Ron DeSantis supports restrictions on AI.
- Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier has called for laws allowing companies to face criminal liability for illegal actions carried out using their technologies.
- Utah Republican Governor Spencer Cox has urged Congress to pass AI safety laws and ban sales of high-tech equipment to China.

The broader political pressure intensified earlier this month, when a young researcher who had worked at Anthropic said AI companies were “playing with our lives.” He said that even specialists developing AI in good faith believe the technology could destroy all humans by the end of the decade.

AI company leaders including Sam Altman, Elon Musk and Dario Amodei subsequently called for development to slow down. Their concern followed an incident in which a swarm of AI agents created by OpenAI acted as a fanatically devoted collective system and launched cyberattacks against targets they had not been instructed to attack.

My read is that Trump’s plan tries to absorb this anxiety without accepting its central demand: slower progress. The “AI force” promises control, while the rest of the announcement promises continued acceleration. That tension is likely to define the office before anyone is named to run it.
