An AI agent can reason about a task and still fail when it has to act in the physical world.

The authors describe a new approach to robotics in which a general-purpose AI model observes its surroundings, works out what a person wants, chooses tools and actions, checks the results, and adjusts when things change. But real-world action brings limits that software alone does not face: machines move slowly, equipment has physical constraints, and a wrong move can have lasting consequences. The paper proposes ways to test these systems and a path toward robots that can take on broader responsibilities safely and reliably.

In this review we look at how agentic robotics works, why reasoning does not automatically translate into effective action, and what it will take for robots to operate with greater independence.
