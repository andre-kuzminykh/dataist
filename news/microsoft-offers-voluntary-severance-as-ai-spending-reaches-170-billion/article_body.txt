## Who is eligible

The exclusions are telling: Microsoft is making room to reduce its workforce while protecting functions it considers important to its AI strategy. But the program also includes Windows, Copilot and Finance, with all employees in those divisions eligible.

The offer comes after a May program for 8,750 employees in the United States and July cuts affecting 4,800 jobs. Microsoft still has about 223,000 employees, roughly the same as in 2022, despite substantial revenue growth.

## A workforce that has barely changed

CFO Amy Hood said Microsoft plans to spend about $170 billion on AI infrastructure in 2026, slightly below its original $175 billion plan. The spending target and the severance offer point in different directions: the company is reducing headcount even as it commits enormous sums to AI.

I think the more revealing detail is not the size of the offer, but where Microsoft draws the line. Data centers, sales, cybersecurity and quantum computing are excluded; the announcement does not explain why every other eligible division is included. That leaves an open question about how Microsoft weighs current products against the teams building and supporting its AI infrastructure.
