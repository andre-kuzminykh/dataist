# Nvidia servers with Blackwell chips found in Shanghai

## What the documents say

According to documents filed with Chinese regulators and reviewed by Bloomberg, Shanghai Shuoyao offered a GB300-NVL72 rack as collateral for a 96 million yuan loan — $14 million — from Shanghai Innovation Bank. Another company, Shanghai Kezhan, listed a GB200 server installed at the same site in its loan documents.

Bloomberg identified the manufacturer of the GB300-NVL72 rack using its serial number in a public warranty database: Taiwan’s Gigabyte.

## Links to Megaspeed

According to Bloomberg, Shanghai Shuoyao is closely linked to Singapore-based Megaspeed. This month, the Donald Trump administration added Megaspeed to the US trade blacklist, suspecting the company of smuggling chips.

Alice Huang founded Megaspeed in 2023. Nvidia approved the company as a partner four days after its founding. Megaspeed later became Nvidia’s largest customer in Southeast Asia, Bloomberg reported. Alice Huang is also linked to the Shanghai data center; Bloomberg’s original report mentioned her romantic relationship with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang.

Reports say new US export restrictions and measures to prevent chip diversion are already creating difficulties for Chinese AI startups.

Dataist’s take: The documents link Blackwell shipments to companies and a site in Shanghai, but do not by themselves reveal exactly how the chips reached China. Exporting them without a US license is prohibited, and Megaspeed is on the trade blacklist on suspicion of chip smuggling.
