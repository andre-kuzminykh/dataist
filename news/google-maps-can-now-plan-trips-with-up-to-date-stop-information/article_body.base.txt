Google has announced new AI-powered features for Google Maps. With Ask Maps, users will be able to plan trips with stops, get up-to-date information about the route and find available appointment times at local businesses. The update begins rolling out on Monday for Android and iOS. At the same time, Ask Maps is expanding to 30 European countries, including Spain, Germany and the United Kingdom.

# Google Maps can now plan trips and find appointment openings

## A day-by-day trip plan

Ask Maps can create a step-by-step trip plan and show up-to-date route information while providing navigation. That could mean a short day of holiday shopping or a three-day road trip with coastal walks and dinners by the ocean. Google promises a day-by-day plan, tips from locals and numbered map markers for each stop. Users can share the finished itinerary with friends and family or export it to Google Docs on a computer.

Before setting out, users can reopen the plan through Ask Maps or find the route icon in their chat history. The service will show traffic conditions, business hours and the atmosphere at the next place. Users can change the schedule with a request—for example, by adding a scenic viewpoint or a lunch stop along the way. Directions and navigation are available for every point on the route.

## Finding available appointments and places

Ask Maps will also be able to find available appointments online at beauty salons, pet-care services, auto repair shops and home repair businesses, and call local businesses on the user's behalf. For example, users can ask it to find a manicure appointment nearby at 4:00 p.m.; the tool will show available times at nearby businesses.

The calling feature comes as technology companies show interest in AI agents that take on tasks such as booking appointments at the DMV and reserving tables. Meta's Muse AI agent previously gained the ability to call businesses on users' behalf.

Another use for Ask Maps is finding places from saved lists. For example, users can ask which of those places can seat a group of five within the next 30 minutes.

Dataist's take: Google is bringing AI assistant features into Google Maps, a service people already use to navigate and find places. The company has announced the start of the rollout and Ask Maps' expansion in Europe, but has not said how long it will take for the new features to reach all users.
