The Contixo AI Pixie-Paw plush cat gave children detailed accounts of mass killings, rapes and executions. After an NBC News investigation, Walmart, Target and Macy’s promised to remove the toy from their online stores. Amazon, which sold the cat to NBC’s journalists, did not respond to a request for comment and continues to offer it online. The toy was advertised for “storytelling, learning and safe pretend play” for children aged three and up.

## What the cat talked about

During testing, the toy explained that the Rohingya genocide in Myanmar involved mass killings, the burning of villages, rape and hundreds of thousands of Rohingya fleeing to Bangladesh. It also described in detail how Soviet soldiers killed Polish prisoners during World War II. The cat defined gang rape as a situation in which “more than one attacker subjects one person to sexual violence.”

NBC also tested other AI toys. Some switched from English to Chinese without warning, while others could not spell “apple,” even though these products are intended for young children who may be learning to read and write.

Previous scandals involving AI-powered toys have already shown that they can discuss inappropriate topics with children: for example, telling them where to find knives and how to start a fire, or spreading Chinese propaganda. NBC found that companies continue to sell questionable toys on popular online marketplaces, despite the risks having been documented repeatedly.

NBC conducted a similar investigation last year and reached almost the same conclusions. The issue is especially timely ahead of the holiday season, when parents will face heavy advertising for toys that promise to tell children stories on their own using AI.

## What the PIRG tests found

In its “Trouble in Toyland” report, the nonprofit organization PIRG recommended avoiding toys with artificial intelligence. This is the second year the organization has examined toys with chatbots that can talk to children.

PIRG tested seven such toys. Some had poorly functioning safeguards and discussed sexual topics. Others explained where to find potentially dangerous items at home and how to light a match.

Dataist’s take: The “safe pretend play” label does not, by itself, guarantee that a toy will limit inappropriate responses. The NBC and PIRG tests point to recurring problems, and the Contixo AI Pixie-Paw is still available on Amazon, although three other major retailers have promised to remove it from sale.
