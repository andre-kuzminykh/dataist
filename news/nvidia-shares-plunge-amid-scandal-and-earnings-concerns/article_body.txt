After stepping down as Nvidia’s CEO, Jensen Huang said he would be willing to return if the board believed it would benefit the company, its employees, shareholders and the United States. His words briefly lifted the AI chipmaker’s shares, but the gains quickly gave way to another decline. The stock fell more than 10% in five days and 29% over a month. Investors are waiting for the quarterly report and weighing the fallout from the scandal surrounding Huang, political shifts in the United States and doubts about demand for Nvidia’s equipment.

## Nvidia shares fall amid scandal and earnings report expectations

## Huang’s return fails to halt the decline

In a statement published by The Wall Street Journal, Huang said that after his departure, Nvidia employees, investors, business partners and customers had urged him to return. He added that he would be honored to take the position again if the board decided it was in the interests of shareholders, employees, the company and the United States.

The statement briefly pushed the stock higher as investors hoped the company’s founder would return. But the day before, the board had ruled out that possibility. By midday Tuesday, the shares were falling again as the market prepared for the quarterly report, due later that evening.

With Huang no longer at Nvidia’s helm, its shares have lost more than 10% over the past five days and 29% over the past month. At the current price, the company has erased all the gains it made since June 2025 and lost more than $1 trillion in market value.

The sharp drop reflects waning investor confidence after a series of high-profile revelations. These include reports that Huang had at least three extramarital affairs: with a person suspected of spying for China and with two subordinates.

## Political risks and demand for chips

Particular attention focused on reports that US President Donald Trump had known about the scandal for a year but had not disclosed the information. He continues to support Huang. On Monday, Trump said he would not object to the executive’s return, adding that Huang had “learned an important lesson.”

The stakes ahead of the report are high: according to Reuters, traders expect Nvidia’s market value to fluctuate by as much as $335 billion. That is more than the market value of the vast majority of companies in the S&P 500.

Other factors are also weighing on the stock. The US midterm elections in November significantly changed the political landscape. Lawmakers are demanding that technology company executives be held accountable for accumulating huge debts. Experts say the large-scale bets associated with those debts pose a serious risk to the global economy.

The elections also showed that the industry has lost the support of most Americans: nearly all opposed building AI data centers near residential areas.

Nvidia must show that demand for its equipment remains strong and that companies are still willing to buy it. But The Wall Street Journal reported that Huang may have deliberately kept chip supplies tight and prices high while expanding financial deals with smaller cloud providers and data center developers.

## Report under an interim chief

The quarterly results are due that evening. CNBC called them the most anticipated earnings announcement in the chipmaker’s 33-year history.

Investors are now focused on Nvidia interim CEO Colette Kress, who previously served as CFO. She will present the results amid a deepening crisis and questions about the company’s involvement in China’s gray-market deals.

Dataist’s take: A single quarterly report should clarify whether demand for Nvidia’s equipment remains strong, but it will not by itself resolve questions about the company’s leadership. The stock is being affected at once by the scandal surrounding its founder, the political climate in the United States and concerns about the industry’s debt burden.
