## The work around drug research

Rosalind Workbench helps researchers plan experiments and analyze results. Orchestra operates in a different layer of the drug-development process, helping biotechnology companies manage ongoing operations.

OpenAI plans to use Orchestra’s team and technology across Rosalind and the Workbench. The company has not said how the tools will be integrated or whether existing Orchestra customers will continue using them.

## A customer—or a competitor?

The acquisition fits a broader move by large AI labs, including OpenAI and Anthropic, to build software for major industries such as pharmaceuticals. That shift puts pressure on startups: an AI lab can add features that make it a direct competitor.

Here, OpenAI is taking a different route. Orchestra was not pushed out by a competing product; it is joining the company. I think the more revealing question is what this means for the boundary between Rosalind’s research tools and the operational software around drug development. OpenAI has named customers for Rosalind, but has not described how Orchestra’s products will fit into that offering.

For startups in the sector, the distinction may matter less than the outcome: a lab’s expanding product line can make it a rival, while an acquisition can bring the startup’s tools and team inside the lab.
