## Nvidia’s startup stakes rise to $65.5 billion as leadership changes

## Scale of the investments

In the first nine months of the previous fiscal year, which Nvidia reported on in November 2025, the company spent $5 billion buying stakes. In the same period of the current fiscal year, investments reached $58 billion. That figure includes deals with public and private companies, while the portfolio’s total value also increased as existing stakes rose in value.

Nvidia said in late 2023 that corporate investments should expand the AI ecosystem: the company backs startups it believes can reshape the market and create new markets. According to PitchBook, the chipmaker has invested in nearly 350 startups over almost three years.

They include Anthropic and OpenAI; newer AI labs Thinking Machines and Reflection; and cloud companies Lambda, Nscale and Crusoe. Nvidia has also invested in AI infrastructure developers, robotics startups and app makers.

## Data center financing

Nvidia has become a major source of financing for data center construction. The company reported $118 billion in guarantees, including $105 billion backing OpenAI’s $500 billion data center project in Ohio.

The company took on these commitments while directing a significant share of its free cash flow to investments. On Tuesday’s quarterly earnings call, Colette Kress said Nvidia would continue helping strong customers with financing. As the market develops and new sources of capital emerge, she said, the company will carefully assess future investments, commitments and guarantees.

## What will change after Huang’s departure

The key question for the venture market is whether Nvidia will continue backing startups at a comparable scale without Jensen Huang. His dealmaking activity reportedly caused concern for Kress. Investors told TechCrunch they expect her to take a more cautious approach: she may scrutinize new projects more closely and invest smaller amounts in companies already in the portfolio.

Kress also said on Tuesday that, in the latest quarter and going forward, Nvidia intends to return all free cash flow to shareholders after setting aside funds for strategic purposes. It is not yet clear how important startups will remain to the company under her leadership or that of a future permanent CEO. Venture investors are preparing for a significant reduction in available capital.

Dataist's take: The size of Nvidia’s portfolio alone does not guarantee that its investment policy will continue unchanged. The company has prioritized returning free cash flow to shareholders and said it will scrutinize future investments more carefully. The role of startups among its strategic priorities remains unclear, as does the approach of its future permanent leader.
