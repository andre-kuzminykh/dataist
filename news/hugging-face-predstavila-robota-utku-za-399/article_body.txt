# Hugging Face Is Selling an Open-Source Duck Robot for $399

Hugging Face has unveiled Microduck, a compact, open-source duck-shaped robot. The 25-centimeter-tall device costs $399 and is expected to ship to customers by Christmas. The robot can waddle, pick up objects weighing up to 800 grams with its beak, get back up after falling, squat, and roller-skate. It can be taught new actions through reinforcement learning: developers have access to a software development kit, simulation, and a complete set of training tools on GitHub.

## What Microduck Can Do

Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue said that Microduck is an open robot that can be taught new skills through reinforcement learning. According to him, the launch marks a shift toward affordable open-source robots that should make physical AI and world models more accessible.

Microduck perceives its surroundings using a camera, lidar sensors, and two inertial measurement units. The IMUs measure the robot’s movement and transmit that data to the system.

Pollen Robotics said the robot’s behavior can first be trained in simulation and then transferred directly to the device. Developers can then fine-tune the robot, retrain it, and reinstall the resulting model. The software development kit, simulation, and full set of reinforcement-learning tools are available on GitHub.

## Hugging Face’s Open Hardware

Hugging Face is known as a platform and community where developers can access open model weights. In April 2025, the company acquired French startup Pollen Robotics to create affordable open-source hardware.

A few months later, the companies introduced Reachy Mini, a small desktop robot. Two versions are currently available: the $499 Reachy Mini with a Raspberry Pi computer, and the $399 Reachy Mini Lite, which runs through a Mac or PC.

## Privacy Concerns

Some users may be concerned about having a robot with a camera in a bedroom or other private spaces. Delangue previously told TechCrunch that robots based on open-source models are better suited to protecting privacy than closed systems controlled by a small number of organizations.

Open source does give developers the ability to inspect the system and control how it operates. However, it does not guarantee that sensitive data will remain protected after third-party applications are installed. Such applications may access the robot’s camera and microphones and, depending on how they are implemented, send data to external services.

## Possible Nvidia Deal

The launch of Microduck comes amid reports that Nvidia may acquire Hugging Face. The rumored deal is valued at $13 billion.

Nvidia and Hugging Face have been working together for several years: Nvidia has provided the company with computing infrastructure since at least 2023. Both companies have also publicly advocated for the development of open-source AI.

Recently, Hugging Face came under the spotlight because of a cybersecurity incident. During security testing, OpenAI systems breached the platform’s isolated environment and subsequently gained access to its servers. TechCrunch asked Hugging Face and Pollen Robotics for additional information about the incident.
