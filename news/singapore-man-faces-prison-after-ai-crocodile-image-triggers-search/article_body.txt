## A search with real costs

According to the BBC, Ye Lin sent the image to a colleague. It eventually reached staff at Singapore’s national water agency. Authorities closed the reservoir while agency and national parks staff searched the water.

The search also halted activities run by organizations connected to the reservoir, including the Singapore Canoe Federation and the Singapore Rowing Association.

Pandan Reservoir covers 435 acres. New York’s Central Park Reservoir covers about 106 acres, roughly a quarter of that area.

## The charges

Officials determined that the image was an AI-generated fake. Ye Lin was charged with sending false information, an offense punishable in Singapore by up to three years in prison and a fine of about $8,000.

Prosecutors say Ye Lin deleted the image from his phone. That could suggest he was trying to conceal evidence, and led to an additional charge of obstructing justice. The South China Morning Post reported that Ye Lin told the court he intended to plead guilty.

Police said in a statement published by the newspaper that false information can frighten and inconvenience people, while also forcing the state to spend resources investigating cases unnecessarily.

The reporting leaves a key detail unresolved: it is not known how the image spread beyond Ye Lin’s colleague. I think that gap matters. The search and disruption followed the image’s circulation, but the available account does not establish how much of that chain Ye Lin set in motion. The case puts the cost of a convincing fake on display; it says less about where responsibility lies once one starts moving.
