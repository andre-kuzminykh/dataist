## What Anthropic is offering

The company plans to:

- Provide Claude, Claude Code and API credits to several hundred Genesis Mission research projects.
- Work with federal agencies and national laboratories on priority areas, including fusion energy and quantum computing.
- Train scientists, help them adopt the tools and provide technical support. For agencies newly joining the initiative, Anthropic will help launch their first projects.

Anthropic announced the commitment at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy’s “Science: A New Golden Age” summit in Washington. The company previously announced a partnership with the Department of Energy and Genesis Mission in December last year; since then, it has worked with the department to bring Claude into national laboratories.

## Part of a broader science push

The new commitment adds to several existing initiatives:

- Claude Science, launched earlier this year, is an AI workspace with tools and packages researchers commonly use.
- Anthropic has made 10,000 free and discounted Claude seats available to academic researchers.
- Its AI for Science program provides credits to researchers working on projects with high potential.
- The company has released a research preview of Model Hardware Standard, a shared specification intended to help AI agents operate laboratory instruments safely.

Anthropic describes its participation in Genesis Mission as the next step in supporting researchers worldwide. I think the more consequential test is not how many projects receive access, but whether researchers can use these tools to move work forward in practice. The announcement offers no breakdown of the $150 million or measure of what success would look like, leaving the value of the commitment difficult to assess beyond its scale.
