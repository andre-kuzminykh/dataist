# Dario Amodei: Letting AI govern society is a bad idea

## Where automation helps

Amodei drew a distinction between automating individual tasks and handing AI control of society. AI can help with driving, developing cancer drugs and writing code, but that does not mean people should give up their free will in politics.

End-to-end automation also carries risks: it could have a serious impact on employment, and AI could create new models without human oversight. At the same time, Amodei said, automation is desirable in many cases and could save many lives. But it has drawbacks, and in some areas it may not be justified. People may also prefer to do certain things themselves to stay involved in what matters to them, even if AI does a better job.

## Anthropic’s position

According to Amodei, AI companies broadly agree on automating individual tasks. Some executives talk about “human control and agency” and say that “AI should just be a tool,” while releasing products designed for increasingly broad automation.

Anthropic, by contrast, refused during its dispute with the Pentagon to allow its existing models to be used in autonomous weapons. Amodei wrote that he was prepared to put Anthropic’s future at risk, including in the face of legal penalties, rather than back down from that decision.

Dataist’s take: Amodei’s position draws a line between using AI for individual tasks and giving it the authority to make political decisions. He acknowledges that automation can save lives, while pointing to risks to employment and the lack of human oversight.
