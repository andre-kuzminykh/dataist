## Who is funding and building Supertake

The round was led by Adjacent, the venture firm founded by Nick Wittenborn. Coinbase Ventures and Union Square Ventures (USV) also participated, along with several angel investors: Nat Friedman, now head of product at Meta Superintelligence Labs; Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch; product manager Nikita Bier; investor Scott Belsky; and Nemil Dalal, a former Coinbase executive who is now a visiting partner at Y Combinator.

Michael Mignano initially developed the startup at USV. He co-founded podcasting platform Anchor and served as its CEO; Spotify acquired the company for more than $150 million in 2019. Mignano then led Spotify’s podcasting business for several years as head of talk content, before becoming a venture partner at Lightspeed and investing in AI companies including Suno and Granola. He also co-founded the educational AI startup Oboe, and joined USV as a general partner in April.

Mignano will remain Supertake’s founder and executive chairman, but day-to-day operations will be handled by the co-founding team: CEO Lincoln Murr and CTO John Peterson. Both came from Coinbase. Mignano says they led the development of AI products and the engineering team there, shipping products including Coinbase advisor, Coinbase for agents and agent wallets.

## How an idea becomes a portfolio

Supertake works with Coinbase and Robinhood and plans to add other brokerage firms over time. Users describe how they think the world will change, and the service’s AI helps refine the idea through conversation and shape it into an investment thesis—a “take”—that can be turned into a portfolio.

The service combines advanced AI models and tools with Supertake’s own software, which sets limits on what the models can do. Users’ ideas are published in a ranking on the site. Others who agree with a prediction can invest in the same portfolio.

For example, one idea in the ranking is that personal care and longevity will become more popular. The corresponding portfolio includes pharmaceutical and cosmetics companies that could benefit from that trend.

For now, the portfolios consist of stocks. The team wants to add other types of trades in the future: short positions, derivatives, perpetual contracts, long positions and prediction markets. Supertake plans to connect to the latter through partnerships with companies in the space.

[FIGURE:3]

Murr says Supertake does not consider itself a prediction market. The service, he says, is developing the idea of a robo-advisor that translates a natural-language request into a set of assets. Murr believes the rise of AI agents makes this approach to digital asset management more timely.

The service can also support short-term ideas—for example, a prediction about who will win an election. Supertake currently focuses mainly on medium- and long-term investments, but does not rule out allowing users to create very short-term strategies.

## What limits apply to AI

The team says it has put safeguards in place to manage users’ money. AI cannot deposit or withdraw funds without the account holder’s explicit consent. The service also limits how far a portfolio can deviate from its specified balance, maintains allowlists and blocklists of tradable assets, monitors suspicious activity in real time, caps trade sizes and prevents agents from viewing brokerage account credentials.

Murr compares the gradual adoption of AI in finance to the development of self-driving cars. People’s attitudes toward these systems change as the technology advances, he says, and financial AI agents have already made more progress than many people think.

Supertake is still in beta and is accepting users through a waitlist. The founders say they have entrusted the service with their own money: Murr has put in “tens of thousands” of dollars, Peterson “thousands” and Mignano “hundreds of thousands.”

The new round follows $2 million in pre-seed funding. Supertake plans to expand its team, including hiring for engineering, design and growth marketing. Mignano and Murr are in New York, while Peterson is in the Austin area and sometimes travels to New York. As the team grows, it expects to settle on a suitable hybrid work arrangement.

Dataist’s take: Supertake shifts investment decisions from choosing individual stocks to expressing a market idea, but execution still depends on the assets and constraints set by the service. For now, the product works with two brokerage firms, remains in beta and requires user consent for transactions involving funds.
