## How users can steer the feed

“Your Algorithm” lets people tell Facebook what topics and material match their interests, rather than relying only on automated recommendations. Users can revise their preferences and interact with the algorithms that rank posts and select content.

Meta has also updated the three-dot menu on posts. From there, users can open “Your Algorithm” or choose “Interested” and “Not interested” to signal how a specific post fits their interests.

Facebook may ask follow-up questions to better understand those signals. Users can explain what they want to see more or less of, and the platform says it will use those answers to adjust recommendations.

## A broader shift across Meta

The feature is part of Meta’s effort to give people more control over what they see as AI plays a growing role in content selection. It also brings Facebook closer to streaming services, where users can tune recommendations to their tastes, and away from feeds shaped mainly by systems operating in the background.

Meta has introduced similar controls elsewhere:

- Instagram added “Your Algorithm” in June and expanded it to the feed and Explore. The feature first appeared in Reels in December 2025.
- Threads launched “Dear Algorithm,” which lets users influence the content recommended to them.

The controls are more direct than the usual like-or-skip signals, but the announcement leaves a key detail unclear: how much weight a written preference will carry against the platform’s existing ranking system. I think the real test is not whether users can state what they want, but whether that choice meaningfully changes what appears in their feeds. If it does, Meta is handing users some influence over recommendation systems that have largely made those decisions for them.
