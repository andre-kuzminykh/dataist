Nvidia has formed a board committee focused on national security, export controls and international trade risks. The company announced the move after CEO Jensen Huang’s departure, amid accusations involving an alleged Chinese spy linked to one of Nvidia’s customers. The decision comes as US lawmakers scrutinize the accusations and Huang faces calls to ease restrictions on exports of advanced AI chips to China.

# Nvidia creates national security committee after Jensen Huang’s departure

## New oversight rules

Nvidia said on Tuesday that the committee will oversee national security policy, compliance with export restrictions and global trade risks. It will meet at least once a quarter. Board member Suzanne Nora Johnson has been appointed chair; Stephen K. Neal and John O. Dabiri will also serve on the committee.

Nvidia already has teams responsible for national security and compliance with international trade rules. They will now report to the committee and, on day-to-day operational matters, to Colette Kress, the company’s CFO and interim CEO since Huang’s departure last week.

## Pressure over chip exports

The day before, the US Department of Commerce announced plans to impose new restrictions on exports of AI and data center chips outside the country. The department aims to stop companies in China and other countries from circumventing existing rules by remotely using computing infrastructure in third countries. According to a source, the interim measures were a direct response to the unfolding Nvidia chip-smuggling scandal.

The committee announcement came hours before Nvidia’s third-quarter earnings call. Analysts, journalists and shareholders expected the company to comment on the situation and explain how it might affect its operations. This is the first major step Nvidia has taken under Kress’s leadership. Huang has agreed to testify before Congress later this month.

Dataist’s take: The committee strengthens board oversight of export risks at a time when regulators are focused on Nvidia. The company has not yet said how the scandal could affect its operations; the committee will need to work with the teams already responsible for these issues.
