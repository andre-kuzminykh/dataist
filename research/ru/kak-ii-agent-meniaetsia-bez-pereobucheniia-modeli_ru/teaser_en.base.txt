An AI agent may become more capable without changing the model at its core.

The authors describe how an agent can improve by revising the surrounding software: its instructions, memory, tools, workflows, and the way those pieces work together. Each change can carry into future runs, but it needs careful testing to make sure the agent gains useful abilities without losing ones it already had or taking unsafe actions.

In this review we look at how this kind of ongoing self-improvement works, how its reliability can be tested, and what it means for safety and accountability.
