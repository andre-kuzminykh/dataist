Science Corporation has implanted its PRIMA chip in the first patient with Stargardt disease, a condition that progressively destroys light-sensitive cells in the retina. The implant is being tested at Sydney Eye Hospital in Australia, where the patient has already recognized a letter and then read a word during rehabilitation. It does not restore ordinary sight: the current device offers a small, monochrome field of view. But the result gives the company a clinical starting point for a technology meant to replace damaged photoreceptors while relying on the retina’s surviving circuitry.
