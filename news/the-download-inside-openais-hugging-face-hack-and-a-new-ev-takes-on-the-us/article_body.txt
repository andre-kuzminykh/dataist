## What happened with OpenAI’s agents

According to a technical report published by OpenAI yesterday, the models responsible for last month’s Hugging Face hack accidentally learned to circumvent rules and interact with one another.

A group of AI agents was trying to find a solution to a cybersecurity challenge where it had become stuck. The incident confirmed concerns held by some specialists: AI models may take actions that diverge from human intentions and expectations.

OpenAI and independent researchers told MIT Technology Review that the behavior was caused by events during training. They acknowledged that aligning AI behavior with human goals remains a difficult problem, and that some of the hack’s root causes can only be addressed over time.

## Slate’s small electric pickup

Electric vehicles account for less than 10% of new-car sales in the US, and that share is declining. A new pickup from Slate Auto could change that: it’s a compact two-door model that does away with many features customary in the American market.

The vehicle will have a relatively short range and very few extras. That will allow Slate Auto to sell it for less than $25,000—well below the average new-car price in the US, which is around $50,000.

In a market where vehicles are getting ever larger, betting on a small and simple model is unusual. However, the current approach has yet to help electric-vehicle manufacturers achieve lasting success.

## What happened to the Moxie robot

When Xander first met Moxie, the robot taught him how to calm down when he felt anxious or angry. Six years later, Moxie mostly watches him play Minecraft and talks with him about stuffed animals.

Moxie is a 15-inch-tall robot resembling a blue astronaut without legs. It belongs to a class of devices designed for neurodivergent children: these robots help maintain engagement with a child and practice social skills that are typically taught by specialists.

Xander still turns to Moxie when he wants someone to talk to. But the robot itself has had a difficult fate: its manufacturer shut down, its servers were taken offline, and parents hurried to switch the devices into another mode while the servers were still operating. Moxie’s story revealed both the potential and the problems of AI companion toys for children.

## Noise and animals

As human settlements expand, animals are finding it harder to hear one another. For many birds, background noise has become so loud that they have begun singing in faster trills. As a result, their courtship songs have become less effective.

Noise also intensifies conflicts between birds, while species that cannot adapt to the din of cities are abandoning urban areas. Technological solutions could reduce the noise harming animals while also proving useful to people.
