Paradium AI, known as The Arena Group until its August rebrand, reported a loss of $3.6 million for the third quarter of 2026. A year earlier, it had posted net income of $6.9 million for the same period, while revenue was about 30% higher. After the results were published, the company’s shares fell about 14% in a day and traded at around $0.077. At the same time, Paradium said it had published 15% more articles with AI than in the second quarter.

# Paradium loses money as AI boosts article output

## Losses and reasons for the decline

Paradium’s shares had been falling for several years and now trade in so-called penny-stock territory, below $1 a share. The quarterly report was its first since The Arena Group changed its name to Paradium in August. The company said it was becoming a publicly traded AI company.

Paradium attributed its weak results to challenges facing digital publishers: declining traffic and problems distributing content. In particular, referrals from Google Discover fell. The company said the decline in traffic had reduced advertising revenue.

Another factor behind the loss was a $1.3 million asset write-off after the company was denied a tax credit related to employee retention.

## AI tools for publishing

During its rebrand, Paradium announced the acquisition of InfoSentience. The company’s website describes the product as a tool that turns structured data into articles in a specified style and enables AI-powered publishing at scale. Paradium also launched Cutter Studios, an AI-powered tool for editing and distributing videos.

The company’s media portfolio includes Parade, TheStreet, Athlon Sports, College Spun Media and Men’s Journal. In its quarterly report, Paradium cited increased publishing volume as an operational result: using InfoSentience and Cutter Studios, it produced 15% more articles than in the second quarter. The company also said it had launched an AI tool to create, edit and manage content in its content management system.

Paradium CEO Paul Edmondson said the company sees opportunities in the AI era and continues to invest in the technology. According to Edmondson, InfoSentience and Cutter Studios are already helping increase output and efficiency, though retaining referrals from external platforms remains a challenge.

Edmondson also mentioned recent updates to the content management system and the upcoming launch of Tablation, an AI-based task management system for working with and developing content. The company expects these tools to help it adapt to changes in the publishing business.

## The AI content controversy

Paradium’s new AI push is tied to an earlier scandal. In November 2023, a Futurism investigation found that The Arena Group had published AI-generated articles on the Sports Illustrated website under the names of fictitious authors. At the time, Sports Illustrated was the company’s flagship brand. The content came from third-party contractor AdVon Commerce, which published similar material for other partners as well.

Several executives were fired soon after the investigation, including then-CEO Ross Levinsohn. The Arena Group later lost control of the Sports Illustrated brand. The company has now changed its name, and AI-generated content has become central to its business model.

Paradium is not the only media company struggling after a major pivot to AI. BuzzFeed, a major digital content player in the 2010s, also reported weak third-quarter results: its controversial AI bet has yet to deliver the expected results.

Dataist’s take: Paradium is trying to offset declining traffic by increasing publishing volume while making AI production the foundation of its business. But the report shows that producing more articles has not yet solved its problems with advertising revenue and distribution.
