## What Alpöge claims

The Riemann hypothesis was formulated in 1859 by German mathematician Bernhard Riemann. It describes a pattern that may lie behind the apparently random distribution of prime numbers along the number line. The problem concerns how prime numbers of a given size are distributed, and has guided research in number theory for about 150 years. Many mathematicians consider it the most important unsolved problem in their field.

Alpöge announced the purported proof in a statement. He wrote that he worked on it with Claude. The mathematician also noted that the Riemann hypothesis has influenced more than number theory: its ideas are reflected in physics as well.

The claim has not yet been independently verified. Along with it, Alpöge published millions of lines of Lean code, which he says can be used to check the result. He has not yet presented a formal scientific paper; according to Alpöge, the proof runs to more than 250,000 pages.

If specialists confirm the result, it would show how AI can help solve mathematical problems that people have worked on for decades. In September, OpenAI said its advanced model had helped solve the Navier–Stokes problem. The claim sparked debate over who should receive credit for the solution.

AI’s growing role prompts mixed feelings among mathematicians, from excitement and curiosity to sadness.
