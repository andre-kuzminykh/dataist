# Daydream launches AI stylist with virtual try-ons and one-click purchases

## From outfit ideas to shopping

Daydream was founded by Julie Bornstein, who previously served as COO of Stitch Fix and created The Yes. Pinterest acquired The Yes in 2022. Daydream began operating in 2023 as an AI-powered search engine.

Before launching the agent, the startup offered a chatbot that helped users find clothes and put outfits together but did not place orders. Daydream Agent expands those capabilities: it takes individual shopping preferences into account and refines its recommendations. Users can upload a photo for the agent to use in virtually trying on saved items. They can buy a suitable item through a one-click checkout.

The agent can track price drops and new collections from brands, send weekly updates and spot emerging fashion trends. Users can save items they are interested in to a permanent “rack,” allowing them to compare options and consider a purchase without leaving them in a shopping cart.

Users can connect their own wardrobe and calendar to Daydream Agent. The agent can then suggest outfits made from clothes they already own and help them choose what to wear to upcoming events.

## How the agent works

Daydream gets up-to-date pricing and availability data from a catalog featuring more than 10,000 brands, and combines that data with AI tools for fashion. The service is built on customized versions of OpenAI, Gemini and Claude models, along with proprietary models trained for specific tasks.

The company believes choosing clothes calls for human involvement. This month, Daydream brought on its first stylist-in-residence: international stylist Francesca Salih, founder of StyleGrid. She creates style materials to help shape the agent’s outfits and advice. These materials are part of the fashion knowledge base the service draws on.

## Daydream’s scale

According to the company, since launching in June 2025, Daydream has helped more than 2 million shoppers find clothes and accessories. The startup has raised $50 million from Forerunner Ventures, Index Ventures, GV and True Ventures.

Other companies are also seeing growing interest in shopping through AI agents. Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg has said that shopping was one of the main activities of more than 10 million Muse users, and that 25% of people who set up Muse now use it daily.

Dataist’s take: Daydream turns finding clothes into a more complete shopping experience. The agent does more than suggest products: it also helps users try them on, compare them and buy them. But recommendation quality depends not only on the models and catalog, but also on the knowledge base created by stylists. So far, the company has described this approach using the example of its first stylist.
