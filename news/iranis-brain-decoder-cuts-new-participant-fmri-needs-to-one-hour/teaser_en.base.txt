A team led by neuroscientist Irena Irani has reconstructed images people saw from their fMRI scans, using a model trained to read patterns of brain activity. The method is more accurate than earlier attempts and needs about one hour of data from a new participant, rather than the roughly 40 hours other tools typically require. That could make the technique more useful for brain research—and brings the harder question of who should be allowed to infer what someone sees or imagines.
