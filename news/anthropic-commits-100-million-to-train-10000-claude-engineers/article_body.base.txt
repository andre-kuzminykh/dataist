## Training around real deployments

The academy’s first offering, the Frontier Deployed Engineer Residency, borrows a structure from medical training: learn from practitioners, work through realistic cases and demonstrate competence before qualifying.

Engineers first attend several days of in-person instruction with Anthropic specialists and licensed instructors. In a simulated large company, they select a suitable use case, complete a security review and deliver a working solution. Those who pass a practical assessment earn the Claude Resident Engineer designation and move into the residency.

Over 12 weeks, each participant leads a real Claude project at their organization, with support from Anthropic engineers and a cohort of peers. A second assessment determines who earns the Claude Frontier Deployed Engineer designation. Anthropic expects the first graduates in early 2027.

The program is for software engineers with strong technical foundations and experience building with large language models and helping colleagues adopt AI. Organizations nominate their strongest candidates, each of whom returns to a specific Claude project. Prior experience building AI agents is not required.

[INFO:steps] In-person training | Practical assessment | 12-week residency | Final assessment

## A network Anthropic wants to scale

Classes are underway in San Francisco, New York and London. Anthropic plans to expand the program to 10,000 engineers by the end of 2027. Participation is by nomination through an organization’s Anthropic customer or partner team.

The first cohorts include engineers from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey and Novo Nordisk. Their stated reasons vary, but the shared pitch is practical: train people to carry AI projects from an initial business need through deployment.

Anthropic presents the academy as an extension of its Claude Partner Network. Specialists from 46,000 companies have earned more than 175,000 Claude certificates, while nearly 4,000 people have completed Basecamp, an intensive program using the curriculum for onboarding Anthropic’s Applied AI team. The new academy is for people expected to lead deployments, not just complete training.

The scale is striking, but the announcement leaves a harder question open: how will Anthropic judge whether these engineers produce lasting business value, rather than simply more Claude projects? I think that distinction will matter more than the number of people trained. Anthropic says graduates should set a standard for business AI deployment; the test is whether organizations can point to better processes or products after the residency ends.
