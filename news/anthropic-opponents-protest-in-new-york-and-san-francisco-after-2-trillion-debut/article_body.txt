## Protests against Anthropic take place in New York and San Francisco

In New York, protesters gathered outside the Nasdaq building with flags reading “STOP THE TECH OLIGARCHS” and “FREEZE AI: END ICE AND WAR.” Representatives of local communities spoke at the rally. New York State Senator Aber Kavas said investment in AI comes directly at the expense of investment in people.

One participant, 46-year-old Jose Gonzalez, took a day off work to attend. He is a policy researcher at the nonprofit New York Communities for Change, which helped organize the protest outside Nasdaq. Gonzalez believes the country needs stricter rules for AI development before people can feel more at ease with AI research.

Protesters raised concerns about environmental safety, national security, Flock cameras, and AI’s links to US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and law enforcement agencies.

After the rally, 30-year-old Jess Vesconte urged acquaintances not to take part in buying Anthropic shares. Vesconte works at a New York employment agency and believes the pursuit of profit is tied to the housing affordability crisis and other shortages, climate problems, and ICE’s actions. In Vesconte’s view, Anthropic’s record-setting IPO makes that connection especially clear.

## Protest outside Anthropic’s headquarters

In downtown San Francisco, around 30 people from two groups gathered outside Anthropic’s headquarters before 11 a.m. One activist held a fake certificate for one share in the company “Misanthropic.”

Sidney von Arx of the group Ban RSI came to draw attention to the danger of recursive self-improvement, a process in which AI trains itself to become more capable. In her view, Anthropic’s stock market debut is a reason to consider how small companies with ambitious goals turn into corporations that put profit ahead of safety.

Von Arx supports a global ban on recursive self-improvement. She believes AI should not create other AI systems: the technology could advance too quickly for people to keep up. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has previously warned that recursive self-improvement could outpace people’s ability to control such systems.

Adam Kaufman, an employee of a nonprofit focused on AI safety, held a sign reading “The only winning move is not to play the RACE.” The letter “A” was designed to resemble Anthropic’s logo. The sign referred to the film WarGames: after running many nuclear war simulations, a computer in charge of nuclear strategy concludes that the only winning move is not to play.

Dataist’s take: The protests showed that Anthropic’s stock market debut gave activists an occasion to discuss not only the risks posed by AI models themselves, but also how business affects safety, the climate, and government policy. Protesters put forward different demands, ranging from stricter AI regulation to a global ban on recursive self-improvement.
