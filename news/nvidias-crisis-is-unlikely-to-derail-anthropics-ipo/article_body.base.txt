# Nvidia’s crisis is unlikely to derail Anthropic’s IPO

## Why Nvidia is in the spotlight

According to Anthropic’s recent S-1 filing, the company uses Nvidia GPUs in computing agreements with third parties. These include SpaceXAI, at $120.5 billion; Nscale, at $44.6 billion; and Lambda, at $41.3 billion.

The leadership crisis at Nvidia began after The New York Times published a report about Jensen Huang’s alleged “close relationship” with Alice Huang, whom the publication described as a suspected Chinese spy. Claims then emerged of an alleged affair with Kimberly Powell, an Nvidia employee who reported directly to him. On November 12, Huang announced he was stepping down as CEO. Colette Kress, Nvidia’s executive vice president and chief financial officer, took over on an interim basis and will remain in the role until a permanent successor is chosen.

After news of Huang’s departure, Nvidia shares fell more than 4% by Thursday’s close, and the company’s market value dropped by nearly $1 trillion. Huang had led Nvidia for decades, so investors are weighing whether his successor can maintain the company’s pace of growth. On November 17, however, Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion for the third quarter of fiscal 2027, up 103% from the same period a year earlier. The article cites the results as evidence of the company’s financial resilience amid the leadership crisis.

Stefan Ury, head of equity research at ODDO BHF, told CNBC that Huang’s vision and experience had largely shaped the development of the AI industry. In the view of Eldar Tuvi, CEO and co-founder of AI procurement platform Vertice, the Nvidia news came at a sensitive moment for the market: investors were already concerned about volatility in the US “AI trade.” Since Anthropic’s IPO valuation is due to be set before Thanksgiving, the uncertainty could affect the offering price.

## How dependent is Anthropic on Nvidia?

Tuvi noted that almost all AI runs on Nvidia chips, so problems at the company could affect the entire ecosystem. At the same time, procurement teams are not cutting spending. The greater risk, he said, is among companies buying AI: their finance teams are scrutinizing the cost of these solutions more closely.

Vertice estimates that Anthropic’s share of spending on AI-native tools has risen to about 38%, from around 1% a year ago. That is more than any other provider of such tools. One in five Anthropic customers now allocates at least 5% of its software budget to the company’s products; a year ago, there were no such customers. Tuvi believes this spending has become part of companies’ core work infrastructure rather than an experiment.

The diversity of Anthropic’s computing resources helps soften the IPO risks associated with Nvidia. In addition to Nvidia GPUs, the company uses AWS Trainium, AMD GPUs and Google TPUs. In the author’s view, this multi-chip architecture should ease investor concerns about dependence on a single supplier.

eMarketer analyst Jacob Bourne does not expect the Nvidia crisis to delay Anthropic’s IPO. But he believes it adds uncertainty and could affect the offering price and the stock’s subsequent performance. Bourne stressed that Nvidia remains a critical chip supplier for the AI industry, but Anthropic’s ties to Google and Amazon—companies that develop and use their own AI chips—give it alternatives.

## What could sustain investor interest

Jyoti Bansal, CEO of Harness and former head of AppDynamics, believes the situation at Nvidia does not change Anthropic’s IPO prospects and could even improve investors’ attitude toward leading AI labs. He pointed to Anthropic’s rapid revenue growth, narrowing losses and significant share of enterprise customers using its models in production systems. In his view, this revenue is easier to forecast, which matters to public-market investors.

Bansal also believes Huang’s departure could benefit Anthropic and other leading AI labs. Huang was a prominent advocate for open-weight models, which compete with the closed models sold by those labs.

Anthropic’s website says the company generated $17 billion in revenue in the third quarter of 2026, 12 times more than a year earlier. Its annualized revenue run rate reached $81 billion. Forbes also notes that Anthropic had a net loss of $23.3 billion, but concludes that neither the loss nor Nvidia’s leadership crisis appears likely to weaken the company’s IPO for now.

Dataist’s take: Nvidia’s crisis could affect investor sentiment and the offering price, but the facts in the article point to two factors that reduce this risk: Anthropic uses chips from multiple suppliers, and its enterprise customers continue to spend on the company’s products. The IPO’s timing and terms will still depend on how the market assesses the uncertainty when the offering takes place.
