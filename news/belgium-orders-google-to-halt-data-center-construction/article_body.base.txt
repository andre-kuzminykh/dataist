# Belgium orders Google to halt data center construction

## Why work was stopped

Inspectors found that construction was continuing even though the permit for the modified project had been revoked. Nicolas Ernotte, a spokesperson for the Walloon government, told Agence France-Presse that the materials would be sent to prosecutors. They describe an alleged serious violation: the unlawful construction of a building covering three hectares.

Google said it intended to maintain an open and constructive dialogue with regional authorities.

The site covers about three hectares in a 50-hectare business park. It is being built on the site of a former coal mine in the municipality of Hozée-Pré, about 15 km west of Charleroi. It is one of three data centers Google originally planned to build there.

## How the project changed

The permit Google received in 2024 covered the construction of three smaller data centers with air cooling. In 2025, the company asked to combine two of them into one larger facility and switch to liquid cooling. The plan involved drawing 600 m³ of water per hour from the nearby Sambre River.

Regional authorities initially approved the changes in April this year. But residents and protest groups filed appeals, and in August Walloon Infrastructure Minister François Desquesnes revoked the permit, citing concerns about drought. Google appealed the decision to Belgium’s Council of State. A decision is expected on January 7.

## Greenpeace complaint

The order to stop work followed a Greenpeace publication. Earlier this week, the environmental organization released a report with drone footage, claiming that Google had continued construction without the required permits. Greenpeace also filed an urgent appeal with local planning authorities.

The organization said the latest version of the building permit was revoked in August, but work continued. Greenpeace linked the dispute to climate risks and argued that the construction of large facilities with high energy consumption should not go unchecked.

The case is the latest dispute over Google data center construction in Europe, amid rising demand for computing capacity driven by the AI boom. In October, Finland opened a criminal investigation into possible environmental law violations in the preparation of a site for a Google data center in the north of the country. Work there has been suspended until environmental impact assessments are complete.

Dataist's take: The dispute shows that data center expansion plans depend on more than investment and technical choices: a project change may require new approval and face objections from residents and environmental groups. In Belgium, a final decision on Google’s appeal is still pending, while authorities have already ordered construction to stop.
