In Outshift’s tests, teams of five AI agents unanimously chose the wrong result in 75% of cases. Yet reports showed low token costs, and monitoring systems did not flag the problem: the agents reached agreement without checking whether their decision advanced the company’s goal. Outshift argues that shared memory containing company knowledge is not enough. Agents also need mechanisms to align on goals, coordinate, and verify the team’s overall result. The company introduced four tools for this as part of its Internet of Cognition project.
