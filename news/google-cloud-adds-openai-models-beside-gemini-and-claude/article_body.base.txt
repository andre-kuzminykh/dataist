## Access comes with one bill

Google says usage costs will appear on a single Google Cloud bill and count toward existing contracts. It also says customer data will not be used to train models. The OpenAI models are set to appear in Gemini for Business and Google Antigravity as well.

## The cost of hosting a rival

Google Cloud’s expanded catalogue raises a strategic question for the company: how much compute should it allocate to customers using models that compete with Gemini? Cloud revenue and Gemini’s position are not neatly aligned when the same infrastructure serves both.

Reports have linked this resource conflict to researcher Noam Shazeer’s return to OpenAI, after Google paid him two billion dollars. That account points to the stakes, but the announcement does not explain how Google will balance cloud demand against the needs of its own model team.

I think the more consequential detail is not that Google now offers OpenAI models; it is that customers can buy access through Google’s existing contracts and billing. That makes the cloud platform a more neutral route to models, even as Google remains a competitor in the same market. The unresolved tension is whether selling compute to rivals strengthens Google Cloud more than it constrains Gemini.
