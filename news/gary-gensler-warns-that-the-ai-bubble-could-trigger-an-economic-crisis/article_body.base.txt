Former U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission Chair Gary Gensler told CNN that AI investment has already created a bubble that could lead to a downturn. Markets are funding infrastructure in anticipation of future returns, he said, but after a period of growth, the technology may stop advancing at its current pace and investment may shrink. Gensler believes either a mild recession or a more serious crisis will begin before the 2028 election.

# Gary Gensler warns of an AI bubble and recession risk

## Why Gensler fears a downturn

Gensler recalled that new technologies have previously attracted large-scale infrastructure investment long before generating revenue. He cited electricity in the 1920s and the internet in the 1990s: at some point, development levels off, and a downturn follows.

Some commentators believe the technology industry can weather excessive investment in AI. Gensler, by contrast, thinks a downturn cannot be avoided. In his view, the only question is whether it will be a mild recession or a sharper crisis. It could begin before the 2028 election.

Asked what ordinary people should do with this information, Gensler compared a possible downturn to a distant storm. He would not predict when it will arrive or how severe it will be, but advised people to prepare in advance.

## Others are sounding warnings too

Gensler is not the only one concerned about the consequences of excessive dependence on AI. Earlier this week, International Monetary Fund Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva compared the global economy to a person standing on one leg — the AI leg. If that support wobbles, she said, financial stability will be at risk.

In Dataist's view, Gensler and Georgieva's warnings point to a shared risk: expectations for AI could affect the stability of markets and the global economy. Gensler, however, gives neither a precise timeline for a downturn nor an estimate of its potential scale.
