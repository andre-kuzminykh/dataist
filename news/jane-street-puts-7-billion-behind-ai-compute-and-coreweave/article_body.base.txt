## From cloud contracts to chip bets

One industry executive estimates that computing costs are now 10–20 times higher than before ChatGPT launched.

The companies are taking different routes to secure capacity:

- Hudson River’s head of AI, Iain Dunning, said the firm spends $1 billion a year on AI and has signed a multiyear agreement with CoreWeave.
- Jane Street invested $1 billion directly in CoreWeave and committed to spending $6 billion on its computing services.
- Jane Street also led an investment round in chip startup Etched and became the first buyer of its AI chip, designed to deliver higher performance at lower voltage.

## The cost of waiting

Bloomberg reports that industry participants see rising costs and rapidly obsolete chips as serious risks. Yet executives, including Jan Bomars, CEO of Amsterdam-based high-frequency trading firm Optiver, argue that doing nothing is more dangerous.

That is the central tension: capacity is expensive, and today’s hardware may not stay useful for long. I think the striking part is not simply that trading firms want more compute; it is that Jane Street is spreading its bet across both a cloud provider and a chip maker. The announcement says little about what returns would justify those commitments, or how quickly the firms expect their AI workloads to repay them. Without that, the spending signals urgency more clearly than it demonstrates an advantage.
