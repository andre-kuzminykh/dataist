“ELIZA” looks back to 1966, when computer scientist Joseph Weizenbaum built a program that imitated a psychotherapist. In Holloway’s play, the program’s simple tricks unsettle its creator: colleagues begin to treat it as if it understands them. The production turns that early AI story into surreal theatre about the danger of mistaking a machine’s responses for thought—but its people can feel less vivid than the idea they serve.
