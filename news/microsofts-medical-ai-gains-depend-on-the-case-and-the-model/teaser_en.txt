Microsoft’s MAI-DxO coordinates multiple AI agents to question patients, request tests and debate diagnoses. In a published study, the system improved some models’ accuracy on difficult cases, but the results were uneven: stronger base models often mattered more than the coordination layer, and DxO brought little benefit in emergency-department cases. The findings sharpen the question behind Microsoft’s medical-superintelligence ambitions: can AI help doctors make better decisions across real care, not just solve selected diagnostic tests?
