# Google Introduces a Planetary Prediction Engine

Google’s research team has introduced an experimental Planetary Prediction Engine (PPE) as part of the Google Earth AI initiative. In response to a natural-language request, the system independently runs the entire geospatial data-processing pipeline: it finds and cleans data, creates features, trains and validates models, and then produces a forecast and report. Across tasks involving healthcare, food security, environmental risks, and socioeconomic conditions, PPE improved accuracy relative to baseline approaches while reducing model development from several weeks of manual work to just a few minutes.

## Why PPE Is Needed

Forecasting food security by region, assessing natural-disaster risk, tracking disease outbreaks, and mapping socioeconomic vulnerability all require detailed geospatial models. Yet the data ecosystem remains fragmented: specialized teams may spend weeks manually selecting and cleaning data, creating features, and conducting spatial validation.

Existing machine-learning automation systems and AI agents based on large language models can run standard pipelines, but they work with preprepared tabular data. They lack the specialized capabilities needed to work independently with geospatial data. As a result, analysis at planetary scale remains a bottleneck, especially when a rapid response is needed during a humanitarian crisis.

Google has already demonstrated how Earth AI can use reasoning to combine different geospatial sources. PPE advances this approach: the system receives a geospatial query and independently finds suitable data, processes it, creates features, trains and evaluates models, and then produces a detailed report.

The authors tested the system on a range of benchmarks and showed that it improves results without manual intervention. As a result, building a complex planetary model can be reduced from weeks of manual data work to the few minutes the system needs to produce a finished result.

## What Is PPE?

PPE divides the data-processing and prediction pipeline into three modules, each managed by a large language model:

1. intelligent data selection;
2. dataset preparation;
3. automated machine learning and prediction generation.

Each stage operates independently, receiving predefined inputs and producing a defined output. This prevents bottlenecks from emerging in the data-processing pipeline. Data artifacts are passed between stages through opaque identifiers rather than being serialized inside requests to AI models. This approach avoids running up against context-window limitations.

## Results Across Different Tasks

Google evaluated PPE using a multidimensional matrix that included different machine-learning methods, regions, and scientific domains.

Across 21 CDC health indicators, intelligent data selection and the combination of different data types produced an average R² of 76.8%, compared with 60.0% for the manual expert pipeline. Similar improvements were achieved when forecasting national FEMA risk indicators: the average R² was 64.9%, compared with the baseline of 60.0%. For the Social Vulnerability Index, PPE achieved 66.2%, compared with 58.6% for the baseline approach.

In regions where data is scarce, reporting at a large geographic scale can conceal local vulnerability. PPE independently combines local market shocks, food-price anomalies, and microclimate indicators. As a result, the system doubled the accuracy of food-security granularity: when moving from the province or state level, ADM1, to the local administrative level, ADM2, R² increased from the baseline of 31.5% to 66.1%. Humanitarian organizations could use such vulnerability maps.

When forecasting new disease-transmission hotspots during the 2026 Bundibugyo ebolavirus outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, PPE achieved a recall of 83.3% under the Recall@10 metric. The system correctly identified 15 of the 18 new health zones into which the infection spread across five consecutive weekly forecasts.

This is 10.3 percentage points higher than the published baseline result for state-of-the-art Bayesian modeling—approximately 73%. The improvement came from combining epidemiological signals with PDFM embeddings and geospatial features selected by PPE.

## Combining Different Data Representations

Across all experiments, the value of jointly using structured statistical features and foundation-model embeddings became clear. Neither type of data fully describes a situation on its own.

Statistical features provide explicit and understandable signals. Population Dynamics embeddings and AlphaEarth Foundations encode complex nonlinear patterns learned from large datasets during pretraining. Ablation tests showed that combining different data types with intelligent source selection consistently outperformed baseline approaches. These representations complement rather than duplicate one another.

In the future, Google plans to expand the system with new geospatial sources and foundation-model embeddings, including multimodal embeddings from Remote Sensing Foundations.

## Conclusion

PPE produced comparable or better results across a broad range of geospatial tasks—from estimating the prevalence of chronic diseases in the United States to detailing food vulnerability in Nigeria and forecasting viral outbreaks in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in near real time.

The system combines intelligent data discovery, the fusion of embeddings from multimodal foundation models, and automated model optimization. This lowers the technical barrier to planetary-scale analysis and makes it possible to launch forecasts more quickly when decisions must be made under tight time constraints.

Google views PPE as an experimental step toward making geospatial forecasting more accessible. Instead of manually working with data, researchers, humanitarian organizations, and government agencies will be able to focus on formulating hypotheses and defining analytical directions without building separate engineering teams for every task.

PPE is still at an early research-project stage. Google plans to explore new use cases and investigate how this approach can help organizations assess and address complex global challenges in advance.

Google’s research team also specifically thanked the paper’s co-authors, the United Nations World Food Programme (WFP), and the Vulnerability Analysis and Mapping (VAM) team for the data and support they provided for the study. For the project on near-real-time Ebola forecasting in the DRC, the company also recognized the contribution of the National Institute for Biomedical Research (INRB). The Data Commons, Google Earth Engine, Population Dynamics Foundation Models, and AlphaEarth teams provided the data and model infrastructure.
