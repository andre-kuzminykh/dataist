# Anthropic investors profited from bets made before its IPO

## Why Notable Capital made a big bet

Notable put about 10% of the US portion of its fund, which exceeds $1 billion, into Anthropic’s Series E round. For a venture investor focused mainly on early-stage companies, it was the largest deal by valuation in its 25-year history: Anthropic was valued at $61.5 billion. Some of the fund’s investors were surprised by the size of the investment.

At the time, Anthropic was behind its main competitor, OpenAI, and DeepSeek had released its R1 model just weeks earlier, threatening to displace closed models. But the Notable team — managing partners Hans Tung and Glenn Solomon, along with director Dan Kahana — studied the data and saw developers increasingly choosing Claude over ChatGPT. By the company’s estimates, Claude was used in 70% of cases; a year earlier, the ratio had been the other way around.

Tung, a Notable co-founder, told TechCrunch that the firm considered developers key to AI’s development because they are the first to try new tools. He also said that experience navigating strained US-China relations strengthened Notable’s belief that critical tasks would be entrusted to closed models built in the United States.

After the IPO, Notable’s stake generated a paper return of about 15 times its investment. The firm was previously called GGV Capital, but separated from its Chinese business in 2024.

## How GIC invested

Singapore’s sovereign wealth fund GIC studied Anthropic throughout 2024, but made its first investment after the fund’s head of technology investments, Chris Emanuel, met Anthropic CFO Krishna Rao. They met in early 2025 at the Italian restaurant Che Fico in San Francisco. Rao impressed Emanuel by showing that improvements to the models were accompanied by increased use by businesses.

GIC first invested in the Series F round, announced in September 2025 at an Anthropic valuation of $183 billion. Claude Code launched at the same time. Emanuel told TechCrunch that the product was very new, but the fund was already seeing signs of its popularity with developers.

Later, GIC co-led the Series G round with Coatue at a company valuation of $380 billion, then became one of the co-organizers of a pre-IPO round that valued Anthropic at $965 billion. A source familiar with the fund’s finances said GIC invested more than $4.5 billion across the three rounds. After the IPO, the fund owned about 1% of Anthropic.

## What convinced Baillie Gifford

Lilian Lee, head of US growth equity investments at Baillie Gifford, studied Anthropic and spoke with its team for nearly two years before the firm’s first investment, in Series F. The firm invested after seeing Anthropic consistently deliver on its plans, grow revenue and maintain an attractive gross margin.

After the first investment, Anthropic continued developing products and entering new fields. Lee cited legal services, life sciences, Cowork and cybersecurity. She also noted that the company remained consistent despite rapid changes in the industry.

Baillie Gifford invested a total of $1.5 billion across three rounds. The investments brought the firm significant paper gains; dozens of other investors who took part in rounds at valuations once considered exceptionally high also benefited.

Dataist’s take: Anthropic’s investors were betting on a combination of developer interest, enterprise demand and the company’s ability to deliver on its promises. The IPO’s outcome shows how profitable those decisions looked on paper, but the source does not say how much profit investors realized by selling shares.
