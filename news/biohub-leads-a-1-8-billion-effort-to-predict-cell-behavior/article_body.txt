## Who gets the data first

Alex Reeves, Biohub’s head of scientific work, said commercially funded datasets will be restricted to their sponsors for one year before becoming publicly available. That restriction will not apply to data funded by the government.

The first dataset is expected in roughly a year, leaving a long gap between the program’s announcement and its first planned output. The announcement does not say how many datasets the $1.8 billion will fund or how the program will measure whether its models predict cell behavior accurately.

## A crowded field, an open question

Other AI companies are also investing in biology. Anthropic has built its own biology lab to develop drugs with AI, while the OpenAI Foundation is directing more than $125 million toward biological and medical datasets.

I think the one-year exclusivity period is the program’s defining trade-off: it gives commercial sponsors a reason to pay for data while delaying wider access. The harder question is whether a year is enough to make the investment worthwhile for sponsors without slowing the public value the program promises. That balance will matter as much as the models themselves.
