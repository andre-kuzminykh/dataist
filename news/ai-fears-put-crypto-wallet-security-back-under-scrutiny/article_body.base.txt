## A threat with no timetable

Drake wrote on X this week that the release showed mathematical superintelligence was near. Humanity, he said, was preparing to live through weeks in which centuries of mathematical progress could happen.

He urged the blockchain industry to prepare calmly and advised cryptocurrency holders to move funds to new addresses whose public keys are concealed behind a hash, a cryptographic function that acts like a digital fingerprint.

Buterin struck a more cautious tone. He does not recommend moving funds to new wallets now, but said the risks from AI-accelerated mathematical progress should be taken seriously. In his view, the industry should reduce its reliance not only on cryptography vulnerable to quantum computers, but also on cryptography that AI might eventually break. He added that, in theory, hashes might not hold up either.

[INFO:compare] Drake :: prepare now | Buterin :: no immediate move

Quantum computers are already a concern for the crypto community: experts warn that their qubit-based architecture could eventually break current encryption algorithms. Buterin allowed that AI could become a more serious threat to crypto than quantum computers.

The potential exposure is substantial. CoinDesk reports that millions of bitcoin are held at addresses whose public keys are visible to everyone on the blockchain.

## The math is still disputed

The claims about AI-driven mathematical progress have drawn skepticism. Critics say there is not yet convincing, understandable evidence for a mathematical revolution.

Valerio Capraro, an associate professor at the University of Milan, wrote on X that OpenAI was claiming revolutionary discoveries, but no one knew whether the proofs it presented were correct or supported the company’s claims.

This week, the Association for Humanistic Mathematics also rejected the claim that OpenAI’s release advances mathematics. Its representatives urged mathematicians and the public to be skeptical about the value of the model OpenAI chose for publishing its results.

I think that dispute matters more than the bunker language. The crypto risk depends on a chain of possibilities: that AI can make major mathematical advances, that those advances can break the cryptography in use, and that the threat arrives before systems can adapt. The announcement does not establish that chain.

That does not mean AI has no role in crypto attacks. CoinDesk reports that attackers have already used AI to find and exploit flaws in wallet firmware, leaving some wallets vulnerable for years. Those attacks did not crack keys through mathematical computation; they took advantage of implementation mistakes.

## Moving wallets carries its own risk

Drake and Buterin agree that the community should not act recklessly. Buterin warned that holders moving funds between addresses for added protection need to be careful. He said he had personally lost more money through failed transfers than in all hacks combined.

My guess is that the harder question is not whether holders should move today, but how they can tell when the cryptographic threat is real enough to justify moving at all. A premature migration can lose funds too; waiting too long could leave exposed addresses at risk. The claims about AI are disputed, but the cost of getting that timing wrong is not theoretical.
