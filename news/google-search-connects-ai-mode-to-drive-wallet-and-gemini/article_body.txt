Google is expanding Personal Intelligence in AI Mode in Search: it will be able to take information from Google Drive, Google Wallet and the Gemini app into account. This should make answers more personalized — for example, helping users plan for tax season or keep track of financial goals. Connecting apps is optional, and access to Wallet will be read-only: AI Mode will not be able to move or manage money. Google is also preparing an icon that will show which context the system used.

## Google Search will connect AI Mode to Drive, Wallet and Gemini

[FIGURE:0]

Personal Intelligence links answers to information from Google apps. The feature already works with Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Photos. Google is adding Drive, Wallet and Gemini, drawing on data users store in its services.

Connecting apps is optional: users can turn Personal Intelligence on or off at any time. For Google Wallet, Google specifies that the system will be able to read account information but will not be able to manage money.

Google Wallet is intended to help users track financial goals, cut everyday spending and make better use of credit card rewards.

In Google Drive, information about projects and important decisions is often spread across documents and spreadsheets. Connecting the storage service will let AI Mode take those files into account when answering — for example, when putting together a personalized plan for tax season.

Context will be transferable between the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. If a user discussed an idea in Gemini, they will not have to start over in Search: AI Mode will be able to continue using the information already available. The transfer will work in the other direction, too.

Google also said an icon labeled “Personalization” will soon appear below answers. Users will be able to use it to see the context AI Mode relied on.

Separately, the company announced that answers in Google AI Overviews and AI Mode in Search will begin taking the user’s recent search history into account.

Dataist’s take: These new connections make Google Search answers more useful by drawing on data from personal files and financial services. Wallet access is limited to reading, and users can choose not to connect the apps or disconnect them at any time.
