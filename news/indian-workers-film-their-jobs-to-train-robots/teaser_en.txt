Indian workers are being paid to film the movements that robotics companies cannot get from simulation alone. In Bengaluru, a barber records his hands at work; elsewhere, people film sewing, cooking and household chores. The footage is collected and prepared for AI companies and research labs training robots for the physical world. For workers, the money can help cover household expenses. For the industry, the recordings are raw material—and a reminder that building robots still depends on people doing ordinary jobs.
