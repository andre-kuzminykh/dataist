An AI agent can learn from another agent’s hard-won success—but only if it can separate the useful know-how from the special setup that made it work.

The authors propose a way to turn successful task-solving attempts into reusable instructions for an AI model. One part extracts practical steps, another checks that the instructions do not rely on hidden answers or tools that will not be available later, and a final part tests them in a fresh environment. This lets the model learn from successes gathered under different setups and carry that experience into a more general one.

In this review we look at how recursive rewriting turns a small collection of successful attempts into a much broader set of training examples, and why that helps an AI agent tackle more challenging tasks.
