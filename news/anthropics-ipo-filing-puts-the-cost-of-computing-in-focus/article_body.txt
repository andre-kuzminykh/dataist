## The cost of computing

Over the past 12 months, Anthropic has struck a series of major deals for computing capacity. Those commitments have worried investors, and a speculative-grade rating could make it harder for the company to raise funds.

The company officially filed to go public earlier this week, prompting speculation about what a large IPO could mean for the AI industry. But the credit-rating forecast is only one possible reading of Anthropic’s finances; the source gives no rating-agency assessment or details of the deals.

## What the forecast leaves open

I think the more revealing question is how Anthropic plans to finance its computing commitments after listing. The announcement offers no figures for the cost of those deals, and no indication of how much capital the company expects to raise in an IPO. Without those numbers, Blackstone’s forecast signals a potential financing constraint, not its likely scale.

If Anthropic does receive a high speculative-grade rating, the tension will be between the computing capacity it has committed to secure and the cost of funding it. The IPO may bring new capital, but it will not by itself make those commitments cheaper.
