An AI agent can learn from the past and still remember the wrong things for the job at hand.

Rather than turning each finished task into a fixed note, the authors keep the full record and select what matters only when a new task arrives. The agent then shapes those past experiences into a concise guide for its current goal, so useful details are not discarded before anyone knows when they might help. Tests across household, shopping, and computer-use tasks show that this just-in-time approach helps agents succeed more often than existing memory methods.

In this review we look at how task-specific memory is assembled, why choosing what to remember later can work better, and what the results reveal about helping AI agents learn from experience.
