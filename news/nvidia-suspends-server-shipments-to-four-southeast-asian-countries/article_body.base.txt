# Nvidia suspends server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries

## Why the US tightened the rules

The Financial Times reported the suspension of shipments to the four countries, citing three people familiar with the situation. It is unclear whether the measure is temporary. Nvidia partners instructed to halt server shipments include Dell and Supermicro.

The restrictions were imposed by the US Department of Commerce’s Bureau of Industry and Security. They are intended to close a loophole in existing sales bans: Chinese companies could rent computing capacity from overseas cloud providers.

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told CNBC that the authorities want to block this indirect access, including smuggling through Southeast Asia and remote use of data centers in third countries.

## B300 chips seek new buyers

The new rules have already affected the hardware market. According to CNBC, sellers put an unusually large number of Nvidia GPUs up for sale after the restrictions were announced. Jack Duhamel, president of GPU Mart, a marketplace for AI infrastructure, and two other sources confirmed to the outlet that the increase in supply was linked to the new rules.

Sellers are looking for buyers for equipment that includes thousands of B300 chips, one of Nvidia’s most advanced models. The processors were originally intended for data centers that rent computing capacity to Chinese clients.

## Cloud providers have 90 days

Cloud providers in Southeast Asia must apply for a license within 90 days and confirm that none of their end users are companies subject to the restrictions. According to the Financial Times, an executive at one cloud provider called the situation chaotic: companies have not been told how to submit applications.

The fallout has also affected major infrastructure projects. Banks have suspended about $500 million in loans to Singaporean cloud provider Aolani. The company planned to rent computing capacity to ByteDance, TikTok’s parent company. According to the Financial Times, ByteDance may now walk away from the deal.

Dataist's take: The new restrictions affect two parts of the same arrangement: server shipments to Southeast Asia and Chinese companies’ rental of computing capacity. But the licensing process has not yet been clarified, according to the cloud provider’s executive, and it is unclear whether the pause in Nvidia server shipments is temporary or permanent.
