# Scott Aaronson: More important than a proof of the Riemann hypothesis is that AI can produce one

## What OpenAI’s proofs show

Aaronson examines an OpenAI proof related to matrix multiplication. The question is how quickly computational costs grow as matrices get larger. The new result establishes a lower bound that matches an already-known fast algorithm. It shows how far computational costs can be reduced within the model OpenAI studied.

The company obtained a similar result for the Fourier transform, a basic mathematical tool for processing sound, images and signals. Researchers found a faster way to compute the transform and proved that it cannot be made substantially faster.

Such proofs of limits are called lower bounds. A new algorithm may simply set a speed record, while a lower bound shows where further improvements run up against a hard limit. Aaronson believes these results give mathematicians not just individual answers to problems in complexity theory, but an entirely new body of mathematical truth that was previously only partly visible.

The work also changed his assessment of the field’s main open question: the P versus NP problem. It asks whether every problem whose answer a computer can check quickly can also be solved quickly. Most researchers assume it cannot, but there is no proof yet. Aaronson now believes for the first time that a proof of P ≠ NP may be near. It would confirm that there are problems whose solutions are easy for a computer to check but hard to find.

## Possible breakthroughs in cryptography

Aaronson says that, based on several reliable sources, AI labs have “one or more major discoveries” in cryptography. He does not yet know the details. In October, Aaronson wrote that AI labs were testing whether their models could break cryptographic systems.

The Riemann hypothesis itself is unlikely to affect everyday life, Aaronson writes. Ideas from this kind of research may lead to new applications in science and engineering, but it is not yet clear what they might be. He believes AI’s ability to do this kind of work could have enormous consequences for everyday life, from transforming the global economy to threatening all life on Earth. Aaronson says people disagree about the scale of these consequences, but urges them not to dismiss AI’s mathematical achievements simply because a proof of the Riemann hypothesis would not help with immediate practical problems.

Dataist’s take: OpenAI’s results matter both as mathematical bounds and as evidence of what AI systems can do. But the claim that a proof of P ≠ NP may be near is Aaronson’s personal assessment, and he has not yet provided details to substantiate reports of cryptographic discoveries.
