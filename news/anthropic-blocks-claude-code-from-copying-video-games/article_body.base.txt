## A rule aimed at more than labels

The new restriction follows a wave of projects made with Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra. Tools such as Reverse Engineer Anything combine AI agents with reverse engineering, turning machine code into human-readable pseudocode.

The same appetite for rapid reconstruction has reached beyond games:

- One developer recreated parts of Adobe Creative Cloud apps.
- After the PlayStation 5 was hacked, a wave of emulators appeared quickly.

The issue is no longer only whether a generated image or video resembles protected work. Software can be taken apart, translated into a form people can inspect, and rebuilt. Anthropic’s rule targets the act of copying, including attempts to disguise a copy with a new name.

## Where the boundary lands

Anthropic spokesperson Osmani said the company would continue changing its rules. The announcement does not explain how Claude Code distinguishes a copy from an original project, or how users can challenge a restriction they believe was applied incorrectly.

I think that uncertainty is the harder problem than the prohibition itself. A rule against copying is clear in principle; applying it to code can mean blocking legitimate work alongside imitations. If reports from users with original projects continue, Anthropic will have to show that its boundary is more than a ban that catches the easiest cases.
