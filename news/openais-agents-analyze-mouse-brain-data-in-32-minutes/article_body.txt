## One gene per agent

Each agent examined the effects of a single gene change, assessing how cells responded and searching scientific literature for explanations. The researchers say this distributed approach produced reports that specialists preferred over a single-agent report in 75% of blind comparisons.

The resulting “Reasoning Atlas” is available on GitHub, and an accompanying paper has been published on bioRxiv. OpenAI says the findings could help researchers decide which mechanisms to investigate first in the lab.

[INFO:facts] 32 minutes :: analysis time | 75% :: preferred reports

## Speed is not validation

I think the more consequential claim here is not that agents can compress months of analysis into minutes, but that their output may help set the next laboratory priorities. That is a useful role for AI—but the announcement does not say whether the proposed mechanisms have since been tested experimentally.

The 75% preference result is encouraging, but it measures specialists’ choices between reports, not whether the suggested biology is correct. The gap between a faster analysis and a validated discovery remains the important one.
