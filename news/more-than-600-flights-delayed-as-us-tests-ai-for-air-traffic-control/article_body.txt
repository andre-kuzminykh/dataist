## What failed

Early reports pointed to a failure in air traffic control infrastructure at one of the region’s major airports, but the details conflicted.

- Bloomberg TV reported a faulty electrical circuit at an air traffic control facility in Philadelphia.
- Reuters reported that a fiber-optic cable was accidentally damaged in New Jersey.
- Boston’s Logan Airport warned passengers of major delays caused by broader “equipment problems in the Northeast,” according to the BBC.
- A Federal Aviation Administration representative told CBS21 that the departure stoppage was caused by problems with specific frequencies at Philadelphia TRACON.

TRACON, or Terminal Radar Approach Control, directs aircraft departing from airports and approaching them for landing. Restrictions on departures appeared to spread north from Philadelphia, affecting aircraft that had been preparing to leave.

The precise cause remained unknown at publication time. What is already clear is that the region’s air traffic control system is interconnected enough for a local failure to become a multi-airport problem.

## SMART arrives at the worst moment

The disruption came as federal authorities began a limited rollout of Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes and Trajectories, or SMART. Bloomberg reported that the system started providing AI-generated recommendations to employees responsible for managing aircraft movement near Washington this week.

The reported rollout includes several East Coast airports, including:

- Reagan National Airport in Virginia.
- Baltimore/Washington International Thurgood Marshall Airport in Maryland.

It is not known which facilities have officially been connected to the AI tool.

There is no evidence in the early reports that SMART caused the flight stoppages. Treating the timing as proof would be a mistake. But the coincidence exposes the harder issue: an AI layer is being added while the underlying infrastructure remains difficult to diagnose and vulnerable to failures that can cross airport boundaries.

My read is that the immediate risk is less about an AI system directly taking control than about introducing recommendations into a system whose data, communications and operating conditions are already unstable. The announcement is quiet about the safeguards around SMART, including how staff are expected to handle bad recommendations and how the system is isolated from failures elsewhere.

The US air traffic control system is in serious condition. The public has not yet had to test a federal system against an AI assistant that can make mistakes and invent information. That gap between limited deployment and demonstrated resilience is the tension behind this rollout.
