# Unsiloed AI raises $7.5 million to process documents for AI agents

## How Unsiloed processes documents

Business documents often spread data across tables, charts and text. AI systems can struggle to extract it accurately, and mistakes can be costly for companies. The stakes are especially high for banks, hospitals and law firms that use these documents in their day-to-day work.

Unsiloed breaks documents into parts, extracts information from tables, charts and other elements, and produces structured data for AI agents. The system keeps links to the original locations in each document, allowing employees to check the extracted information rather than take the model’s answer on trust.

Unsiloed co-founder and CEO Aman Mishra told TechCrunch that large language models work probabilistically, so they can stray from the correct answer and hallucinate. He said such errors can lead to serious consequences in subsequent stages of work.

Mishra founded Unsiloed with Adnan Abbas, a former classmate at the Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur. After developing the technology, they joined Y Combinator’s fall 2025 batch.

## Early customers and competitors

Unsiloed is already working with customers in finance, legal services and healthcare. One bank uses it to process documents, while a law firm has deployed the system on its own servers to work with contracts and old court cases. Mishra did not disclose the customers’ names.

Among the customers he named is Bolna AI, a startup developing voice AI systems. It uses Unsiloed to upload knowledge bases that it then uses to build voice agents.

The San Francisco company is entering a competitive market. Specialized document-processing software developers include Reducto and Unstructured; Microsoft and Amazon also offer their own products. Companies can also extract information using general-purpose AI models.

## Technology and test results

Mishra says Unsiloed’s advantage lies in its ability to process complex documents. The company uses custom “dual-stream” computer vision models: one stream handles text, while the other processes images. Heatmap-based segmentation helps identify the most important parts of a document.

The system then directs more computing resources to elements such as figures, numbers and charts. A specialized decoder accounts for industry terminology and helps distinguish words whose meaning changes with the business context.

Mishra shared results from a test on 542 financial documents provided by an enterprise customer. According to a technical description shared by the startup, Unsiloed achieved 99.78% accuracy across approximately 84,000 extracted data points. The test compared the system with large AI models, Microsoft Azure Document Intelligence and the customer’s internal tool. The results have not been independently verified.

Unsiloed also claims to outperform competitors on olmOCR-Bench, an open benchmark developed by Allen AI. In the startup’s own tests, its system scored 88.0; Reducto’s agentic system scored 66.0, and Unstructured scored 39.9. The benchmark included 1,403 PDF pages and tested the processing of tables, mathematical expressions, scans and complex layouts.

## Pricing and plans

Mishra says Unsiloed’s pricing starts at one cent per page. He says selective processing can reduce costs by directing computing resources primarily to the parts that need more attention.

The startup currently has seven employees. It plans to use the new investment to hire researchers and engineers, expand sales and build proprietary datasets to improve its technology.

For Unsiloed, extraction accuracy and the ability to verify data sources are key selling points in a market where customers can choose between specialized products and general-purpose models.
