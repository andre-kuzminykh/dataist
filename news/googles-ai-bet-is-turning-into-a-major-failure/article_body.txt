Google is expanding AI’s role in search, but that could weaken its advertising business. When a ready-made answer appears in an AI Overview, users have little reason to scroll down to the links, where ads are placed. Advertisers are also reassessing their spending: potential customers are spending time in Google’s AI Mode instead of visiting companies’ websites.

## Google’s AI answers cut search ad revenue

“We may be seeing the biggest change in search user behavior,” Valerie Schlosser, head of marketing at the agency VML, told The New York Times. Marketer Marshall Evans, who helps small businesses place ads in Google Search, told the newspaper that AI now takes up a significant share of search results.

## Advertisers shift their spending

Evans said one of his clients used to spend $2,000 a month on search ads and now spends only $500. The client is redirecting the freed-up money to advertising on streaming services, including Tubi, and on platforms such as Reddit. The issue, the article emphasizes, is not that businesses have stopped paying for ads: advertisers simply do not want to invest in a search environment where AI is taking up more and more space.

Thomas Monteiro, a senior analyst at Investing.com, believes Google is trying to turn a business that was the best in the world for a decade into a different one, with different advantages. In his assessment, making that transition has proved harder than the company’s leadership expected.

## Search’s share of revenue is falling

In Google’s latest report, for the third quarter, search advertising brought in less than half of the company’s quarterly revenue for the first time, at $128 billion, The New York Times reports. Search revenue growth also slowed: annual growth fell from 14.5% to 12%.

This happened despite Google saying that its search engine handles more queries than ever. The company’s chief commercial officer, Philipp Schindler, attributed the slowdown to “lower monetization that we are seeing in AI Mode.”

Dataist’s take: Google is bringing AI into search faster than it is finding ways to preserve the returns its ads used to generate. The company itself has linked slower revenue growth to lower monetization in AI Mode; the source provides no data on how long this trend will last.
