The startup Pedestal AI has emerged from stealth with $4.7 million in seed funding. It develops AI agents to make supply chains faster and more reliable, and is already working with Fortune 500 companies and major carriers. In one project, the system helped cut missed deliveries by 20%: agents called customers 30 minutes before the driver arrived, asked about special instructions and sent a message if they could not reach them.

# Pedestal AI raises $4.7 million to automate logistics

## How the funding will be used

Eclipse led the round. RRE, Converge VC, Timescale Ventures and VitalStage also participated. Pedestal plans to expand its product development and engineering teams, grow its marketing department and support new deployments.

The company uses AI agents to remove obstacles from the delivery process. In a project for a large furniture company, agents took over some tasks that drivers were expected to handle. The system called customers 30 minutes before delivery to collect special instructions. If the call did not go through, a separate system sent a text message.

During the deployment, Pedestal gathered data on problem areas in the delivery process. The company expects to use it to improve the system further.

Pedestal CEO Raj Aggarwal says today’s AI systems can work with this kind of data, even though it changes constantly, is stored in different formats and is spread across many legacy systems.

## Startups move into supply chains

Interest in AI for supply chains has picked up in recent months, as former members of autonomous-driving teams launch their own companies. Atomic, a startup founded by former Tesla employees, emerged from stealth in 2025 and focused on inventory optimization. Another venture, Loop, was launched by former Uber employees and works on predictive analytics.

Aggarwal says delivery is well suited to training reinforcement-learning systems: the volume of operations is high, and the results are easy to measure. This makes it possible to tune AI systems to use approaches that work more often and abandon ineffective ones.

A successful deployment can directly affect a customer’s financial results, making it easier for companies to justify spending on the technology. Aggarwal believes a properly configured system can have a significant impact on a business.

Dataist’s take: Pedestal is building its product around a measurable outcome — the number of missed deliveries — and collecting data to improve the process. However, the source does not say how much the deployment at the furniture company cost or how the project’s results affected other customers’ operations.
