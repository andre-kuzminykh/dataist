The Crown Prosecution Service cited a nonexistent court case while arguing that an 11-year-old boy should face criminal prosecution over the alleged rape of his seven-year-old sister. The fake precedent appeared in detailed arguments by Ravinder Dhillon, a senior prosecutor in the CPS unit for rape and other serious sexual offences. The High Court said the citation did not affect the outcome of the challenge to the decision to prosecute. But the case raises a more consequential issue: how an error generated during legal research made it into arguments by the state.
