The UK’s National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) has reported a notable rise in cyberattacks using AI. Since September, the agency has recorded several incidents involving autonomous hacking tools that led to intrusions into organizations’ networks and service outages. The vulnerabilities were known before, but the tools can now find and exploit them faster. The NCSC says many attacks could be prevented with basic security measures: the problem often stems from years of accumulated technical debt.

# AI has accelerated cyberattacks, exposing old security problems

## How the tools work

Attacks use autonomous penetration-testing tools: software that security professionals use to test how resilient systems are. These tools can search for systems accessible from the internet, try weak or simple passwords, and exploit API endpoints through which developers access data.

The rise in attacks has coincided with the wider spread of open-source tools, including Artex and Cairn. They are free to download and use. The US FBI has also warned that malicious actors use AI-powered penetration-testing tools to carry out cyberattacks at unprecedented speed.

## Old weaknesses, found faster

According to the NCSC, attackers are not yet using new memory-corruption vulnerabilities, nor are they limiting themselves to known vulnerabilities for which no patch has been released. Instead, they are exploiting security weaknesses that went unaddressed for years, partly because of accumulated technical debt.

AI has sped up the search for these weaknesses and their exploitation: issues that went unnoticed for years can now be found in minutes. The NCSC stresses that the new tools do not make familiar cybersecurity measures obsolete. On the contrary, strong basic security has become more important.

Dataist's take: AI is not creating the weaknesses described by the NCSC; it is shortening the time attackers need to exploit them. That makes regular checks of internet-accessible systems, passwords, and APIs especially important: these are among the targets listed in the attacks.
