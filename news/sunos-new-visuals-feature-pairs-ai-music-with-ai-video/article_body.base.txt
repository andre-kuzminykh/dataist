# Suno adds video generation to AI music

## How Visuals works

To create a clip, users can upload a reference photo, write a prompt or choose one of Suno’s ready-made characters. In lyric videos—clips featuring song lyrics—users can change fonts and colors, extend clips on the timeline, and choose the duration and aspect ratio. There are two quality settings: Fast and Standard.

Filters block prompts and uploads that mention specific artists, celebrities or copyrighted works. Before content is published outside Suno, the platform adds an embedded watermark.

Visuals is available on Suno’s website, as well as on iOS and Android. It requires separate credits. Pro and Premier subscribers will temporarily receive credits to try the feature.

## Legal disputes involving Suno

At the end of July, the Munich I Regional Court ruled against Suno in a lawsuit brought by GEMA, a German music rights organization. The court found that Suno had infringed copyright. Soon after, Suno announced the launch of tools that let users label songs created with the service as AI-generated on other platforms.

The company has reached an agreement with Warner Music, while Universal and Sony continue their legal disputes.

Dataist’s take: Visuals expands Suno from a music generator into a tool for creating videos to accompany songs. The feature also restricts the use of artists’ names and copyrighted works, and content published outside the platform is marked with a watermark.
