## A framework, not just a voice stack

Pipecat orchestrates real-time voice and video pipelines, helping reduce latency between components. Daily provides hosted infrastructure for voice and video calls. Deepgram says the combination will let developers build expressive agents and full-stack speech systems using different models and a context bus that links system components.

The open-source commitment appears central to the deal. Daily co-founder Kwindla Kramer said the company received many acquisition offers, but was persuaded by Deepgram CEO Scott Stephenson’s promise to keep Pipecat open permanently, as well as the companies’ similar engineering approaches. He said Daily’s team would be able to work across more parts of the technology stack inside a larger organization.

That promise also fits Deepgram’s stated approach to models: developers can choose Deepgram’s products or alternatives that better suit their needs. If Deepgram’s offerings lag in an area, Stephenson said, the company is willing to work with partners while improving its own products.

## A bet on real-time software

Kramer said Daily began experimenting with voice-and-text interaction between people and AI around the release of GPT-4. Those experiments led to Pipecat. He described the framework not as a set of voice tools, but as infrastructure for a new kind of software built for real-time interaction.

That broader ambition is reflected in Deepgram’s launch of the Real-Time Context Standard, or RCS, an open protocol developed with the Pipecat team. It defines metadata for voice agents, including audio context such as whispering, instructions for how a language model should account for those signals, and guidance on how a response should sound.

Deepgram has been looking to work with open-source software, Stephenson said, and sees agent orchestration as a promising direction. The purchase gives it a way to explore formats beyond voice through Pipecat’s support for real-time communication.

## The value of staying open

The deal arrives amid fresh investment in voice AI. Daily raised more than $62 million from investors including Tiger Global, Highfield Capital, Nat Friedman, Lachy Groom and Kevin Weil, according to PitchBook. Deepgram became a unicorn earlier this year after a $130 million Series C. The companies were both part of the same Y Combinator batch in 2016.

Other companies are also drawing funding: ElevenLabs, Gradium and Fish Audio have raised millions of dollars, while LiveKit and Vapi received multimillion-dollar investments this year.

I think the interesting test is whether Deepgram can own more of the infrastructure without making Pipecat less useful to developers who choose competing models. Kramer’s argument is that openness can be a commercial advantage: a company at the center of a growing ecosystem benefits when other participants can develop freely. That principle is now part of what Deepgram has bought—and what it will have to preserve.
