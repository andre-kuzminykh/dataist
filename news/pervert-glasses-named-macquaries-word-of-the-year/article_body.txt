“Pervert glasses” named Macquarie Dictionary’s word of the year

Australia’s Macquarie Dictionary has named “pervert glasses” its word of the year for 2026. The phrase refers to smart glasses with built-in cameras, microphones and AI assistants, including Meta Ray-Ban. Their use has raised concerns about people being filmed without their consent: some swimming pools and playgrounds in Australia have already banned the devices. The phrase also won the readers’ vote. Other contenders included “nudify,” AI used to create pornography, and “AI; didn’t read,” a play on the English phrase “AI; didn’t read.”

The language experts on the committee called “pervert glasses” a concise reflection of 2026. In their assessment, the name points both to the rapid development of AI and technologies built into devices, and to calls to restrict that development. Smart glasses may be marketed as camera glasses, but the committee said the chosen phrase better captures the concerns surrounding them.

Among the words recognized by the committee was <strong>PMOS</strong>, short for “polyglandular metabolic ovarian syndrome.” The term has gained visibility as information spreads about discrimination against women in medicine.

Another recognized word was <strong>agentic</strong>. It describes the ability, especially in artificial intelligence, to make decisions independently.

<strong>“Marrickville pause”</strong> is a colloquial term for a temporary break in conversation. It refers to pauses made by people who live under Sydney’s flight path.

The readers’ vote recognized the phrase <strong>“digital dementia”</strong>, used for short-term memory problems attributed to dependence on technology and AI. The committee stressed that it is not a real medical diagnosis, although fears about excessive dependence on digital devices are widespread. Experts say people can find it difficult to get around without Google Maps and to plan or remember things without iCal. The committee is interested in whether handing such tasks over to technology will lead to a long-term decline in cognitive abilities.

The shortlist also included “nudify,” a term for pornography created by AI, and “AI; didn’t read,” a shorthand label for material generated by AI but not read by a person.

Last year, the committee chose “AI slop” as the word that best reflected language trends in 2025. Experts said then that the word of the year should reflect an important event or social change from the year.

The winning words are selected from new articles added to Macquarie Dictionary’s annual online update. The readers’ choice is determined by votes submitted on the dictionary’s website.

Dataist’s take: The choice of “pervert glasses” shows that discussion of AI increasingly concerns not just what devices can do, but also consent to being filmed and privacy. “Digital dementia,” meanwhile, remains a public concern rather than a medical diagnosis.
