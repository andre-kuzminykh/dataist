## Controls outside the model

In his post, Nadella said it was time to stop and assess the architecture of trust in AI. He used “superintelligence,” a term preferred by the Trump administration, to describe AI.

His proposal has three parts:

- Separate the model from the wrapper that organizes its work.
- Keep controls and safeguards external to the model.
- Log every significant action in a tamper-resistant record that humans can understand.
- Give an authorized person the ability to pause or shut down the model during a task.

Nadella spoke amid reports from leading companies of more frequent cases in which they appeared to lose control of their models. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei had earlier published a plan for more cautious AI development.

## The missing test

I think the most consequential part is not the shutdown switch but the demand for a legible record of what the model did. A control is only useful if people can see when it should be used; Nadella’s proposal puts that visibility alongside the ability to intervene.

What the post does not specify is how to decide which actions count as significant, or who gets authority to stop a model. Those details determine whether an emergency brake is a practical safeguard or a reassuring metaphor. The architecture Nadella describes makes oversight possible in principle; its value will depend on whether the people responsible can understand the record and act in time.
