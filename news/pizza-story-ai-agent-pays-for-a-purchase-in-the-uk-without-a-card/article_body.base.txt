# AI agent pays for an order in the UK without a bank card for the first time

## How the payment worked

TrueLayer, a London-based company that enables cardless payments between bank accounts, called this the first time in the UK that an AI agent had paid a merchant for a real customer.

The team told the agent why they were ordering and about their dietary requirements. It suggested flavours and the number of pizzas, then people confirmed the order. The Papa John’s agent was authorised to access the bank account and request a transfer to the merchant’s account.

For the trial, Lloyds set strict limits: a maximum amount for a single purchase and a monthly cap. The agent was not given the account’s security details.

TrueLayer CEO Francesco Simoneschi told BBC Radio 4 that the payment was made without sharing an account number or password, while maintaining a high level of security.

## Purchases and responsibility

Simoneschi called the trial a first step towards widespread use of a technology that, in his view, could change shopping habits. People already turn to AI to put together shopping lists; next year, he suggested, agents may start buying gifts on users’ behalf. Eventually, they could shop, manage finances and help people understand complex products and compare them—as assistants available to millions of consumers.

Users will still need to make conscious decisions about which purchases to delegate to agents. Simoneschi said their work needs to be monitored, and the technology tested to ensure it is fit for purpose and meets high security standards.

If an agent makes a mistake—for example, ordering the wrong product—existing consumer protection rules and regulations will protect customers, Simoneschi said.

Earlier that week, the FCA warned companies in the financial sector that AI agents must comply with existing requirements. The regulatory sandbox trials, Simoneschi explained, are intended to study the technology before it becomes widespread.

## Other ways to pay

Meta’s personal AI assistant Muse, launched in the UK on Tuesday, can also make purchases online. Unlike TrueLayer, Muse processes payments using the user’s bank card.

Dataist’s take: the trial shows how an AI agent can initiate a payment without receiving the user’s banking details. But this scenario still requires spending limits, human oversight and compliance with existing consumer protection rules.
