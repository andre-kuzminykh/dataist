## A network loses visibility

Futurism reported that Brown Brothers Media’s sites had often appeared near the top of Google News’s “For you” tab. The network had bought publications including VegOut, Silicon Canals and Space Daily, which were once considered trustworthy. Futurism said the sites ran long, emotionally charged headlines and repeated versions of the same story, crowding out original journalism.

About two weeks after the investigation appeared, Futurism observed that the sites had vanished from Google News and become difficult to find through search. Even queries using the publications’ exact names returned social media pages, discussions elsewhere and unrelated organizations with similar names.

A site-specific Google search showed only about ten pages indexed across VegOut’s domain. The remaining results included static pages such as its privacy policy and advertising section, rather than published articles. Futurism reported a similar pattern for Silicon Canals and Space Daily.

The apparent restrictions extended across much of the portfolio, from spirituality publication Hack Spirit to Europe-focused Scandinavian Standard. MakeTechEasier and Ideapod, a social network turned digital publisher, remained visible.

## Publishing stops

The sites’ publishing schedules also changed after the investigation:

- Science Blog’s last article appeared the previous Saturday.
- Silicon Canals had published nothing new since the previous Monday.
- VegOut stopped publishing and appeared to remove all articles published after May.
- Ideapod updated an older article that week but had published nothing new since Sunday.

Futurism had contacted Google while preparing its investigation. Google responded with a general statement, but the publication saw no lasting consequences for the network at the time. After the investigation, Futurism sought comment from Google and Brown Brothers Media again and received no response.

Futurism’s separate follow-up also reported that one of Brown Brothers Media’s founders had long-standing ties to Ghislaine Maxwell, the imprisoned accomplice of Jeffrey Epstein.

## What the pause does not explain

I think the striking part is not simply that publishing stopped, but that the company has offered no explanation for the apparent changes. It remains unknown whether Brown Brothers Media has paused operations temporarily or shut down for good, or whether it runs other sites that have not been publicly identified.

The corporate homepage still says the company publishes and manages editorial projects read by millions each month, across science, technology, food, space and psychology. It says each publication has its own editors and voice, with a shared commitment to clear writing, sources and treating readers as adults.

That description now sits beside a network whose articles have become difficult to find and whose publishing has largely stopped. The gap between the company’s stated editorial standards and the reported pattern of output is the unresolved story.
