# Nvidia may have restricted chip availability to keep prices high

## Excess inventory and customer financing

The situation described took shape several months before Huang was accused of having an affair with an alleged Chinese spy. According to The Wall Street Journal, demand for Nvidia hardware remained high, but even major cloud providers could no longer afford to buy it and began looking for alternatives. At the same time, the company was facing an “avalanche” of chip wafers and memory, the main components of its graphics cards.

In response, Huang reportedly expanded financing deals with smaller cloud providers and data-center developers. Nvidia hoped this would bring in new loyal customers and reduce its growing inventory of hardware.

The reports gave rise to the theory that the company may have artificially inflated chip prices even as supply increased. This approach is called “artificial scarcity”: companies with monopoly pricing power restrict product availability, make it harder for competitors to enter the market, and increase profits. The practice is considered legally questionable. In Nvidia’s case, it may have created the impression of a chip shortage and thereby justified extremely high prices.

AI industry critic Ed Zitron previously claimed that about half of Nvidia’s chips remain in warehouses. In his estimate, the hardware is worth hundreds of billions of dollars.

## Money flows back to Nvidia

The discussion of inventory also concerns another industry problem: circular financing. Under this arrangement, money is invested in ways that ultimately bring it back to Nvidia, which in turn helps drive up the company’s share price. Economists are concerned about this practice.

Nvidia has long been accused of maintaining a monopoly: major cloud companies are eager to get its AI chips, while buyers have few alternatives. The company has made substantial profits selling hardware amid the AI boom.

But the scandal surrounding Huang’s personal life and his subsequent resignation could affect the status quo. The US Congress is already seeking hearings with the billionaire early next year. They could draw more attention to Nvidia’s business practices.

After Huang announced that he was leaving the company, effective immediately, shares of Nvidia’s largest competitors rose last week. This points to investors’ desire to spread their investments across different companies.

West Monroe chief AI officer Bret Greenstein told Canadian Press that the changes could turn a de facto monopoly into a contest among companies competing for growing demand. Nvidia did not create that demand, he said; a shift in the balance of power could diversify supply chains, expand choice for buyers, and strengthen the AI industry.

Dataist’s take: The reports described here call into question not only Nvidia’s pricing, but also the resilience of a market in which a hardware supplier also helps finance its customers. But claims of possible artificial scarcity remain speculation for now, and upcoming congressional hearings could shed light on the company’s business practices.
