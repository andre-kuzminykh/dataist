On Tuesday, AI company leaders gathered around President Donald Trump and congratulated one another on signing an AI safety agreement. My colleague Maddie Warner has examined its terms; the short version is that the AI labs are largely expected to watch themselves. The next day, Trump put the arrangement more bluntly in the Oval Office: companies would monitor themselves, monitor one another and police themselves—and, he said, everything would be fine. That is the current shape of AI safety policy: an agreement built around voluntary restraint, offered as the technology’s economic and political stakes keep rising.
