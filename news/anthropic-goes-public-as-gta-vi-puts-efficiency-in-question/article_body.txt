# GTA VI and Anthropic’s IPO showed the cost of efficiency

GTA VI was eagerly awaited beyond the gaming audience. Even startup founders planned breaks around the game. Dima Beseda, co-founder of Spiry, told TechCrunch that he redistributed his workload to free up his weekends and even signed up for intravenous vitamin drips to keep his energy up. For some players, the wait was also bound up with nostalgia: they pulled out their dusty Xbox consoles to play a game they had dreamed about since childhood.

Ben Feder, managing partner at TIRTA Ventures and former CEO of Take-Two Interactive, Rockstar’s parent company, believes GTA may be the last game of its kind made this way. In his view, this approach to development has reached its natural limit.

## A decade of hands-on work

GTA VI was made without generative AI. Its world includes 30,000 non-player characters with distinct appearances and personalities. Large open-world games typically use procedural generation for landscapes and objects, but Rockstar crafted the world’s details by hand. The result looks so realistic that gameplay can be hard to distinguish from footage of real life.

This approach was set against the AI industry’s promise to automate work and free people from time spent on experiments that lead nowhere. Instead, as the author describes it, a user enters a prompt into a large language model and gets an answer.

The article also points to consequences of this kind of automation: children see repetitive, meaningless content on YouTube, lawyers cite nonexistent cases in court, and Meta laid off tens of thousands of employees as part of the company’s declared “year of efficiency.”

## Game generation and the debate over creativity

The gaming industry is already discussing how AI will affect development. Google’s Project Genie, launched earlier this year, lets users create three-dimensional worlds from prompts to a large language model and explore them. Around the time the project launched, shares in several gaming companies fell. The author links this to possible investor uncertainty about future demand for game developers.

Take-Two Interactive CEO Strauss Zelnick called the idea that a new tool could let one person press a button, create a hit, promote it and bring it to millions of people around the world untenable. He said nothing like that had ever happened in the entertainment industry.

On Meta’s earnings call this year, Mark Zuckerberg described a different expectation: soon, people will have AI that understands them and creates high-quality personalized content for them. Elon Musk suggested that AI could make a game on the level of GTA VI and understand on its own which video game a user would like.

In the week Anthropic went public, GTA VI set records as the highest-rated game and the most successful game launch. Its first-day sales exceeded the box office for Avengers: Endgame — $2.9 billion — and Taylor Swift’s Eras Tour — $2 billion. For the article’s author, the coincidence prompted the question of whether Anthropic’s IPO signaled the end of an era in game development and, more broadly, in art.

## Why creativity needs inefficiency

Feder believes works of art, including video games, often require a slow process. Getting close to a masterpiece means deleting and reworking a lot; for a complex game, that is expensive and, by definition, inefficient.

The freedom to make mistakes, try different approaches and return to them allowed the developers of GTA VI to expand and refine the game, even when that pushed its release back twice. This is precisely the kind of process AI companies, including Anthropic, are trying to make more efficient.

In the author’s assessment, the game shows the mark of human work: every small detail looks intentional. That helped GTA VI attract a broad audience and create a shared cultural moment — a rarity in a media landscape where people are increasingly surrounded by personalized social media recommendations.

The game also makes players slow down. Early missions involve running errands and installing drywall to please the protagonist’s girlfriend, while the game still has car chases and shootouts. This pace may be irritating or seem like a good choice, depending on the player’s sense of humor. The game is 212 GB, and the wait for a sequel lasted more than three presidential terms. In the author’s view, the main pleasure here is not only playing a criminal, but also being able to take one’s time, explore a vast world and notice its details. The game has found an audience even among people who dislike violent video games.
