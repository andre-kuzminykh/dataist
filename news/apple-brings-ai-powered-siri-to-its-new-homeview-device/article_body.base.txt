## A wider home lineup

HomeView is the centerpiece, but Apple is also tying more hardware into its Home app.

- LG’s lineup includes indoor and outdoor cameras, a doorbell camera, a floodlight camera and a smart lock.
- Schneider Electric has made a smart thermostat with Apple’s involvement.
- The LG devices are set up and controlled through Apple’s Home app; users can unlock the lock with it.

The launch lands amid competition from Amazon, which introduced premium tablets last week, and from AI companies such as OpenAI, which are also planning to release their own devices.

## Siri is the hinge

HomeView’s software story is an updated Siri powered by Apple Intelligence. Apple says the assistant will be more capable and conversational, using information from personal apps such as Messages and Calendar to give more precise answers. The company also says Siri can draw on personal context from an iPhone while keeping personal data private and secure.

That ambition meets a familiar weakness. Apple launched the Home app in 2016 and introduced HomePod speakers in 2018, but analysts have pointed to two obstacles: compatibility with third-party devices and Siri’s limited ability to provide useful answers. A better assistant could make Apple’s existing home hardware more useful, but the announcement does not show how much better Siri will be in practice.

HomeView will not be sold in the European Union. Apple says AI-powered Siri is not yet available there because of EU legal requirements.

I think the more consequential test is not whether Siri can answer questions from personal apps, but whether it can reliably control a home that includes devices from different manufacturers. Apple’s announcement adds more products to that ecosystem, yet leaves the hardest question unanswered: whether its updated assistant can make the whole system work together.
