## The factory comes first

Toyota’s competition includes Tesla and Xpeng, while Hyundai plans to begin producing its Atlas robot in the United States in 2028. Toyota has not given a comparable production date in the information available here.

Starting at its own factories is a practical choice. Controlled conditions may make deployment easier, but they also leave the central question unanswered: whether the systems can do useful work at a cost that justifies the investment.

## Spending is not proof

Investors are treating the plans cautiously, pointing to Toyota’s earlier efforts in hydrogen cars and solid-state batteries, which did not meet expectations. That history does not establish that the robotics plan will fall short, but it gives investors reason to distinguish a large budget from a demonstrated result.

I think the most telling omission is a concrete account of what Toyota’s robots will do on the factory floor. Without tasks, milestones or a deployment timeline, $6.4 billion a year signals commitment, not evidence that humanoids can become productive machinery.
