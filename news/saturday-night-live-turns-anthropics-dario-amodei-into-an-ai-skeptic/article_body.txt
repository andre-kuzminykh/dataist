## The joke is the contradiction

Che opened by saying Amodei had made an uncertain impression on his press tour and seemed to agree with a former employee’s claim that AI could destroy humanity. Che also stumbled over the pronunciation of the Anthropic leader’s surname.

Ashley Padilla wore an oversized wig and delivered her lines with long pauses. Her Amodei periodically argued with her own dark side, in a Gollum-like exchange. At one point, she called AI the devil and herself its creator.

The sketch’s invented Amodei then said AI executives shared one view: they did not approve of what they were doing.

“AI isn’t a weapon, it’s a tool for making weapons,” she said, urging viewers to urge her to stop.

## A ten-year cancer joke

When the sketch turned to possible benefits such as curing cancer, its Amodei offered a bleakly specific estimate: in ten years, there would be about a 10% chance that cancer would no longer be a problem for anyone.

I think the sharpest part of the sketch is that it makes the distance between warning and remedy the punchline. It gives the audience no policy proposal or technical argument to assess; instead, it stages the discomfort of leaders who describe grave risks while continuing to build the technology.

What the segment leaves out is whether the joke lands on Amodei’s particular claims or on the broader spectacle of AI executives warning about their own industry. That distinction matters: the first is a critique of one leader, the second of a recurring tension in how the industry talks about its work.
