Daniel van Strien of Hugging Face compared eight accessible AI models for recognizing text in images. Over 18 months, the cost of processing 1,000 pages fell from about 51 cents to less than 10 cents, while digitizing a million pages costs $65–130 in GPU rental, depending on the material. The model is not the only factor affecting the cost: optimizing the setup can save just as much or more.

# Digitizing a million pages with AI now costs from $65

## What was tested

Van Strien tested the models on 850 pages, including old natural history books, the 1771 edition of the Encyclopaedia Britannica and archival documents with printed text. All eight models ran on the same Nvidia RTX PRO 6000 GPU, which the researcher rented through Hugging Face for $2.75 an hour.

The comparison used images of the same size. The oldest model, RolmOCR, released in April 2025, cost about 51 cents per 1,000 pages. The newest, LightOnOCR-3-0.8B, released in October 2026, cost just under 10 cents.

## Setup choices also affect the cost

According to van Strien, optimizing how the models ran saved about as much as switching to newer models, and possibly more. The changes included distributing pages more evenly across processing, using smaller scans, updating software and running the models on a faster GPU.

The new model recognized book pages at least as well as the old one. However, some models hallucinated text on blank pages or repeated the same passage many times.

The cost of processing 1,000 pages fell from about 51 cents with RolmOCR to less than 10 cents with LightOnOCR-3-0.8B. The five models in between were nearly the same price.

Dataist's take: The cost reduction reflects both advances in models and how their runs are organized, so both factors matter when choosing a solution. A low price alone does not guarantee accurate results: some models added text to blank pages or repeated what they had recognized.
