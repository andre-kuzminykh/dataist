## How the shipments were routed

According to the Justice Department, Louis, also known as “Yu Kong Lui,” ran technology company Earthmade Computer. From 2023 to 2024, the company purchased and shipped goods to China without licenses required by the US Commerce Department.

The servers were first sent to countries including Malaysia and Singapore, then illegally re-exported to China, the Justice Department said.

Bill Essayli, the US attorney for the Central District of California, said protecting national security means preventing advanced supercomputing technologies from strengthening the military capabilities of US adversaries. He added that authorities would seek convictions against people who put national security at risk for profit.

## The gap between controls and enforcement

The case lands amid a broader US effort to slow China’s development of advanced AI systems by restricting access to the chips used to build them. China was long considered behind the US in advanced AI, but is closing the gap quickly. The restrictions block access to the most powerful US semiconductors, including Nvidia chips.

I think the striking detail is the route: the alleged scheme used countries where licenses were not required as an intermediate step, rather than sending the servers directly to China. The announcement does not specify what kinds of servers were involved or whether they contained restricted chips. Without those details, the $300 million figure establishes the scale of the shipments, but not how directly they connect to the chip controls at the center of US policy.
