# Dropbox buys Tigris Data for $350 million to build AI infrastructure

## What DBX Agent Cloud will offer

At launch, DBX Agent Cloud will include object storage as a standalone product. Companies will be able to buy it separately from other services and use it with existing tools and applications for Amazon S3, a cloud service that lets users store and retrieve large amounts of data.

Dropbox is also adding AI capabilities to its core product. Users will be able to assign supported file-related tasks to AI, choose from different models, and connect Dropbox content to third-party tools, including ChatGPT, Claude, and Muse.

Over time, the company plans to teach DBX Agent Cloud to work with different types of content: documents, images, audio, and video. The platform will also gain file system features so AI agents can manage files and maintain continuity across tasks and sessions.

More than 70,000 teams are already using the updated version of Dropbox. It is initially available to eligible Standard and Advanced plan customers in the US, UK, and Australia; a broader rollout is planned for 2027.

## What Tigris Data does

Tigris Data was founded by former Uber engineers who helped build the company’s data storage infrastructure. Tigris technology lets users store and access data across different regions. The platform automatically places data closer to the users and computing resources that need it, reducing access times and improving performance.

Last year, Tigris Data raised $25 million in a Series A funding round.

The acquisition of Tigris could be the largest in Dropbox’s history, according to TechCrunch. The company has previously acquired several AI-related businesses: Reclaim.ai, Promoted.ai, and Mobius Labs.

The deal connects the acquisition of an infrastructure company with the launch of a standalone storage product for developers of AI models and agents. For now, though, Dropbox has described the initial product as object storage, while additional capabilities for handling different types of data and managing files are still planned.
