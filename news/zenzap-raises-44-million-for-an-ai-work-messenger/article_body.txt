## A founder reluctant to raise

The funding is a first for founder Weiss. He launched e-commerce marketing platform StuccoMedia with his own money in 2012 and sold it to Market Group in 2015, then spent several years investing. He founded Zenzap in 2022 with co-founders Noam Neumann and Jonathan Bien after meeting Bessemer partner Adam Fisher.

Weiss initially paid for development himself and worked from Bessemer’s office. He told TechCrunch he had not wanted to raise money: after succeeding with his first company, he was afraid of failing on a second attempt. He said he did not need to work for money, so he began developing the idea with his own funds. Fisher persuaded him that Zenzap would need venture capital to scale.

## Built for workers outside the office

Zenzap’s intended market spans hospitality, construction, healthcare and fast-food chains. Weiss argues that WhatsApp and iMessage do not meet corporate IT requirements for workplace use with AI agents. Slack and Microsoft Teams, he says, are not suited to the everyday mobile tasks of frontline workers.

The product combines messaging with workplace tools:

- The free plan includes group and direct chats, task management, calendar and schedule connections, voice chat and AI search.
- Organization plans start at $51 a month for a team of up to 20 people. They add voice and video calls, automated task sorting, task templates and higher file-storage limits.
- Paid plans also include AI search across the workspace and the ability to turn messages into tasks.
- AI agents for workflow automation, plus transcription of voice notes and calls, are charged through a credit system.

Zenzap lists FedEx, Dollar General, Taco Bell, Starbucks, Casey’s and Regal as customers. Weiss said workers at some restaurant chains, including IHOP and McDonald’s, use the platform to coordinate shifts and discuss deliveries.

The company has 50 employees in the United States, Tel Aviv and Europe. It plans to hire soon for product development and research and development.

## Bessemer’s bet on a broader workforce

Bessemer partner Adam Fisher says Zenzap’s broad target audience could work in its favor. He said Bessemer’s investment team uses the messenger to discuss deals and manage deal flow, which he sees as evidence that it can serve different kinds of users.

Fisher contrasted Slack’s popularity among developers with Zenzap’s focus on other employees: people who use consumer messaging apps and want their own AI agent. He also argued that workers need a clear boundary between work and personal life, a need made more pressing as both personal and workplace AI agents spread.

<strong>My question is whether bundling chat, tasks and AI is enough to make a new workplace inbox stick.</strong> Zenzap has named recognizable customers and outlined a wide feature set, but the announcement gives no figures for adoption or usage. That leaves the central tension intact: the workers the company wants to reach may need a better work tool, but they still have to choose to use one.
