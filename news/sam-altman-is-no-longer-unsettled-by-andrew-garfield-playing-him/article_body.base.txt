Sam Altman said a film about him no longer surprises him: in his words, plenty of things in his life are stranger. Andrew Garfield will play the OpenAI chief in the biographical comedy-drama “Artificial,” due in theaters at Christmas. The film will cover the company’s development and the internal conflict that led to Altman’s brief ouster in 2023.

## Sam Altman isn’t surprised Andrew Garfield is playing him

Hollywood has repeatedly turned to Silicon Valley’s scandals and power struggles in recent years. The documentary “You Can See Everything” about Elizabeth Holmes exceeded box-office expectations, and the miniseries “The Altruists,” about Sam Bankman-Fried and the collapse of FTX, premieres on Netflix on Thursday.

Altman spoke about the upcoming film on Wednesday at The New York Times’ DealBook Summit. He said that 20 years ago, the idea of a film about him would have seemed very strange, but now it wouldn’t even make the top 10 strangest things in his life.

Garfield is known for playing Spider-Man, and his breakthrough role was as Facebook co-founder Eduardo Saverin in the 2010 film “The Social Network.” Jesse Eisenberg played Mark Zuckerberg in that film. Released this year, “The Social Reckoning,” which could be considered a sequel to “The Social Network,” flopped at the box office.

## What Altman finds strange

Altman described being responsible for developing a technology that many people have serious concerns about as an unusual situation. He said he and others found themselves in a position where they had to make sure the technology led to a good outcome.

TechCrunch also mentions other peculiarities in Altman’s life: he uses ChatGPT for parenting advice and has little regard for olive oil. But the author finds it even stranger that the upcoming film is based on Altman’s brief ouster by colleagues who were concerned about whether he could be trusted.

## How Zuckerberg reacted to a film about himself

Mark Zuckerberg had a different take on “The Social Network.” On a podcast last year, he said the film got some details right, such as his clothes, but misrepresented his motives and the overall story. In the film, his character is trying to find a girlfriend, even though Zuckerberg was dating Priscilla Chan before Facebook was founded.

Zuckerberg expected employees would want to see the film and arranged a group trip to the theater, even though his portrayal was unflattering. Later, he bought at auction the Ardsley High School T-shirt Eisenberg wore in the film and wore it himself in a video with YouTubers Colin and Samir.

Eisenberg said he did not want to think of himself as connected to someone like Zuckerberg. “Artificial” has not come out yet, but Garfield has also spoken critically about the tech executive he plays. In an interview with Esquire, the actor said he did not know Altman, but suggested that anyone taking on such responsibility for a company or technology capable of seriously damaging the human soul should be able to distance themselves from what is happening.

Dataist’s take: The film will arrive amid open debate over who is responsible for the consequences of AI development. It is not yet clear how “Artificial” will portray the reasons for Altman’s ouster and the internal conflict at OpenAI: the film has not been released, and its plot is known only in broad outline.
