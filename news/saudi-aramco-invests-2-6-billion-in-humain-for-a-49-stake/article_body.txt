## From capacity plans to customers

Humain’s business is already expanding beyond a long-term buildout plan. In late August, AMD said infrastructure developed with Cisco and Humain was serving customers both inside and outside Saudi Arabia.

Aramco also has an existing foothold in AI hardware: through its investment arm, Wa’ed Ventures, it owns a stake in South Korean AI-chip developer Rebellions.

The investment gives Aramco two routes into AI: using Humain’s technology internally and selling its own AI-based solutions. The announcement, however, does not say how much revenue either route might generate.

## Infrastructure under pressure

The regional conflict is also shaping how Humain builds. In September, CEO Tareq Amin told Semafor the company was strengthening data-center protection, drawing on Aramco’s experience securing oil infrastructure. He said demand for Humain’s services had not fallen despite the war.

I think that resilience is central to the deal’s logic: the 3 GW target matters only if the infrastructure can be built and kept operating. What I’d want to know is how much of that capacity is already committed to customers. Without that figure, the investment signals confidence in Humain’s ambition, but not yet the commercial scale behind it.
