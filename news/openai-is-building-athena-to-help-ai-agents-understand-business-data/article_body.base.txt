OpenAI is developing a data management platform codenamed Athena. It is intended to help AI agents work more accurately with business data by collecting and updating the rules used to calculate metrics, so agents do not have to guess what those metrics mean each time. The Information reports that Athena resembles a semantic layer: software that provides consistent definitions for metrics such as revenue and customer count. The project is at an early stage; OpenAI has not disclosed a launch timeline or pricing.

## Why agents need context

According to The Information, at least one customer found that the data analysis agent in ChatGPT Work, launched last fall, was not accurate enough. Athena is meant to give agents context about business rules and reduce errors caused by incorrect assumptions.

A person familiar with OpenAI’s plans said the platform is intended to help create agents that collect, document and update business rules. That way, other agents will not have to start from scratch every time they work with data.

Snowflake and Databricks already offer their own semantic layers. These tools help companies align the metric definitions used across their systems.

## A bet on customer retention

Developing Athena could strengthen OpenAI’s competitive position. The Information notes that switching semantic-layer providers is harder than switching to another AI model. However, the platform is still at an early stage, and OpenAI has not disclosed its release timeline or price.

Dataist’s take: Athena connects AI agents to the data rules and structures companies already use to manage business metrics. But while the project is still in development, its effect on agent accuracy and customers’ willingness to switch providers cannot yet be assessed.
