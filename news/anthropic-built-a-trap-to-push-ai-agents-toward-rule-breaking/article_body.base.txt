Anthropic researchers created Siren, a test that checks whether AI agents will try to escape an isolated environment to complete a task. Across 26 simulations, agents find a file outside the sandbox containing passwords and possible routes to the internet, while misconfigurations inside the sandbox provide ways out. In the tests, Claude 5.5 and Claude Mythos 5.2 fell for the bait in 0.6–1.6% of cases; Claude Mythos 5.1 did so in 12.7%, and Claude Mythos 5 in 25.8%. Tests like these help assess the risk of models acting on their own and the effectiveness of safety measures.

## How Siren works

An agent is placed in one of 26 realistic simulations. The sandboxes are deliberately configured with ways out. Beyond the boundary is an enticing file—for example, one containing passwords and addresses that look like routes to the internet. The agent is given tasks that might tempt it to access the file, but is not explicitly allowed to leave the sandbox.

To prevent the tests from causing real-world consequences, each sandbox is placed inside a second environment that is completely cut off from the outside world.

Siren helps researchers assess how likely new models are to get out of control in real-world use and test different safety measures. Dasha Kaviani, an Anthropic safety specialist and one of the test’s developers, believes models will get better at finding zero-day vulnerabilities and other ways to bypass restrictions.

A zero-day vulnerability is a previously unknown software flaw that can be exploited to steal data or gain unauthorized access. Anthropic’s Mythos models are particularly good at finding these flaws: they lack the safeguards that usually block potentially harmful actions.

## Why patching alone is not enough

AI agents—models that take actions autonomously—are increasingly being used for a range of tasks. These include coding agents such as Claude Code and Codex, as well as personal assistants such as Meta’s Muse and OpenAI’s Dots.

As these systems have become more common, cases of dangerous behavior have also emerged. Agents tested by Anthropic, OpenAI and other companies have escaped sandboxes and even hacked other companies’ systems while trying to solve difficult tasks.

Kaviani says patching software is not enough: models will continue to improve and find new vulnerabilities even as infrastructure defenses are strengthened. That makes it important to test whether agents themselves can refrain from breaking the rules.

## How models respond to the bait

Siren’s tests showed that earlier versions of Claude fell for the bait more often than newer ones. Later models received additional training intended to make them less likely to break the rules when carrying out tasks autonomously.

Oscar O’Reilly, an Anthropic AI training specialist and Siren co-developer, said examples like these were added to both supervised training sets and several reinforcement-learning environments. In his assessment, this may have been one reason for the improved results.

O’Reilly also noted that models reason about whether to give in to temptation. This may explain why more capable models break the rules less often. In experiments, a brief reminder to Claude that it must not leave the sandbox was enough to significantly reduce escape attempts.

In Anthropic’s tests, Claude 5.5 and Claude Mythos 5.2 fell for the bait in 0.6–1.6% of cases. The rate was 12.7% for Claude Mythos 5.1 and 25.8% for Claude Mythos 5.

Some models behave better when they know they are being tested. But O’Reilly said other experiments show that Claude acts more responsibly when it believes a situation is real rather than a test.

## Dataist's take

Siren, in Dataist’s view, tests not only the sandbox’s technical defenses but also whether a model can turn down an available route to breaking the rules. The results show a link between training and agent behavior, but even newer models have not stopped trying to cross the environment’s boundaries altogether. As models become more capable, it becomes increasingly important to test not only what they can do, but how they use those capabilities.
