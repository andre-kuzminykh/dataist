After Jensen Huang returned as Nvidia’s chief executive, the company’s shares, which had risen more than 30% in a day, fell nearly 9% in midday trading on Tuesday. Nvidia’s market value had climbed by more than $900 billion the previous day. Anthropic also fell about 12%, to $77.19 — below last week’s IPO price of $83. Investors are again worried about high volatility and questioning whether the huge investments in AI will pay off.

## Tech stocks fall again after Nvidia rally

## The market retreats after a rally

By midday in New York, the tech-heavy Nasdaq was down 2.3%, while the broader S&P 500 had fallen 1.1%. The Dow Jones, by contrast, edged higher. Major European stock markets ended the session slightly lower after rising in line with Wall Street the previous day. Europe has fewer tech companies than the markets in Asia and the US.

Aside from Monday’s rally, shares in companies tied to AI fell sharply throughout November. Investors are concerned about the profitability of these businesses and the enormous sums invested in the sector.

## Huang’s return did not resolve the questions

Huang left the post less than two weeks ago over extramarital relationships — reportedly with someone believed to be a Chinese spy, as well as a senior Nvidia employee. Donald Trump then publicly and insistently called for Huang to return. The day after he was reinstated, Nvidia shares rose more than 30%.

Charu Chanana, investment strategist at Saxo Markets, believes Huang’s return removed one of the main uncertainties surrounding AI investment. He not only leads Nvidia but is also one of the industry’s leading representatives, she said; his relationships with customers, suppliers and sources of capital are difficult to replicate.

But Kathleen Brooks, director of research at XTB, said Huang alone would not be enough to sustain investor interest in AI for long. She also said Nvidia’s share price decline on Tuesday was to be expected after the sharp rise the day before.

Analysts broadly agree that Huang’s return does not bring the investigations to a close or remove the uncertainty that has weighed on the AI sector for six months. Nvidia remains under close scrutiny from investigators, including the FBI, and the US Congress. The company is suspected of helping Chinese firms gain access to its advanced chips.

Dataist's take: Huang’s return helped Nvidia surge, but it did not resolve questions about the investigations or whether AI investments will pay off. A leadership decision alone was therefore not enough to keep the stock rally going.
