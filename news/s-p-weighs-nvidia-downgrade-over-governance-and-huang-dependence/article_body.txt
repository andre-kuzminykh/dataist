# S&P reviews Nvidia rating over governance concerns

## Why S&P is reviewing its assessment

S&P Global Ratings, one of the three largest credit rating agencies, has pointed to risks related to Nvidia’s governance. Although the company’s financial results remain strong, Huang’s departure as CEO and subsequent return have raised questions about executive oversight and succession planning.

The agency also says Nvidia’s prospects depend on Huang: if the company’s success is largely determined by one person, that creates additional risk. S&P expects to complete its review within 90 days. A possible downgrade from AA to AA- would be a one-notch reduction; AA- is the agency’s fourth-highest rating.

## How the rating could affect borrowing

Even after a possible downgrade, Nvidia is expected to remain investment grade. However, its borrowing costs could rise. Nvidia also supports neocloud providers and other infrastructure companies that lack strong credit ratings. If Nvidia’s rating falls, their debt could become more expensive, and lenders could demand more collateral.

In August, Nvidia announced plans to arrange $500 billion in AI infrastructure financing alongside six financial institutions. They include Goldman Sachs, Apollo and Blackstone. Under the initial terms, Nvidia was to guarantee 25% of the loans.

Even before Huang’s departure and return, lenders were demanding that Nvidia increase its share of the guarantees, Bloomberg reported. If the company’s rating is downgraded, lenders are likely to ask it to take on even greater obligations.

Dataist’s take: Nvidia’s financial results do not resolve questions about how dependent its governance is on one executive. A possible downgrade could affect not only the company’s borrowing costs but also the financing terms for infrastructure projects it supports.
