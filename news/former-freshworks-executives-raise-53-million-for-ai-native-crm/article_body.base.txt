Dreamteam, founded by former Freshworks executives, has raised $53 million to compete with Salesforce and HubSpot in the CRM market. The startup is betting on automatically collecting customer data from emails and conversations, as well as AI agents that prepare for meetings, handle follow-ups and track deals. The funding comprises a seed round and a Series A, led by Together Fund and Accel, respectively, with Premji Invest also participating. Dreamteam did not disclose the size of either round or its valuation.

# Dreamteam raises $53 million for an AI-agent CRM

## How Dreamteam's CRM works

Traditional CRMs require sales teams to manually log customer interactions and update deal information. As a result, records can be incomplete or out of date. Dreamteam gathers context from emails, customer conversations and other sources, while its AI agents handle some routine tasks.

Dreamteam co-founder and CEO Anand Venkatraman told TechCrunch that in more than 25 years working with enterprise software, including at Salesforce and Freshworks, he had repeatedly seen the same problem: salespeople spent a lot of time updating records, yet managers still struggled to get an accurate picture of the business. Venkatraman said he had never once been able to get a forecast from a CRM with the click of a button.

Venkatraman founded Dreamteam in 2025 with two former Freshworks employees:

- Senthil Kandaswamy spent more than nine years at Freshworks, most recently leading product for Freshservice.
- Ramesh Parthasarathy worked on AI and platform development at Freshworks.

At the core of the CRM is Company Brain, a virtual memory that combines information from emails, meetings and other customer interactions into a shared record of the relationship. Four built-in AI agents use this data to research prospects, draft follow-up emails, flag potential deal risks and configure CRM workflows. The sales team retains control over messages sent to customers.

In its first three months of active sales, Dreamteam signed more than 100 customers in 13 countries. Most pay for the service, Venkatraman said. Several companies have already replaced their previous CRMs with the startup’s product: healthcare company DMS Health left Salesforce, while other customers moved on from HubSpot.

Plex’s advertising team chose Dreamteam over HubSpot and other CRMs built with AI in mind. Plex co-founder and chief product officer Scott Olechowski cited Dreamteam’s integration with the AI tools the company already uses as an important advantage.

## Can Dreamteam take on the big CRM platforms?

Replacing established CRMs at scale will be harder. Venture firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) wrote in a blog post earlier this year that AI could change how companies manage customer relationships, but that existing CRMs would likely remain the systems of record for customer data. New AI tools built on top of those systems could capture most of the value.

Venkatraman agreed that Salesforce and HubSpot are not going away anytime soon. But their current position, he said, does not guarantee they will lead the next generation of CRM. He compared the shift to the arrival of electric vehicles and added that AI calls for rethinking how CRM works, not simply adding features to existing platforms.

Kandaswamy believes startups have two to five years to establish themselves in the market while major CRM vendors adapt their products for AI. Rebuilding software on a different architecture takes time, giving new companies an opportunity to attract customers while incumbents update their products.

Dreamteam plans to expand beyond sales and cover other stages of the customer relationship: onboarding, support and upselling. The CRM already includes Magic Apps, a feature that lets users create custom apps and dashboards with natural-language instructions, without writing code.

Dreamteam has more than 35 employees. Its engineering team is based in Chennai, while its go-to-market team is in the US. Kandaswamy said the company expects to reach eight-figure annual revenue by the end of 2027, but declined to disclose its current revenue.

Dataist's take: Dreamteam shows how AI can automate part of CRM work and help a startup win customers from major platforms. But even after switching, companies may continue to keep customer records in existing systems, as a16z has noted. The key question for Dreamteam is whether it can scale CRM replacement, rather than just offer AI tools that work with existing systems.
