Anthropic has disclosed details of its agreements with Amazon and Google ahead of its IPO. After converting some of its debt, Amazon will own 14.7% of the company and Google 13.7%; both stakes will be held as non-voting shares. Amazon will retain some of its debt for now: if it converts all of it, its stake will rise to 23.8%. Separately, a group of major technology companies has agreed to buy $26 billion worth of non-voting shares.

## Amazon and Google will own nearly 30% of Anthropic after the IPO

## Amazon’s stake could grow

In 2023 and 2024, Amazon invested billions of dollars in Anthropic and received debt that can be exchanged for shares. The conversion price depends on the year of the investment and is about $2.09 or $5.05 per share.

For the IPO, Amazon decided to convert some of its debt into a special class of non-voting shares. They represent a stake of about 15% — more precisely, 14.7%. According to the IPO documents, these shares may receive dividends and can be converted into voting Class A shares upon sale.

Amazon will retain the rest of its debt for now. If the company converts all of it, its stake in Anthropic will be 23.8%.

## Google and other investors

Google’s stake after the IPO will be 13.7%, also held as non-voting shares. The shares will be issued through the conversion of debt and a private placement tied to the IPO.

AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners have agreed to buy a total of $26 billion worth of non-voting shares. Google and Nvidia will each invest $10 billion.

The IPO could bring billions to other Anthropic investors too, but Amazon will also benefit from its cloud business: outside the share transaction, AWS expects to earn revenue from Anthropic’s commitment to spend more than $100 billion on Amazon cloud services over the next decade.

Dataist’s take: The disclosed stakes show how closely the IPO ties Anthropic to the largest cloud and technology companies. Amazon’s 14.7% is not a final ceiling: its stake could rise to 23.8% if it converts its remaining debt.
