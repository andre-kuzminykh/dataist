## A broader pitch than a new name

Lehane described several ideas OpenAI wants to bring into the debate:

- Set “speed limits” for AI.
- Show how AI expands people’s capabilities in everyday life.
- Push for US participation in developing international standards.
- Rename data centers “AI computing campuses.”

The naming proposal sits alongside a more contentious conversation about where these facilities are built. Lehane called that debate “useful and healthy.” Disputes over data center projects have already led Amazon and Microsoft to say they will no longer use nondisclosure agreements in negotiations with local authorities.

Lehane also argued that local communities should hold companies accountable and demand meaningful benefits from projects.

## The question behind the language

I think the proposed name is less important than whether OpenAI can make the benefits of these sites tangible to the communities hosting them. “Campus” may frame the facilities differently, but the announcement offers no details on what meaningful local benefits would look like.

That gap matters as the company tries to shape a political conversation that it expects to intensify by 2028. OpenAI is asking communities to judge projects by what they deliver, while its own public pitch is still focused on how they should be discussed.
