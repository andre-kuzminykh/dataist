# Palantir’s revenue nearly doubled, but corporate deals slowed

## Record revenue and a shift in the main source of income

Palantir’s quarterly revenue increased 20% from the previous quarter, topping $2 billion for the first time. The result exceeded analysts’ expectations of $2.18 billion. Adjusted earnings per share came to $0.44, compared with a forecast of $0.41. The company also raised its full-year revenue forecast.

US commercial customers brought Palantir $984 million, up 148% from a year earlier. For the first time, that exceeded revenue from the US government, which came to $953 million.

Corporate customers use Palantir’s software to work with advanced models and tailor open models to their own data. The company says customers can do this securely, without sharing their data or business processes with external AI labs. Palantir also helps customers adapt its products to their needs through engineers who work directly with them.

## Faster launches and shorter trial contracts

Growing corporate interest in AI is changing how companies buy software. Businesses are trying new products more quickly, bypassing familiar bureaucratic procedures, but are more often starting with short-term trials and are quicker to terminate contracts.

Palantir CEO Alex Karp told analysts that commercial customers are moving faster from initial contact to deployment. Contracts have become shorter, but companies can significantly expand their scope once work begins.

Karp described the influx of potential corporate customers with a metaphor: Palantir, he said, had gone from “the most attractive person in physics class” to captain of the football team. He also said that companies that start working with Palantir do not return to their previous approach, pointing to the company’s business metrics.

However, quarterly figures do not yet confirm that sales growth will be sustained. The value of new contracts with US businesses was $1.46 billion, below the previous quarter’s record $2.13 billion. This points to a possible slowdown in sales.

The next financial report will show whether Palantir has strengthened its position as a growing US provider of enterprise AI solutions. Historically, many companies make their largest technology purchases in the final quarter of the year. Palantir expects US commercial revenue to exceed $3.559 billion, with growth of at least 143%.

Dataist’s take: The results reveal two sides of corporate demand for AI: customers are starting trials faster, but shorter contracts make sales less predictable. Quarterly revenue growth alone does not resolve that question: the value of US commercial contracts signed fell from the previous quarter.
