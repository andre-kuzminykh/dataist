Comedians are turning artificial intelligence’s grandest promises into punchlines. Their targets range from AI doctors and recruiters to data centers, subway advertising and the idea that machines should mediate every part of human life. The jokes matter because they give shape to a public unease that opinion polls already measure: people are worried about lost jobs, social disruption and the physical cost of the systems being built around them. This is not comedy made with AI. It is comedy aimed at the companies, products and rhetoric surrounding it.
