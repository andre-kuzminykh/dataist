## A payment that deepens opposition

PennLive reported that the developer’s offer to pay each resident $10,000 is unusual in a region where data centers face strong opposition. Mary Fox, executive director of the Pennsylvania State Ethics Commission, told the outlet she considered the approach inventive; the commission had not encountered a similar proposal before.

Residents see the offer differently. Local resident Brian Malloy compared it to a bribe. Ed Negra, a homeowner and small-business owner, said property values would fall sharply and that $10,000 would cover only a small part of the harm he believes approval of Project Hazelnut would cause. He said he did not know what else to call the payment.

The project has not been approved. State and local officials have been evasive, PennLive reported. Governor Josh Shapiro previously described the site as a “technology campus” and issued an order accelerating the process for building data centers in Pennsylvania.

## The uncertainty behind the offer

Hazel Township manager Anthony Grigoli has struggled to present a unified position with NorthPoint Development, according to PennLive. His office is also drafting zoning changes that could allow Project Hazelnut to move forward.

At a township meeting in August, residents asked Grigoli about the payments they called bribes. He said the developer had told him that was its plan, but added that he did not know whether the offer was still in force or whether the payments would happen.

I think the gap between the offer and the residents’ response is the story: $10,000 is a concrete number, while the expected effect on property values and the town’s future is harder to pin down. The announcement leaves a central point unclear: whether the payment is a firm commitment, and who would qualify to receive it. Until that is settled, the offer risks becoming another reason to distrust a project that still needs local rules to change.
