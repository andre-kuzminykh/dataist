Google says schools that ban AI entirely will lose access to Google Search. Instead of conventional search, the company suggests keeping AI Overviews enabled or giving students access to Gemini’s education-focused chatbot, Guided Learning. The decision affects schools where teachers and administrators are trying to limit AI on school devices because they are concerned about its effects on children.

# Google ties schools’ access to Search to their use of AI

## Why schools are limiting AI

Schools across the country have long relied on Google services: Search, Gmail and Chromebooks, affordable laptops widely used in classrooms. But the company’s relationship with educators is now growing more strained: teachers worry that AI is harming students whose minds are still developing.

Research and observations cited in the source link AI use to weaker critical thinking among young people and lower test scores. In the most severe cases, it may contribute to isolation and AI-related psychosis.

The New York Times reports that Google is refusing to support schools that want to block AI entirely and is cutting off their access to Google Search. The newspaper describes the move as pressure on educational institutions: Search is a key way to access information on the open internet, and disabling it could weaken the position of teachers and administrators who oppose AI in classrooms.

## Search and AI Overviews

Google has placed AI Overviews at the top of search results, closely linking the two features. Yet the service’s answers are often wrong, meaning users can encounter misinformation immediately.

Google’s vice president of education, Chris Phillips, told The New York Times that Search and AI are deeply integrated. If a school bans AI, students will be unable to use Search and will see a message from the school saying the service is unavailable.

The company is promoting the Gemini chatbot for schools as a tool to help teachers and provide personalized learning. Earlier this year, Google made AI features the default in classrooms, drawing sharp criticism from educators.

## What options Google has left

The decision comes amid debate over how to regulate AI use in public schools and among minors. Several school districts in New York and California have restricted some features on school devices, hoping to restore conventional search without AI Overviews.

Now, according to Google’s announcement this week, schools have two options:

- keep Search with AI Overviews enabled;
- choose Guided Learning, a version of the Gemini chatbot designed for students.

Educators who want to protect children from potential harms of AI may not welcome either option.

Dataist’s take: Google is effectively making access to its search engine conditional on a school agreeing to use one of two AI features. This limits the options available to districts that restricted AI on school devices to preserve conventional search.
