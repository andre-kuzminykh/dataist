# Bill Gates Proposed Introducing a Robot Tax and “Professions Reserved for Humans”

Bill Gates published a lengthy essay on the social consequences of AI on the Gates Notes website. The Microsoft co-founder supported the idea of slowing the development of advanced models, but questioned whether such an approach would be sustainable. At the same time, he highlighted the benefits of AI for science and healthcare and proposed two new tools to protect the labor market: a robot tax that would fund retraining and social support, and a list of professions in which the use of AI would be prohibited. In Gates’s view, such measures could significantly reduce the profits of major AI labs.

Gates generally advocates a responsible approach to AI. He believes that the open letter “Pause Giant AI Experiments,” in which employees of AI companies called for a slowdown in the development of the technology, would have been a reasonable initiative, but doubts that it could be maintained for long.

He also speaks with interest about the benefits of AI for science and medicine, but is concerned about the technology’s impact on employment. Anthropic has already raised similar questions in its work on public policy.

## A Robot Tax

One of Gates’s proposals is to introduce a “robot tax.” Currently, an employer pays taxes on an employee’s wages, while the purchase of a robot is usually immediately written off as a business expense. The tax system therefore encourages companies to replace people with machines.

Under Gates’s proposal, such a tax would slightly slow the transition from human labor to machines and generate funds for retraining workers and strengthening the social safety net.

> 📌 **Gates’s idea:** robot tax → fewer incentives to replace people → more funding for retraining and social protection.

## “Professions Reserved for Humans”

The second proposal is to designate certain professions as “reserved for humans,” meaning that AI would be prohibited from being used for specific tasks. Gates believes it would be relatively easy for policymakers to introduce such restrictions.

Some professions could be kept for humans for economic reasons—for example, if automation displaced a large number of workers who would find it difficult to change their specialization. Someone who has worked in construction all their life and reached the age of 55 would find it difficult to switch to eldercare and be expected to find such work suitable.

In other cases, the decision could depend on additional circumstances. In medicine, for example, there are no technical reasons why a robot could not tell a patient about an incurable illness. But, in Gates’s view, it should not do so.

The list of professions reserved for humans could change over time. Some occupations could be protected even now, with AI introduced gradually over years or decades and an agreement made in advance to leave some jobs for people.

> 👤 **Reserved for humans:** specific tasks and professions where AI cannot be used or its adoption is extended over many years.

Both ideas could significantly reduce the profits of major AI labs—which may be why they have barely been discussed until now.

Unresolved questions also remain: who exactly should establish such restrictions, and what form they would take if these measures were ultimately introduced.
